      Galaxy Rotation Curves

      Stars far from a galaxy's center orbit as fast as those near it, though the visible matter says they should slow down. Build the curve from a bulge, disk, gas and a dark matter halo, measure it with Doppler shifts, and compare the Milky Way and NGC 3198.

      In Astronomy

      Before the worksheet: Circular orbits and gravity; Doppler shift; integrating a mass density

      The Milky Way and Spiral Arms

      Measure 141 real globular clusters, as Shapley did, and find that the Sun is far from the center of the Galaxy, and see how dust once fooled him. Sort the clusters into old metal poor and young metal rich populations, ride the Sun around the Galaxy for a galactic year and weigh the Galaxy from its orbit, then watch spiral arms wind up or move like a traffic jam.

      In Astronomy

      Before the worksheet: Brightness falls as one over distance squared; orbits and Kepler's third law in AU and years; light years

      Galaxy Collisions

      Fly two disk galaxies past each other, as Toomre and Toomre did in 1972, and watch tides pull out long tails and bridges of stars. Turn the disks, change the masses and the closest approach, run the Milky Way into Andromeda in about four billion years, and see why the stars themselves almost never hit.

      In Astronomy

      Before the worksheet: Gravity weakens as one over distance squared; tides on the Earth; time equals distance over speed

      Active Galaxies and Quasars

      Find how big a quasar can be from how fast it flickers, then power it with gas falling into a supermassive black hole: L = η ṁ c², the Eddington limit and a disk hot enough to glow in the ultraviolet. Finally see why blobs in jets like M87's seem to cross the sky faster than light.

      In Astronomy

      Before the worksheet: The speed of light and light years; E = mc²; power as energy per second

      Galaxy Clusters and Dark Matter

      Weigh a cluster of galaxies three ways: from its galaxies' speeds as Zwicky did, from its hot X-ray gas, and from how it bends the light of galaxies behind it into arcs and rings. All three find about seven times more mass than the stars and gas. Then collide two clusters and see why the Bullet Cluster points to dark matter.

      In Astronomy

      Before the worksheet: Weighing with an orbit (M = v²r/G); Wien's law; light is bent by mass

      The Big Bang and the Early Universe

      Watch every galaxy move away from every other as space stretches and time the distances to find Hubble's law and the age of the universe.       Planet Temperatures and Habitable Zones

      Move a planet nearer or farther from its star, change how much light it reflects and how much heat its air traps, and match the measured temperatures of Mercury, Venus, Earth, the Moon and Mars. Then find the habitable zone around the Sun and two red dwarfs, and see which worlds can keep their air.

      The Fate of the Universe

      Set how much matter and dark energy the universe holds and watch it slow down, recollapse, speed up or tear itself apart, from the Friedmann equation. Test each universe against 1,473 type Ia supernovae, the evidence from 1998 that the expansion is speeding up, draw giant triangles on flat, closed and open space, and follow each ending to its last chapter.

      Before the worksheet: Redshift and Hubble's law; the inverse square law for brightness; reading a graph

      Life in the Universe

      Squeeze Earth's history into one day or one year and see how late animals and people arrive. Find the habitable zone of the Sun, a red dwarf or TRAPPIST-1 and watch it move out as the Sun brightens. Then multiply the Drake equation, factor by factor, and scatter the civilizations it predicts across a model Milky Way.

      Before the worksheet: Square roots and powers of ten; what a star's luminosity is; reading a straight line graph
