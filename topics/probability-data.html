      Data Explorer

      One data set shown every way the statistics book draws it, all at once: frequency table with relative and cumulative relative frequency, stemplot, dot plot, histogram with a draggable start and bin width, box plot and time series. Drag a point and watch the mean and median, quartiles and percentiles, IQR and the outlier fences, s and skew respond. Simulated lab readings (pendulum timings, lap times, decay counts) and data sets from OpenStax Introductory Statistics, or your own.

    • Demo

      Chance Lab: Dice, Cards and Candies

      Roll dice, deal cards with or without putting them back, and draw candies from a cup. Pick two events and watch every trial land in a Venn diagram, a tree diagram and a contingency table; check the addition and multiplication rules and independence; and watch relative frequencies settle toward probabilities and averages toward expected values.

      Open demo Start here5 min · explore 15 min Worksheet: Dice and Cards: Predict, Simulate, Compare Notes

    • Demo

      Distribution Explorer

      Ten probability distributions from the statistics book with sliders: binomial, geometric, hypergeometric, Poisson, uniform, exponential, normal, t, chi square and F. Shade P(a < X < b) and read it with the calculator command the book would use, find z scores and percentiles, and draw random values to watch a histogram fill against the curve. Compare t with the normal as df grows, and the binomial with its normal and Poisson approximations.

      Open demo Start here5 min · explore 15 min Notes

    • Demo

      Sampling Lab

      A lecture hall of 400 simulated students, every one shown. Sample them five ways (simple random, stratified, cluster, systematic, convenience) and see who gets picked and how biased and spread each method's estimates are; compare a self selected study with a randomized one and find the lurking variable; then draw thousands of samples and watch the means and sums pile up into a normal curve with spread σ/√n: the central limit theorem.

      Open demo Start here5 min · explore 15 min Worksheet 1: How You Sample Matters Worksheet 2: The Central Limit Theorem Notes

    • Demo

      Galton Board

      Balls fall through rows of pegs into bins: one ball is unpredictable, thousands make the binomial distribution and, with many rows, a bell curve. Set the rows and the chance of bouncing right; compare the counts with theory.

    • Lab

      Measurement Uncertainty

      Measure g with a pendulum and judge what the data can tell you: time it by hand or with a photogate, one swing or many, repeat, read the length to the right place or the wrong one, and release from small or big angles. Random scatter, resolution, bias, propagation, fits and residuals, with every instrument error simulated and labeled. A Histogram graph shows 100 timings against the normal curve.
