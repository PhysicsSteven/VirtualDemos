      Families of Functions

      Turn a point around the unit circle and watch it draw sine and cosine with the angle as arc length; stretch and shift any typed function and see what each number does; reflect a graph in y = x to make its inverse, with the slope turning into 1/slope. Phasors, simple harmonic motion and exponential decay with its half life are the same moves.

      Limits

      Zoom into a point and watch a function settle, or not: one sided limits, holes, jumps, blow ups and oscillation. Pick an ε band and see the largest δ that fits it, computed rather than guessed. Limits at infinity and asymptotes, sin x / x, and L'Hôpital's rule with f/g beside f′/g′; average velocity closing in on instantaneous velocity and the small angle limit of a pendulum.

      Curves and Motion

      Type a path as x(t) and y(t), a polar curve r(θ) or a curve in space and watch a point move along it: velocity and acceleration, the acceleration split into tangential and normal parts, T and N, the osculating circle and curvature, arc length and swept area. Cycloid, projectile, circular motion, a proton's helix, and orbits as conics from a focus and a directrix with Kepler's equal areas.

      Slope Fields and Euler's Method

      Type a differential equation dy/dt = f(t, y) and see its slope field; click to start solution curves, step along them with Euler's method against the accurate solution, and read the equilibria and their stability from the phase line. Newton's cooling, decay, RC circuits, falling with drag, logistic growth and two mysteries to solve.

      Riemann Sums and Integrals

      Fill the area under any typed function with left, right, midpoint, trapezoid or Simpson pieces, raise n and watch the sum close in on the exact integral, read each rule's order from its error on log axes, and follow the area as it builds up: work, impulse, charge, displacement and improper integrals.

    • Demo

      Taylor Polynomials and Series

      Type any function and add the terms of its Taylor polynomial about any center: watch it hug the curve, see where it stays within 1% and the Lagrange error bound, and find the radius of convergence from the poles and branch points in the complex plane. Physics setups: the small angle pendulum, the binomial approximation, relativistic kinetic energy, gravity above Earth and the dipole field. Build new series by substitution, differentiation and integration.

      OpenStax 1:13.2 1:15.4

      Open demo Worksheet: The Small Angle Approximation: How Small Is Small? Notes

    • Demo

      Related Rates and Optimization

      One quantity drives a whole scene: a ladder slides down a wall, a cone fills with water, a shadow grows as you walk from a lamp, with every rate shown and the chain rule checked against the motion. Then find the best box, field and can, where the derivative is zero, and the physics: the lifeguard's fastest path is Snell's law (Fermat's principle), and the longest throw is at 45° on level ground and lower from a height.

    • Demo

      Chance Lab: Dice, Cards and Candies

      Roll dice, deal cards with or without putting them back, and draw candies from a cup. Pick two events and watch every trial land in a Venn diagram, a tree diagram and a contingency table; check the addition and multiplication rules and independence; and watch relative frequencies settle toward probabilities and averages toward expected values.

      Chi-Square Tests

      Observed against expected counts with each cell's (O − E)²/E drawn: a loaded die, Mendel's peas, Geiger counts against a Poisson distribution, tables of two variables, and the spread of stopwatch timings. Simulate data sets where the hypothesis is true and watch their chi square values fill a histogram under the curve.
