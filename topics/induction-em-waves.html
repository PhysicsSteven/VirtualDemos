      Faraday's and Lenz's Laws

      Push a magnet into a coil and watch the flux, EMF and current, with the coil pushing back. Move it fast or slow and the same total charge flows. Spin a generator coil and slide a bar along rails.

      Induced Electric Fields

      A long solenoid seen end on with a changing current: the electric field it induces circles the axis, inside and in the empty space outside. Drag a closed path and compare the circulation of E with the rate of change of flux.

      Three devices that run on Faraday's law: shake a magnet through a coil to charge a flashlight, draw with a magnetic pen on a grid of wires, and swipe a card's magnetic stripe past a reading head.

      Eddy Currents: Magnet in a Pipe

      Drop a strong magnet down copper, aluminum and plastic pipes side by side. Currents induced in the metal walls push back by Lenz's law, and the magnet drifts down at a slow terminal speed.

      Two coils on an iron core: change the turns and the load and watch V_S/V_P follow N_S/N_P on live traces, switch to a real core with leakage and winding resistance, then send power down a long line and see the I²R loss fall as 1/V².
