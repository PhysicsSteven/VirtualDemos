Virtual Physics Demos

Interactive demonstrations for introductory physics. Each one runs in the browser with nothing to install.

Astronomy

  • Blackbody Radiation Slide a temperature from a human body to a blue supergiant and watch the Planck curve, Wien's peak and the star's color change.
    Planck curve · Wien's law · Stefan-Boltzmann · star colors
  • Reasons for Seasons Earth's tilted axis, the Sun's path across your sky, day length and sunlight through the year, and why distance from the Sun is not the reason.
    axial tilt · solar declination · day length · insolation
  • Moon Phases Drag the Moon around Earth and see its phase from the ground, when it rises and sets, and why eclipses only happen in eclipse seasons.
    lunar phases · synodic month · line of nodes · eclipses
  • Kepler's Laws Launch a planet by dragging its velocity arrow. Watch ellipses form, equal areas sweep out in equal times, and the orbit land on P² = a³/M with the real planets.
    ellipses · equal areas · P² = a³ · escape speed
  • Stellar Evolution Follow a star of any mass across the HR diagram from the main sequence to a cooling white dwarf or a supernova, and date a star cluster from its turnoff.
    HR diagram · main sequence lifetime · white dwarf cooling · cluster turnoff
  • Finding Exoplanets Watch a planet tug its star and cross its face, with the transit light curve and radial velocity curve drawing live. See why Earth twins are so hard to find.
    transit method · radial velocity · Kepler's third law · planet density
  • Star Slice Cut a real MESA star model in half and zoom from the envelope to the core: composition, convection zones, burning shells, and a white dwarf crystallizing from the center out.
    stellar interiors · convection · shell burning · MESA

Modern physics

  • Photoelectric Effect Shine light on a metal, find the stopping voltage, and measure Planck's constant from the slope, the way Millikan did in 1916.
    photons · work function · stopping voltage · Planck's constant
  • Spectra Explorer Click any of 99 elements to see its emission or absorption lines, measured by NIST. Includes the Bohr model of hydrogen and a mystery spectrum game, including a star.
    atomic spectra · Bohr model · Balmer series · stellar absorption lines
  • Spacetime Diagrams Place events on a Minkowski diagram and change observers. Light cones stay at 45° while simultaneity, clocks and lengths change, through the train, the rod and the twin paradox.
    light cones · Lorentz transformation · relativity of simultaneity · twin paradox
  • Quantum Wave Packets Launch an electron at a barrier and watch part of it tunnel through, settle into energy eigenstates in a box or an oscillator, and collapse when you measure where it is.
    Schrödinger equation · tunneling · energy eigenstates · uncertainty principle · measurement
  • Hydrogen Orbitals Every hydrogen state up to n = 6 as a 3D cloud sampled from |ψ|², a slice showing its nodes, and its radial probability against the Bohr orbit. Mix 1s and 2p and watch the charge slosh at the Lyman α frequency.
    quantum numbers · orbitals · radial and angular nodes · superposition

Mechanics

  • Projectile Range Launch a baseball, a ping pong ball or a cannonball with and without air resistance, and see why the best launch angle drops below 45°.
    projectile motion · quadratic drag · terminal speed

Thermodynamics

  • Gas in a Box Watch pressure and temperature emerge from molecules hitting the walls, speeds relax to Maxwell-Boltzmann, and a moving piston heat the gas.
    kinetic theory · ideal gas law · adiabatic compression

Electricity and magnetism

  • Coulomb Sandbox Drag point charges and watch the field lines, equipotentials and potential update live, as a 2D map or a rotatable 3D landscape.
    electric field · potential · superposition

Optics

  • Optics Bench Drag lenses, mirrors, prisms and light sources. Rays bend and bounce live, and images form where they cross.
    thin lens equation · mirrors · total internal reflection · dispersion
