## What students should come away with

- Energy per kilogram spans about eight powers of ten: lithium ion batteries 0.7 MJ, chemical fuels 15 to 55 MJ, uranium 235 about 8 × 10⁷ MJ. Nuclear energy per kilogram is millions of times chemical because nuclear binding energies are MeV, chemical bonds eV.
- Making electricity from heat loses most of it: a power station converts roughly a third of the heat.
- Renewables move huge masses for modest energy: water falling 100 m gives under a kilojoule per kilogram, so hydro and wind need enormous flows.
- Food is a fuel: fat carries more energy per kilogram than TNT (TNT's advantage is how fast it releases it).

## Before class

Students need units of energy (J, kWh) and the idea of efficiency. The chart is logarithmic: each grid line is ten times the last. **Blind** hides the bars and numbers so the class can rank the sources first.

## Running it in class

1. **Rank first.** Blind on, a household's 30 kWh for a day as electricity: ask for a ranking of the sources by mass needed. Reveal: 3.8 mg of uranium, 6.7 kg of gasoline, 150 kg of batteries, 120 tonnes of water through a 100 m dam, 3600 tonnes of air at 10 m/s.
2. **Heat vs electricity.** Uncheck "Delivered as electricity": every fuel mass drops by the factor 1/0.35. The battery and the renewables do not change.
3. **TNT vs food.** TNT 74 kg, food fat 8.2 kg for the same energy (as electricity). Discuss power: energy per second, not per kilogram.
4. **Wind speed.** Double the wind speed and the air needed drops by 4 (½v² per kg); per second through a rotor it delivers 8 times the power (more air arrives too).
5. **Solar.** 31 m² of 20% panels collect 30 kWh in a day at an average 200 W/m², which depends strongly on place and season.

## Questions

**Q1.** Show that fissioning 1 kg of U-235 at 200 MeV per fission releases 8.2 × 10¹³ J.

**Q2.** How much gasoline does it take to provide 1 kWh of electricity at 35% efficiency? How much heat?

**Q3.** Why is so much water needed for hydroelectric power? Find the mass of water falling 100 m for 30 kWh at 90% efficiency.

**Q4.** Why does doubling the wind speed increase a turbine's power by eight times?

**Q5.** Fat has about nine times the energy per kilogram of TNT. Why is TNT an explosive and fat is not?

## Common misconceptions

- **"Energy is used up."** It is converted; a power station's "lost" two thirds goes into the cooling water and air as heat.
- **"Batteries are an energy source."** They store energy made elsewhere, and hold far less per kilogram than fuels.
- **"Explosives contain the most energy."** They release it fastest; per kilogram they hold less than fuels and food.

## Physics behind the demo

- Energy per kilogram: typical higher heating values (natural gas 55, gasoline 46, coal 27, dry wood 18 MJ/kg; samples vary). Food from the Atwater factors 9 and 4 kcal/g. TNT 4.184 MJ/kg by definition. Lithium ion cells about 200 Wh/kg.
- Uranium: 200 MeV per fission (thermal fission of U-235 releases about 200 MeV in total, some of it to neutrinos), 1 kg = (1000/235) mol of nuclei, all fissioned. Real fuel is a few percent U-235 and is not fully burned.
- Fuels delivering electricity are divided by the power station efficiency; batteries deliver electricity directly.
- Hydro: gh per kilogram at 90% turbine efficiency. Wind: the kinetic energy ½v² per kilogram of air passing through the rotor times the Betz limit 16/27. Solar: 20% panels at the average irradiance set on the slider, including nights.
- No national energy statistics are used.
