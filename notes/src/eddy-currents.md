## What students should come away with

- A magnet moving past a conductor changes the flux through rings of the conductor, which drives currents around them (eddy currents).
- By Lenz's law those currents oppose the change: below the falling magnet they repel it, above it they attract it, so it falls slowly at a steady terminal speed.
- The drag grows with conductivity: copper slows the magnet more than aluminium; plastic not at all. Cooling copper makes it conduct better and slows the magnet more.
- The magnet's lost gravitational energy becomes heat in the pipe.

## Running it in class

1. Ask Q1, then **Drop all three**. Plastic: 0.45 s, free fall. Aluminium: 8.0 s. Copper: 12.6 s.
2. Look at the wall currents: opposite circulation just above and just below the magnet, none level with it.
3. Thin the wall to 0.8 mm: copper's time drops by about 40% (the drag goes as w/a⁴, and the radius a shrinks a little too).
4. Cool to −196 °C (liquid nitrogen): copper takes about 91 s.

## Questions

**Q1.** Predict which magnet reaches the bottom first, and explain using Faraday's and Lenz's laws.

**Q2.** Above the falling magnet (north pole down), which way does the induced current circulate, seen from above? Below it? Why is there no current in the ring level with the magnet?

**Q3.** The magnet reaches terminal speed in the copper pipe. Where does its gravitational potential energy go? Estimate the heat for the 1.0 m fall.

**Q4.** Why does the fall time double when the conductivity doubles?

## Common misconceptions

- **"The magnet sticks to the copper."** Copper and aluminium are not attracted to a still magnet; the force exists only while the magnet moves.
- **"The pipe is magnetized."** The force comes from currents induced in it.

## Physics behind the demo

- The magnet as a point dipole on the pipe's axis in a thin-walled pipe gives drag F = kv with k = 45μ₀²m²σw/(1024a⁴) (Levin and coworkers, Am. J. Phys. 2006); exact fall with linear drag. A real magnet that nearly fills the pipe falls at the same order of speed but not exactly this one.
- NdFeB magnet 12.7 mm × 12.7 mm, 12.2 g, m = 1.6 A·m² by default; pipe inner radius 9.5 mm; copper 59.6 MS/m and aluminium 37.7 MS/m at 20 °C, linear in temperature, measured ratios at 77 K. Air drag ignored.
- Wall currents are drawn from the induced emf around each ring, ε = −v dΦ/dz, darker where it is larger.
