## What students should come away with

- Velocities do not simply add: a probe fired at u′ from a rocket at v moves at (v + u′)/(1 + vu′/c²), always below c, and light fired from any rocket moves at c.
- Newton's second law survives as F = dp/dt with p = γmv. A steady force makes the momentum grow in step with time while the speed creeps up to c.
- Kinetic energy is (γ − 1)mc², not ½mv². Electrons given more and more energy (Bertozzi, 1964) take almost the same time to fly 8.4 m, while their momentum keeps growing; E² = (pc)² + (mc²)² ties energy, momentum and rest energy together.

## Before class

Students should know the Lorentz factor γ and time dilation. The demo has three views. **Adding velocities**: two beacons 3.000 light seconds apart time a probe fired by a passing rocket, at a launch speed measured by the crew; a light pulse can be fired instead, and a mystery rocket hides its speed. **Steady force**: a particle starts from rest in a uniform field (electron, muon, proton, deuteron or alpha particle, 0.1 to 10 MV/m); readouts give t, x and v. **Electron flight**: particles accelerated through 0.01 to 10 000 MV are timed over 8.400 m and bent by a magnet onto a ruler; a mystery particle hides its identity.

## Running it in class

1. **Rocket fires a probe.** Ask Q1, then launch. The probe takes 3.5455 s between the beacons: 0.8462c, not 1.10c.
2. **Rocket fires light.** Ask Q2. The pulse takes 3.0000 s whatever the rocket speed.
3. **Electron, steady force.** Ask Q3. Run it: the speed bar creeps to 0.986c by 10 ns while Newton's marker leaves the scale at 1.70 ns. Momentum p = Ft grows in step with time.
4. **Bertozzi's electrons.** Ask Q4. Fire at 0.5, 1, 5 and 20 MV: 32.47, 29.77, 28.14 and 28.03 ns. The bend radius keeps growing (5.82, 9.49, 36.61, 136.79 cm at 0.05 T).
5. **Mystery particle.** Students measure the flight time and radius at a few voltages and find mc² (Q5).

## Questions

**Q1.** A rocket at 0.60c fires a probe forward at 0.50c (crew's measurement). What do the beacons, 3.000 light seconds apart, time it at?

**Q2.** The same rocket fires light instead. What time do the beacons show, and what if the rocket is at 0.90c?

**Q3.** An electron starts from rest in a 1.0 MV/m field. When would Newton's mechanics have it reach c? What is its speed after 5.0 ns?

**Q4.** Predict the flight time over 8.400 m for electrons of 1 MeV, and the shortest flight time any particle could show.

**Q5.** A mystery particle of charge e, given 100 MeV, bends on a 117.71 cm circle in a 0.50 T field. Find pc, then its rest energy. What is it?

## Common misconceptions

- **"Speeds add."** Only at everyday speeds. Distances and times measured in the two frames differ, so the rocket's 0.50c is not 0.50c on the ground clocks.
- **"A steady force gives a steady acceleration."** dp/dt = F is steady; dv/dt falls as γ grows.
- **"Mass increases with speed."** The rest mass m is fixed; momentum and energy carry the factor γ.
- **"More energy means more speed."** Above a few MeV, an electron's speed is c to within 0.1%; extra energy raises momentum and γ.

## Physics behind the demo

- Velocity addition is the exact one dimensional Lorentz rule. Beacons at rest in the ground frame time each body between them; the run plays in real time at 1×.
- Steady force: p = Ft from rest, v/c = kt/√(1 + (kt)²) with k = F/(mc), x = (mc²/F)(√(1 + (kt)²) − 1). Radiation by the accelerated charge is left out (under 10⁻¹⁰ of the work done at every step the demo reaches).
- Electron flight: K = qV, γ = 1 + K/mc², t = 8.400 m/v, r = p/(qB) with pc = √(K² + 2Kmc²). Rest energies are CODATA 2018 values. Muons are timed as if none decayed (about 3% would at 10 MV over 8.4 m).
