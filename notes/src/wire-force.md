## What students should come away with

- A current in a magnetic field feels F = IL × B, size ILB sin θ, perpendicular to both the wire and the field. A current along B feels nothing.
- The magnet feels an equal and opposite force (Newton's third law), which is how a current balance weighs it.
- On a loop, the forces on opposite sides make a couple: torque τ = μ × B with μ = NIA, largest when the loop's plane lies along B and zero when μ lines up with B.
- A commutator that reverses the current each half turn keeps the torque one way: a DC motor.

## Before class

Students should know the right hand rule for F = qv × B. All views are from above, with B to the right from N to S. The balance sits under the magnet and was zeroed with no current, so it reads the reaction on the magnet in grams. The **Mystery magnet** hides B.

## Running it in class

1. **Current balance.** 2.0 A through 4.0 cm of wire in 0.20 T: 16 mN on the wire, into the page (down). The balance reads −1.63 g: the magnet is pushed up. Reverse the current: +1.63 g.
2. **Wire at an angle to B.** At 30° the force halves (8.0 mN); at 0° it vanishes.
3. **Loop: torque.** 20 turns, 1.0 A, 4 cm × 4 cm, 0.20 T: μ = 32 mA·m², largest torque 6.4 mN·m. Release it: it swings about μ ∥ B and settles, like a compass needle.
4. **DC motor.** With the commutator it keeps turning. Watch the torque readout rise and fall twice per turn.
5. Lab: capture the balance reading at seven currents from −3 A to +3 A, including zero, and fit for B; repeat with the Mystery magnet.

## Questions

**Q1.** Predict the balance reading for 3.0 A through 4.0 cm of wire in a 0.20 T field, at right angles. Which way is the wire pushed, and which way the magnet?

**Q2.** Why does the balance, which sits under the magnet, measure a force on the wire, which does not touch it?

**Q3.** In Loop: torque, find μ and the largest torque. Where in the turn is the torque largest and where is it zero? Which position is stable?

**Q4.** Without a commutator the loop just swings and settles. Explain how reversing the current every half turn turns it into a motor. Where should the reversal happen?

**Q5 (lab).** From a plot of F = Δm g against I at fixed L and θ, show that |slope| = BL sin θ, and find B for the Mystery magnet.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The force is along the field lines."** It is perpendicular to both the wire and B.
- **"The magnet pushes the wire but the wire doesn't push the magnet."** The forces are equal and opposite; the balance measures exactly that.
- **"The torque on a loop is largest when it faces the field."** When μ ∥ B the torque is zero; it is largest when the loop's plane lies along B.
- **"A motor runs on a steady torque."** The torque varies through each turn; the rotor's inertia smooths the motion.

## Physics behind the demo

- Uniform field between the poles and zero outside; L is the length of wire inside the field. F = ILB sin θ with the direction from IL × B.
- The balance reads Δm = F_z/g with F_z the vertical force on the wire, since the magnet receives −F; rounding 0.01 g.
- The loop is 4 cm × 4 cm, mass 20 g, moment of inertia ma²/6 about its axis, with a small viscous friction at the axle. Torque τ = −NIAB sin θ toward μ ∥ B.
- The supply holds the current fixed, so back emf is ignored; a real motor's speed is limited mainly by back emf, not friction. The commutator reverses the current each time μ lines up with B.
- The loop is shown 4 times slower than real, the motor about 30 times slower; the rotation rate readout is real.
