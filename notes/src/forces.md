## What students should come away with

- Draw every force on one object, then add them: ΣF = ma. The free body diagram is the problem.
- Static friction is whatever is needed to stop sliding, up to μsN; it is not always μsN. Kinetic friction is μkN and opposes the sliding.
- The normal force is not always mg: it changes on a slope and when a force pulls up or pushes down.
- Work by each force adds up to the change in kinetic energy. Friction's (negative) work becomes thermal energy.
- Third law partners act on different objects and never cancel each other.

## Before class

Students should know Newton's second law and the idea of components. The free body diagram is drawn to scale beside the ramp; the weight's components along and across the ramp are dashed. **Release at the top mark** times the block over 1.000 m from rest, for the μk lab. **Mystery surface** hides μs and μk.

## Running it in class

1. **Push on a level floor.** F = 40 N on 10 kg with μs = 0.5: static friction is exactly 40 N, less than μsN = 49.1 N, so nothing moves. Raise F past 49 N; once sliding, friction drops to μkN = 29.4 N. At 60 N, a = 3.06 m/s².
2. **Ramp: when does it slip?** With μs = 0.5 the block holds at 20°. Tilt with ↑ ↓: it slips at 26.6° (tan θ = μs), for any mass.
3. **Pull at an angle.** 50 N at 30° above the floor lifts 25 N of the weight off: N = 73.1 N, a = 2.14 m/s². Ask Q2.
4. **Hanging mass on a table.** a = 1.96 m/s² and T = 15.7 N, less than m₂g = 19.6 N.
5. **Ramp and hanging mass.** At rest, static friction (4.9 N) points up the ramp. Raise m₂ to 4 kg: it slides up, friction now points down the ramp.
6. Turn on **Show third law partners** in any setup and **Work and energy since release** during a slide.

## Questions

**Q1.** In Push on a level floor, what is the friction force when F = 20 N, F = 40 N and F = 60 N? Why is it not μsmg = 49 N each time?

**Q2.** In Pull at an angle (50 N, μk = 0.3), find the acceleration at 0°, 30° and 45°. Which angle gives the largest acceleration, and why does pulling slightly upward help? Find the best angle in general.

**Q3.** A block on a ramp with μs = 0.5 and μk = 0.35. At what angle does it start to slip? At 35°, what is its acceleration down the ramp, and how long does it take to slide 1.000 m from rest?

**Q4.** In Hanging mass on a table, find a and T. Draw separate free body diagrams for the block and the hanging mass, and explain why T < m₂g.

**Q5 (lab).** Using **Release at the top mark** at several angles above the slip angle (F = 0, no hanging mass), plot a/cos θ against tan θ. Show from Newton's second law that the slope is g and the intercept is −μkg, and find μk for a Mystery surface.

**Q6.** In Ramp and hanging mass with m₂ = 4 kg, the block slides up. List the work done on the block over the first meter by gravity, friction, the string and the normal force, and check that they add to ½mv².

## Common misconceptions

- **"Friction is always μN."** Static friction matches the push until it can't.
- **"The normal force equals the weight."** Only on a level surface with no other vertical forces.
- **"A block at rest on a slope feels no friction."** Static friction holds it there, mg sin θ up the slope.
- **"Action and reaction cancel, so nothing can accelerate."** They act on different objects.
- **"The tension equals the hanging weight."** Only when nothing accelerates.

## Physics behind the demo

- Rigid block on a rigid ramp, g = 9.81 m/s², Coulomb friction (static up to μsN, kinetic μkN, independent of speed and area), massless inextensible string over a frictionless massless pulley.
- The motion is stepped exactly: within each step all forces are constant, and a step in which the block would stop is split at the stopping instant, after which static friction is tested again.
- Work by each force is F_s Δs summed over these exact pieces, so ΣW = ½mv² holds to rounding. A hard stop at the ends of the ramp removes kinetic energy and is listed separately.
- If F's vertical part exceeds the weight's component across the ramp, the block would lift off; the demo says so and does not model it.
- The timer gives t = √(2d/|a|) for d = 1.000 m from rest, rounded to 0.001 s.
