Speeding Up on a Curve

  • Open the demo
  • PHYS 201
  • 10 to 15 minutes

What students should come away with

  • On a circle at changing speed the acceleration has two perpendicular parts: tangential a_t = dv/dt along the path and centripetal a_c = v²/r toward the centre. The total, √(a_t² + a_c²), points inside the circle but not at the centre.
  • Speeding up from rest the acceleration first points along the path, then swings toward the centre as v²/r grows.
  • On a flat road friction supplies the whole acceleration, so speeding up or braking in a curve uses up grip: the car slips when √(a_t² + a_c²) exceeds μs g, at a lower speed than when cruising.

Before class

Students need uniform circular motion (the Circular Motion demo). The car goes anticlockwise from the bottom of the circle. Arrows: v (green), a_t, a_c and the total a. The graph shows the three accelerations against time with the μs g line. Blind hides the accelerations and the slip prediction.

Running it in class

  1. Speeding up from rest, 2.0 m/s² on a 60 m curve, μs = 0.70. At first a points along the path (angle 90° from the centre). At 6 s (12 m/s) a_c = 2.41 m/s² and the angle is 40°.
  2. Slip. It slides off at 9.93 s at 19.9 m/s, below the 20.3 m/s it could hold at steady speed. Straight line, not outward.
  3. Braking. From 18 m/s at 2.0 m/s²: the total starts at 5.76 m/s², inside the grip limit, and shrinks as it slows. From 20 m/s it slips almost at once (cruising at 20 m/s it would just hold).
  4. Gentle vs hard. Raise a_t to 5 m/s² with μs = 0.5: a_t alone is more than μs g and it slips immediately.

Questions

Q1. At 6.0 s in step 1, find v, a_c, the total acceleration and its angle from the direction to the centre.

Q2. Why does the car slip at a lower speed when it is speeding up than when it cruises?

Q3. Show that the slip speed satisfies (v²/r)² + a_t² = (μs g)², and find it for step 1.

Q4. A car brakes in a curve. Explain why easing off the brakes can keep it on the road.

Common misconceptions

  • "Circular motion means acceleration toward the centre." Only at constant speed.
  • "Braking in a curve is always safer." Braking needs friction too, and the grip is shared with the turn.
  • "When it slips the car flies outward." It continues along its velocity, a tangent line.

Physics behind the demo

  • A point car on a flat circular road with a constant rate of change of speed; braking stops at v₀/|a_t|. Static friction supplies the whole horizontal force, up to μs mg in any direction (a friction circle); the car slips the first time the needed acceleration reaches μs g, found exactly from (v²/r)² + a_t² = (μs g)².
  • After slipping the car moves in a straight line at the speed it had (kinetic friction on the slide is not modelled). Real tyres have different limits along and across the tread.
