Circular Motion

  • Open the demo
  • PHYS 201
  • 15 to 30 minutes

What students should come away with

  • Uniform circular motion is accelerated motion: a = v²/r toward the center.
  • Something real must supply the net force mv²/r: a string, friction, the tilt of a road, or the track and gravity together. There is no extra "centripetal force" on the free body diagram.
  • Cut the string and the object goes straight, along its velocity: there is no outward force.
  • A car can take a flat curve only up to v = √(μs g r); banking helps; on a vertical loop a ball needs v ≥ √(gr) at the top.

Running it in class

  1. Ball on a string. 0.5 kg at 3.0 m/s on a 1.0 m string: a = 9.00 m/s², T = 4.50 N. Ask Q1, then press Cut the string.
  2. Car on a flat curve. 1200 kg at 15 m/s on a 50 m radius needs 5400 N; dry road friction gives up to 8240 N (μs = 0.7). Top speed 18.5 m/s (67 km/h). Try μs = 0.4, a wet road.
  3. Banked curve. 25°, μs = 0.3: ideal speed 15.1 m/s (no friction needed); allowed 8.5 to 20.9 m/s. Watch friction flip from up the slope to down it. This setup opens in 3D, so students can see the road tilt and the car lean in (drag to turn the view; the Flat button, or ?view=flat, gives the view from above).
  4. Speeding up or braking. Same road, radius 60 m, μs = 0.70, from rest at a_t = 2.0 m/s². At first the acceleration points along the road; as v²/r grows it swings toward the center (40° from the center at 12 m/s). The car slides off at 9.93 s at 19.9 m/s, below the 20.3 m/s it could hold cruising: the grip is shared between speeding up and turning. With a_t negative it brakes; a car already near the cruising limit slides at once.
  5. Vertical loop. r = 1.5 m: it needs 8.58 m/s at the bottom to keep contact at the top. Lower v₀ to 8 m/s and watch it leave the track.

Questions

Q1. Predict the path of the ball when the string is cut. Draw the free body diagram (from above) just before and just after.

Q2. Find the top speed for a car on a flat curve of radius 50 m with μs = 0.7, and with μs = 0.4. Does the car's mass matter?

Q3. For a curve banked at 25° with radius 50 m, find the speed at which no friction is needed. Which way does friction point at 20 m/s, and why?

Q4. Show that a ball sliding without friction inside a vertical loop of radius r needs at least √(5gr) at the bottom to stay on the track at the top.

Q5. Speeding up from rest at 2.0 m/s² on a 60 m curve, find v, a_c, the total acceleration and its angle from the direction to the center at 6.0 s.

Q6. Show that a car speeding up slips when (v²/r)² + a_t² = (μs g)². Find the slip speed for μs = 0.70 and explain why it is lower than the cruising top speed. Why can easing off the brakes in a curve keep a car on the road?

Common misconceptions

  • "There is a centrifugal force pushing outward." In the ground's frame, no: the object tends to go straight, and the inward force keeps bending its path.
  • "Centripetal force is another force to add to the diagram." It is the name for the net inward force.
  • "Constant speed means no acceleration." The direction changes.
  • "At the top of a loop the track pushes up." It pushes down, toward the center, if it pushes at all.

Physics behind the demo

  • Speeding up or braking: a point car on a flat curve with a constant rate of change of speed (braking stops at v₀/|a_t|). Static friction supplies the whole horizontal force, up to μs mg in any direction; the car slides the first time √(a_t² + (v²/r)²) reaches μs g, found exactly. This setup runs at real speed. (It replaces the separate Speeding Up on a Curve demo, whose link now opens this setup.)

  • Constant speed on a circle, a = v²/r; g = 9.81 m/s². Flat curve: static friction up to μs mg. Banked curve: N and static friction solved from the horizontal and vertical balances, with the friction limit μs N giving v_min and v_max.

  • Loop: frictionless sliding (no rolling energy); speed from energy conservation, N = m(v²/r + g cos φ) with φ from the bottom; the ball leaves the track where N = 0 and then moves as a projectile.
  • After release the object moves in a straight line (string, car) or a parabola (loop). Cars are shown at a quarter of real speed and the loop at half; readouts are real.
