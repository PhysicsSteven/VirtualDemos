    Waves

    Vol 1 · Ch. 166 of 6 sections with an activity

    • Demo

      Standing Waves

      Add two traveling waves into a standing wave, find the harmonics of a guitar string, drive it into resonance, pluck it and see which harmonics vanish, and hear two close tones beat.

      • 1:16.1 Traveling Waves
      • 1:16.2 Mathematics of Waves
      • 1:16.3 Wave Speed on a Stretched String
      • 1:16.5 Interference of Waves
      • 1:16.6 Standing Waves and Resonance

      Open demo Worksheet: Standing Waves on a String Notes

    • Lab

      Ripple Tank

      Make waves in a tank of water with dippers or a straight bar, draw your own walls and shallow plates, and watch waves spread through slits, interfere, reflect, refract and focus. Measure wavelength, speed and the angles of the quiet lines.

      • 1:16.1 Traveling Waves
      • 1:16.5 Interference of Waves

      Open demo Worksheet: Ripple Tank: Wavelength, Refraction and Interference Notes

    • Lab

      Energy and Power of a Wave

      Send waves along a string into an absorber and read the power meter as you change the amplitude, the frequency and the tension, then find how the power a wave carries depends on each.

      • 1:16.4 Energy and Power of a Wave

      Open demo Worksheet: Power Carried by a Wave Notes

    • Lab

      Two Speaker Interference

      Walk a microphone across a room with two speakers playing the same tone, find the loud and quiet lines from the path difference, and get the wavelength and the speed of sound.

      • 1:16.5 Interference of Waves

      Open demo Worksheet: Two Speaker Interference Notes

    Sound

    Vol 1 · Ch. 178 of 8 sections with an activity

    • Lab

      Sound Waves and the Speed of Sound

      What a sound wave is: watch the air in a tube swing back and forth with the pressure a quarter cycle behind, then time a clap between two microphones to measure the speed of sound, and race sound through 25 gases, liquids and solids over a course up to 10 km long.

      • 1:17.1 Sound Waves
      • 1:17.2 Speed of Sound

      Open demo Worksheet: Speed of Sound by Time of Flight Notes

    • Demo

      Resonators

      What sets an instrument's pitch: find the speed of sound with a resonance tube, pluck and bow a string, and blow across a bottle as you fill it, with sound.

      • 1:17.2 Speed of Sound
      • 1:17.4 Normal Modes of a Standing Sound Wave

      Open demo Worksheet: Speed of Sound Notes

    • Lab

      Kundt's Tube

      Stroke a metal rod and the cork dust in a tube of gas gathers at the nodes: one frequency, two wavelengths, and the speed of sound in both the gas and the metal.

      • 1:17.2 Speed of Sound
      • 1:17.4 Normal Modes of a Standing Sound Wave

      Open demo Worksheet: Kundt's Tube Notes

    • Lab

      Ultrasound: Echoes and Blood Flow

      Time the echoes of an ultrasound pulse from the boundaries between fat, muscle and bone to find their depths, then point the beam at an artery and get the blood speed from the Doppler shift.

      • 1:17.2 Speed of Sound
      • 1:17.7 The Doppler Effect

      Open demo Worksheet: Ultrasound Echoes and Doppler Flow Notes

    • Lab

      Sound Intensity and Decibels

      Walk a sound level meter away from a loudspeaker in an open field: the level drops 6 dB every time the distance doubles, and two equal sources add only 3 dB.

      • 1:17.3 Sound Intensity

      Open demo Worksheet: Sound Intensity and Decibels Notes

    • Lab

      Room Acoustics and the Anechoic Chamber

      Clap in a hard room and time the echoes dying away, add absorbing panels and test Sabine's formula, then line the walls with wedges for an anechoic chamber with a clean 1/r² and a low frequency cutoff.

      • 1:17.3 Sound Intensity

      Open demo Worksheet 1: Reverberation Time Worksheet 2: Anechoic Chamber Notes

    • Demo

      Timbre and Harmonics

      Build a tone from its harmonics and hear it: square, sawtooth and triangle waves, spectra like real instruments, the missing fundamental, and why phase barely matters.

      • 1:17.4 Normal Modes of a Standing Sound Wave
      • 1:17.5 Sources of Musical Sound

      Open demo Worksheet: Timbre and Harmonics Notes

    • Demo

      Doppler Effect and Shock Waves

      Drag a source and an observer through the wavefronts: hear an ambulance's pitch fall as it passes, break the sound barrier into a Mach cone and a sonic boom, then switch to light for redshift, blueshift and the transverse Doppler effect.

      • 1:17.7 The Doppler Effect
      • 1:17.8 Shock Waves

      Open demo Worksheet: Doppler Effect and Shock Waves Notes
