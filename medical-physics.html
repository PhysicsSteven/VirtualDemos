Medical Physics

For the junior and senior medical physics track, beside the radiation physics class and ahead of the seniors’ lab at the proton therapy center: sources and decay, photon interactions, charged particles in matter, dosimetry, radiobiology, shielding, proton therapy and imaging. Calculus throughout; the upper level worksheets read cited data tables and carry uncertainties, and the other worksheets on each card work as warm ups.

    Units follow the radiation physics topics, from sources to imaging. A demo can appear in more than one unit: it has one card, in the first unit that uses it, and later units link to it. Cards marked Coming are demos being built for this module.

    1 · Sources and decay

    Decay laws, the medical isotopes and the ⁹⁹Mo to ⁹⁹ᵐTc generator with transient equilibrium, Q values for isotope production, and the X-ray tube with its filtration and half value layer.

    • Lab

      Radioactive Decay

      A grid of nuclei that each decay at random, and a count that halves every half life anyway. Carbon 14 dating, iodine 131 and decay chains where a daughter builds up and dies away.

      Open demo Worksheet 1: A Technetium Generator (Medical Physics) Worksheet 2: Measuring a Half Life (warm up) Worksheet 3: Parent and Daughter (warm up) Notes

    • Lab

      Nuclear Binding Energy

      The curve of binding energy from 2,550 measured AME2020 masses: why fusing light nuclei and splitting heavy ones both release energy, why stars stop at iron, the liquid drop model term by term, magic numbers and the chart of nuclides.

      Open demo Worksheet: The Curve of Binding Energy (warm up) Notes

    • Lab

      Shells, the Periodic Table and X-Rays

      Fill orbital boxes one electron at a time and watch the periodic table build up, see where the n + l rule fails, then fire electrons at a target and read its X-ray lines, the Duane–Hunt cutoff and Moseley's law.

      Open demo Worksheet 1: Filtration and Half Value Layer (Medical Physics) Worksheet 2: Shells and the Periodic Table (warm up) Worksheet 3: X-Ray Spectra and Moseley's Law (warm up) Notes

    • Also used in this unit: Nuclear Reactions, Fission and Fusion with Worksheet: Nuclear Reactions (warm up) (card under 6 · Protection and shielding)

    2 · Photon interactions

    Compton kinematics and the Klein–Nishina cross section, attenuation coefficients from XCOM, and the photoelectric effect as review.

    • Lab

      Photon Interactions in Matter

      How X-rays and gamma rays are stopped: the NIST coefficients of any element or tissue from 1 keV to 100 MeV split into coherent, Compton, photoelectric and pair, where each one wins, a Monte Carlo beam through a slab for buildup, and beam hardening with half value layers.

      Open demo Worksheet: Photon Interactions in Matter Notes

    • Demo

      Compton Scattering

      A photon bounces off a free electron and comes away with a longer wavelength. Drag the scattered photon around to see the shift, the electron's recoil and the momentum triangle, from Compton's 1923 X-rays to cesium 137 gamma rays and the Compton edge.

      Open demo Worksheet: Compton Scattering (warm up) Notes

    • Lab

      Gamma Spectroscopy

      A virtual NaI(Tl) spectrometer in a lead castle: set the high voltage and gain, acquire Cs-137, Co-60, Na-22 or Ba-133 spectra on a multichannel analyzer, mark peaks, calibrate, measure resolution and lead attenuation, and identify an unknown source.

      Open demo Worksheet 1: Calibrating a Detector (warm up) Worksheet 2: Absorbing Gamma Rays (warm up) Notes

    • Demo

      Photoelectric Effect

      Shine light on a metal, find the stopping voltage, and measure Planck's constant from the slope, the way Millikan did in 1916.

      Open demo Worksheet: Photoelectric Effect and Planck's Constant (warm up) Notes

    3 · Charged particles in matter

    Single Coulomb scattering, the step that multiple scattering adds up, then stopping power and range.

    • Demo

      Rutherford Scattering

      Fire alpha particles at a gold nucleus one at a time or as a beam, count them by angle against 1/sin⁴(θ/2), and see why Thomson's plum pudding atom can never send one back.

      Open demo Worksheet: Rutherford Scattering (warm up) Notes

    • Lab

      Charged Particles in Matter

      Send protons, alpha particles or carbon ions into water, through bone, lung, plastic or metal first. Watch the tracks slow, scatter and stop, build the Bragg peak from them, and compare the Bethe stopping power and CSDA range with NIST PSTAR.

      Open demo Worksheet: Stopping Power, Range and the Bragg Peak (Medical Physics) Notes

    • Also used in this unit: The Bragg Peak (card under 7 · Proton therapy)

    4 · Dosimetry and detectors

    Dose rate from a point source, absorbed dose against equivalent dose, and a scintillation detector with its calibration and resolution.

    • Lab

      Dosimetry: Kerma, Equilibrium and the Ion Chamber

      Watch collision kerma and absorbed dose part in the buildup and run together past it, with Monte Carlo histories; collect the ions in a Farmer chamber against its voltage; and calibrate a proton beam step by step as TRS-398 does it: temperature and pressure, polarity, recombination and k_Q.

      Open demo Worksheet 1: Kerma, Dose and Charged Particle Equilibrium (Medical Physics) Worksheet 2: Calibrating a Proton Beam: TRS-398 Step by Step (Medical Physics) Notes

    • Also used in this unit: Nuclear Reactions, Fission and Fusion with Worksheet: Radiation Dose and Shielding (warm up) (card under 6 · Protection and shielding) · Gamma Spectroscopy (card under 2 · Photon interactions)

    5 · Radiobiology

    Cell survival, fractionation and the relative biological effectiveness of protons.

    • Lab

      Radiobiology: Cell Survival and Fractionation

      How many cells survive a dose: linear quadratic survival curves in air and without oxygen, a simulated clonogenic assay to count and fit, BED and EQD2 for 1 to 40 fractions, and the RBE of protons against LET and dose.

      Open demo Worksheet: Radiobiology: Cell Survival and Fractionation Notes

    6 · Protection and shielding

    Time, distance and shielding: dose rate from a point source, broad beam transmission and buildup in lead, concrete and water, and a barrier calculation for a PET uptake room with workload, occupancy and the NCRP design goals (Dose view of Nuclear Reactions).

    • Lab

      Shielding Rooms

      Design the walls of a CT room, a linear accelerator vault with its maze door, and a proton therapy room, and compare how lead, steel, concrete and polyethylene do at each energy.

      Open demo Worksheet: Shielding Rooms: CT, Linac Vault and Proton Room (Medical Physics) Notes

    • Lab

      Nuclear Reactions, Fission and Fusion

      Balance a nuclear reaction and weigh it with AME2020 masses to find Q and the threshold energy; grow a chain reaction and find the critical size of a uranium or plutonium sphere; see how the Gamow peak lets the Sun fuse protons at 15 million kelvin; measure the dose from a gamma source with distance and shielding.

      Open demo Worksheet 1: Shielding a PET Uptake Room (Medical Physics) Worksheet 2: Nuclear Reactions (warm up) Worksheet 3: Chain Reactions and Critical Size (warm up) Worksheet 4: Fusion and the Gamow Peak (warm up) Worksheet 5: Radiation Dose and Shielding (warm up) Notes

    • Also used in this unit: Photon Interactions in Matter (card under 2 · Photon interactions) · Gamma Spectroscopy with Worksheet: Absorbing Gamma Rays (warm up) (card under 2 · Photon interactions)

    7 · Proton therapy

    Preparation for the seniors’ QA runs and dosimetry walkthrough at the proton therapy center, modeled on its IBA Proteus ONE pencil beam scanning system: the spread out Bragg peak, the morning QA and patient plan checks, a TRS-398 beam calibration and the RBE of protons.

    • Lab

      The Bragg Peak

      A proton beam's depth dose in water, muscle, fat or bone: move the peak with the energy, stack weighted energy layers into a spread out Bragg peak over a target, and compare it with a measured 6 MV photon beam.

      Open demo Worksheet: The Bragg Peak and the Spread Out Bragg Peak (Medical Physics) Notes

    • Lab

      Proton Therapy QA

      Run the morning QA of a pencil beam scanning proton machine (output, range, spot position and size against TG-224 tolerances), follow a drifting machine over weeks, and check a patient plan with a 2D chamber array and gamma analysis.

      Open demo Worksheet: Proton Therapy QA: Morning Checks, Drift and Patient Plans (Medical Physics) Notes

    • Lab

      CT Numbers and Proton Range

      Calibrate a CT scanner for proton planning: scan a phantom of known plastics, turn CT numbers into stopping power ratios with Schneider's stoichiometric method, test the curve with a mystery tissue in a proton beam, and see how CT number and I value errors move the range in a patient against a 3.5 % + 1 mm margin.

      Open demo Worksheet: CT Numbers and Proton Range: Calibration, Stopping Power and Margins (Medical Physics) Notes

    9 · Imaging

    Echo ranging, impedance, tissue attenuation in dB per cm per MHz and Doppler; the Larmor relation for hydrogen. X-ray imaging, MRI and nuclear medicine imaging come in a second phase.

    • Lab

      Ultrasound: Echoes and Blood Flow

      Time the echoes of an ultrasound pulse from the boundaries between fat, muscle and bone to find their depths, then point the beam at an artery and get the blood speed from the Doppler shift.

      Open demo Worksheet 1: Attenuation in Tissue (Medical Physics) Worksheet 2: Ultrasound Echoes and Doppler Flow (warm up) Notes

      Spin Resonance

      Tune an electron or proton spin resonance probe, sweep the field, find the absorption peak on the oscilloscope, and measure g for DPPH and the gyromagnetic ratios of hydrogen and fluorine.

      Open demo Worksheet: Spin Resonance (warm up) Notes
