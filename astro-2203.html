    Solar system astronomy

    Astro 2203

    From the sky, gravity and light to the planets, their moons, the small bodies and planets around other stars, in three units of eight class days. Each week lists its class days through the Winter Quarter, with a test after each unit and one required worksheet most weeks. Readings are in OpenStax Astronomy 2e. The worksheets are written for an introductory class: algebra, ratios and short graphs.

    Units follow the weeks of the course, with each test where it falls. Each week opens with what is required: the demo and the worksheet you hand in. Everything else for the week, including the lessons, sits below it under Supplementary, closed until you open it; nothing there is handed in. A demo can appear in more than one week: it has one card, in the week that needs it most, and other weeks link to it. Every demo here is also on the Astronomy page, with the other astronomy demos.       Open demo Worksheet: Building a Telescope65 min · algebra

    Supplementary

    7 demos and 7 worksheets, not handed in

    This week’s lessons

    Day 6, telescopes: how a telescope works, light gathering power, resolution, aberrations and the air. Day 7, light: waves, photons, blackbodies, inverse square brightness and the windows in the air. Day 8, spectra: three kinds of spectra, why atoms make lines, and the Doppler shift.

    • More with Build a Microscope or Telescope: Start here (5 min) · Notes

    • Demo

      Optics Bench

      Drag lenses, mirrors, prisms and light sources. Rays bend and bounce live, and images form where they cross.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Lenses, Mirrors and Color50 min · algebra Notes

    • Lab

      Circular Apertures and Resolution

      Light through a round hole makes an Airy pattern, computed exactly. Measure its first dark ring on a screen, then bring two point sources together until they blur into one: Rayleigh's criterion for an iris, the eye and a car's headlights, the Hubble telescope and a camera lens stopped down.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Seeing Fine Detail60 min · algebra Notes

    • Lab

      Telescope Aberrations

      Why a real telescope blurs a star, computed: the image is the diffraction pattern of the aperture with the wavefront error found by tracing real rays. Spherical and parabolic mirrors, coma off the axis, a pinched mirror, a single lens against an achromat, Hubble's 1990 flaw and its fix, and moving air with adaptive optics. Then a mystery telescope to diagnose and fix.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet 1: Fixing a Blurry Telescope65 min · algebra Worksheet 2: Seeing and Adaptive Optics55 min · algebra Notes

    • Demo

      Electromagnetic Waves

      A plane wave in 3D with E, B and the Poynting vector, polarization from linear to circular, the energy and pressure of sunlight and a laser, the full spectrum, and a dipole antenna launching waves with exact field lines.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: The Electromagnetic Spectrum60 min · algebra Notes

    • Demo

      Blackbody Radiation

      Explore thermal emission across temperatures, test Planck’s spectrum, Wien’s displacement law and Stefan–Boltzmann scaling, and connect the results to visible color.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Star Colors and Temperatures55 min · algebra Notes

    • Demo

      Spectra Explorer

      Click any of 99 elements to see its emission or absorption lines, measured by NIST. Includes the Bohr model of hydrogen and a mystery spectrum game, including a star.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Fingerprints of the Elements55 min · algebra Notes

    • Demo

      Doppler Effect and Shock Waves

      Drag a source and an observer through the wavefronts: hear an ambulance's pitch fall as it passes, break the sound barrier into a Mach cone and a sonic boom, then switch to light for redshift, blueshift and the transverse Doppler effect.

      Open demo Notes

    • Demo

      Building a Solar System

      Watch a spinning cloud collapse into a disk, find the frost line where ice can form, grow planetesimals into rocky planets and giant cores, and see which early planetesimals melt and grow iron cores.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Building a Solar System65 min · algebra Notes

    • Demo

      Rotation and Torque

      Push a door near the hinge and at the handle, race a hoop, disk and sphere down a ramp, throw a ball that skids until it rolls and trace the cycloid of a rolling wheel, race a yo-yo against a dropped one, spin a skater who pulls her arms in, and balance a seesaw.

      Open demo Start here5 min Notes

      Planet Temperatures and Habitable Zones

      Move a planet nearer or farther from its star, change how much light it reflects and how much heat its air traps, and match the measured temperatures of Mercury, Venus, Earth, the Moon and Mars. Then find the habitable zone around the Sun and two red dwarfs, and see which worlds can keep their air.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Planet Temperatures and Habitable Zones70 min · algebra Notes

      Life in the Universe

      Squeeze Earth's history into one day or one year and see how late animals and people arrive. Then multiply the Drake equation, factor by factor, and scatter the civilizations it predicts across a model Milky Way.

      Drop an asteroid on the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Earth or Mars and see its crater, from a bowl to a basin with a ring of peaks. Count craters to date lunar and Martian surfaces with the crater clock, and cool a Moon, a Mars and an Earth to see why small worlds go quiet first.

Day 5, the Earth: its layers and how we know, differentiation, plate tectonics, the magnetic field and keeping an atmosphere.

    • More with Craters, Cooling and Surface Ages: Start here (5 min) · Worksheet 2: Dating the Surface of Mars · Notes

    • Demo

      Tides

      See why the Moon raises two ocean bulges, not one: subtract the pull at Earth's center from the pull everywhere else. Watch a city's tides over a day and spring and neap tides over a month.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Tides: Two Bulges, the Moon and the Sun60 min · algebra Notes

    • Lab

      Radioactive Decay

      A grid of nuclei that each decay at random, and a count that halves every half life anyway. Carbon 14 dating, iodine 131 and decay chains where a daughter builds up and dies away. A Counting view counts a steady source again and again: Poisson counts, √N and exponential waits.

      Open demo Start here5 min Notes

    • Lab

      Fluids: Pressure, Buoyancy and Flow

      Drag a pressure probe down a tank, drop blocks that float or sink, weigh one in an overflow can like Archimedes, see an iceberg float 89% under, watch a Venturi tube trade pressure for speed, and aim Torricelli's jet.

      Open demo Start here5 min Notes

    • Demo

      Gas in a Box

      Watch pressure and temperature emerge from molecules hitting the walls, speeds relax to Maxwell-Boltzmann, and a moving piston heat the gas. Then let two gases mix through a divider: measure diffusion from the mean square displacement and test Graham's law through a small hole.

      Open demo Start here5 min Worksheet: Why Small, Hot Planets Lose Their Air65 min · algebra Notes
