    Units follow the course schedule, one per topic, with a review unit before each test. A demo can appear in more than one unit: it has one card, in the first unit that uses it, and later units link to it.

    Topic 1 · Intro material

    The size of the solar system: distances in astronomical units, light travel times and powers of ten.

    • Demo

      Units, Scales and Estimates

      The first week toolkit: zoom through 42 powers of ten from the observable universe to a proton, exact unit conversions, dimensional analysis, significant figures and Fermi estimates.

      Open demo Notes

    Topic 2 · Star charts and motion of the Sun and Moon

    Positions on the sky, the Sun along the ecliptic and the seasons, the phases of the Moon, eclipses and precession.

    • Demo

      Reasons for Seasons

      Earth's tilted axis, the Sun's path across your sky, day length and sunlight through the year, and why distance from the Sun is not the reason.

      Open demo Worksheet: Why We Have Seasons (Astro 2203) Notes

    • Demo

      Moon Phases

      Drag the Moon around Earth and see its phase from the ground, when it rises and sets, and why eclipses only happen in eclipse seasons.

      Open demo Notes

    • Demo

      Gyroscope Precession

      A spinning wheel held up at one end swings round instead of falling. See the torque turn the angular momentum, and measure how the precession rate depends on the spin.

      Open demo Notes

    Topic 3 · Planetary motion, the Copernican revolution and gravity

    From Earth centered models to Kepler’s laws and Newton’s gravity, and weighing the Sun and planets with orbits.

    • Lab

      Kepler's Laws

      Launch a planet by dragging its velocity arrow. Watch ellipses form, equal areas sweep out in equal times, and the orbit land on P² = a³/M with the real planets.

      Open demo Worksheet: Kepler's Laws from Mercury to Pluto (Astro 2203) Notes

    • Demo

      Gravity Near Earth

      How g changes with height and latitude: from sea level to the space station and geostationary orbit, and how Earth's spin takes a little off at the equator.

      Open demo Worksheet: Gravity from the Ground to Orbit (Astro 2203) Notes

    Topic 4 · Telescopes, light and the electromagnetic spectrum

    How telescopes gather and resolve light, the spectrum from radio to X rays, thermal radiation and spectral lines.

    • Demo

      Build a Microscope or Telescope

      Put lenses and stops on a rail and look through them with an eye or a camera: find each image, the magnification, the exit pupil, the field of view and how bright the view is, for a magnifier, a microscope or a telescope of your own design.

      Open demo Notes

    • Demo

      Optics Bench

      Drag lenses, mirrors, prisms and light sources. Rays bend and bounce live, and images form where they cross.

      Open demo Notes

    • Lab

      Circular Apertures and Resolution

      Light through a round hole makes an Airy pattern, computed exactly. Measure its first dark ring on a screen, then bring two point sources together until they blur into one: Rayleigh's criterion for an iris, the eye and a car's headlights, the Hubble telescope and a camera lens stopped down.

      Open demo Notes

    • Demo

      Blackbody Radiation

      Explore thermal emission across temperatures, test Planck’s spectrum, Wien’s displacement law and Stefan–Boltzmann scaling, and connect the results to visible color.

      Open demo Notes

    • Demo

      Spectra Explorer

      Click any of 99 elements to see its emission or absorption lines, measured by NIST. Includes the Bohr model of hydrogen and a mystery spectrum game, including a star.

      Open demo Notes

    • Demo

      Electromagnetic Waves

      A plane wave in 3D with E, B and the Poynting vector, polarization from linear to circular, the energy and pressure of sunlight and a laser, the full spectrum, and a dipole antenna launching waves with exact field lines.

      Open demo Notes

    Topic 5 · Formation of planetary systems

    A spinning cloud flattens into a disk and builds planets; what sets a planet’s temperature, and the habitable zone.

    • Demo

      Rotation and Torque

      Push a door near the hinge and at the handle, race a hoop, disk and sphere down a ramp, spin a skater who pulls her arms in, and balance a seesaw.

      Open demo Notes

    Topic 6 · The Earth and Moon system

    The Moon forming impact, craters and the ages of surfaces, why small worlds cool first, and the geology of the Earth.

    • Lab

      Radioactive Decay

      A grid of nuclei that each decay at random, and a count that halves every half life anyway. Carbon 14 dating, iodine 131 and decay chains where a daughter builds up and dies away.

      Open demo Notes

    • Lab

      Fluids: Pressure, Buoyancy and Flow

      Drag a pressure probe down a tank, drop blocks that float or sink, weigh one in an overflow can like Archimedes, see an iceberg float 89% under, watch a Venturi tube trade pressure for speed, and aim Torricelli's jet.

      Open demo Notes

    Topic 7 · Inner planets to Mars I

    Mercury, Venus and Mars compared with Earth: spin, atmospheres, greenhouse warming and surfaces.

    • Demo

      Gas in a Box

      Watch pressure and temperature emerge from molecules hitting the walls, speeds relax to Maxwell-Boltzmann, and a moving piston heat the gas. Then let two gases mix through a divider: measure diffusion from the mean square displacement and test Graham's law through a small hole.

      Open demo Notes

    • Also used in this unit: Kepler's Laws (card under Topic 3 · Planetary motion, the Copernican revolution and gravity)

    Topic 8 · Mars II, geology

    Volcanoes, canyons, old water and crater counts on Mars, and its two small moons.

    • Also used in this unit: Kepler's Laws (card under Topic 3 · Planetary motion, the Copernican revolution and gravity)

    Topic 9 · Minor planets and the asteroid belt

    The asteroid belt, the largest asteroids, near Earth asteroids, and how Jupiter shapes small bodies’ orbits.

    • Demo

      Lagrange Points

      Drop or throw test particles in the rotating frame of two orbiting bodies: the five Lagrange points, zero velocity curves, Jupiter's Trojans, tadpole and horseshoe orbits, and halo style orbits near JWST's L2.

      Open demo Notes

    • Also used in this unit: Kepler's Laws (card under Topic 3 · Planetary motion, the Copernican revolution and gravity)

    Topic 10 · Outer planets: Jupiter and Saturn

    The gas giants, their rings and their moons, and the tidal forces that heat moons and limit rings.

    • Demo

      Tides

      See why the Moon raises two ocean bulges, not one: subtract the pull at Earth's center from the pull everywhere else. Watch a city's tides over a day and spring and neap tides over a month.

      Open demo Notes

    • Also used in this unit: Kepler's Laws (card under Topic 3 · Planetary motion, the Copernican revolution and gravity)

    Topic 11 · Uranus, Neptune and the Kuiper belt

    The ice giants, Uranus’s tipped axis, Pluto and the Kuiper belt, and comets.

    • Also used in this unit: Reasons for Seasons (card under Topic 2 · Star charts and motion of the Sun and Moon) · Kepler's Laws (card under Topic 3 · Planetary motion, the Copernican revolution and gravity)

    Topic 12 · Exoplanets

    Finding planets around other stars by their pull and their shadows, and what they are made of.

    • Demo

      Finding Exoplanets

      Watch a planet tug its star and cross its face, with the transit light curve and radial velocity curve drawing live. See why Earth twins are so hard to find.

      Open demo Notes

    • Lab

      Cosmic Distance Ladder

      Measure a parallax from two photos, time a Cepheid's light curve, find a supernova's peak, and fit Hubble's law to galaxy redshifts. Each rung calibrates the next.

      Open demo Notes

    • Also used in this unit: Kepler's Laws (card under Topic 3 · Planetary motion, the Copernican revolution and gravity)
