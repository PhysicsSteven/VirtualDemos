## Goal

Build mesons and baryons from quarks, check that their charge, baryon number and strangeness come out right, find the rules that decide which combinations exist, and use the pattern of the baryon decuplet to predict the mass of the Ω⁻, as Gell-Mann did in 1962.

## Background

Hadrons are made of quarks (§11.3). A **baryon** is three quarks, an **antibaryon** three antiquarks, and a **meson** a quark and an antiquark. Each quark has spin ½ and baryon number ⅓; the up, charm and top quarks have charge $$+\tfrac{2}{3}e$$ and the down, strange and bottom quarks $$-\tfrac{1}{3}e$$; the strange quark has strangeness −1 (Table 11.3). An antiquark has the opposite charge, baryon number and strangeness.

Each quark also carries a **colour** charge: red, green or blue. A hadron must be colour neutral: one quark of each colour in a baryon, or a colour and its anticolour in a meson. This is why quarks are never seen alone. Colour also lets three quarks of the same flavour, like the uuu of the Δ⁺⁺, sit in the same state with their spins lined up, which the exclusion principle would otherwise forbid.

Hadrons with the same spin and the same light quarks (u, d, s) form families: the baryon octet (spin ½), the baryon decuplet (spin 3/2) and the meson nonets. Their charges follow the Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation

$$Q = I_3 + \frac{B + S}{2}.$$

Within the decuplet, each strange quark that replaces a u or d adds roughly the same amount of mass.

## Procedure

**Octet.** With one spin down (spin ½), build and record the eight baryons with u, d and s quarks: n, p, Σ⁻, Σ⁰ and Λ⁰ (both uds), Σ⁺, Ξ⁻ and Ξ⁰.

**Decuplet.** Clear the readings. With all three spins up (spin 3/2), build and record the four Δ's, the three Σ*'s and the two Ξ*'s. Do not build sss yet.

**Predict the Ω⁻.** Fit your decuplet masses against strangeness (Analysis 3), predict the mass of the sss baryon, and write down your prediction. Then build sss with all spins up and record it.

**Mesons.** In a new copy of the table, record π⁺, π⁻, K⁺, K⁰, K⁻ and K̄⁰ with opposite spins (spin 0), then u d̄ and s s̄ with parallel spins (spin 1).

**Not hadrons.** Record what the demo says for: u d; u u u with spin ½; u d s with two quarks of the same colour; u d̄ with a red u and an antigreen d̄; t ū.

**Heavy quarks.** Record c c̄ (both spins), c ū, u d c and b b̄ (both spins).

## Analysis

1. For three of your hadrons, add up the quark charges by hand and check the demo's charge.
2. For the octet, plot Q against $$I_3$$. Show that the points lie on lines, one for each value of $$S$$, and check the Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation for every reading.
3. Plot the decuplet masses (without the Ω⁻) against $$S$$. Fit a straight line, and predict the mass of the S = −3 member. Compare your prediction with the Ω⁻ mass you then recorded.
4. The proton and the Δ⁺ have the same quarks, and so do the Λ⁰ and the Σ⁰. What makes the masses differ in each pair?
5. Compare the mass of the proton with the sum of the masses of its quarks (Particles view). Where does most of the proton's mass come from?

## Uncertainty

Use the scatter of the decuplet masses about your fitted line to estimate the uncertainty of your Ω⁻ prediction. Does the measured mass fall within it?
