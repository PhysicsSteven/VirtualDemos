## Goal

See what really happens when two galaxies pass through each other: whether their stars collide, where the long tails come from, and what a near or far pass does to the Milky Way and Andromeda.

## Background

**Crowded galaxies, empty space between stars.** Galaxies are big compared with the gaps between them: the Milky Way and Andromeda are only about 25 Milky Way diameters apart, so galaxies meet often. Stars are tiny compared with the gaps between them. Near the Sun they are about 5 ly apart, and the Sun is only $$1.39\times10^6$$ km across, so the next star is about 34 million Sun widths away.

**Tides.** Gravity weakens as one over distance squared. When a galaxy passes close to another, the other's mass pulls harder on its near side than on its far side. Treat the other galaxy as a point at distance d from this galaxy's center, and this galaxy as a disk of radius R. The near side is $$d - R$$ away and the far side $$d + R$$ away, so

$$\frac{\text{pull on the near side}}{\text{pull on the far side}} = \left(\frac{d + R}{d - R}\right)^2.$$

That difference stretches a galaxy, the same way the Moon stretches the Earth's oceans: the near side is drawn toward the other galaxy into a bridge, and the far side is left behind and flung out into a long tail.

**Which way a disk spins.** If a disk turns the same way the two galaxies swing around each other, its stars ride along with the other galaxy for a while and feel its pull for a long time. If it turns the other way, its stars rush past and the pull has little time to act.

**Andromeda.** Andromeda is about 2.5 million ly away and coming toward us at about 110 km/s. Time is distance divided by speed, so with the speed in km/s you need the distance in km: 1 ly = $$9.461\times10^{12}$$ km and 1 year = $$3.156\times10^7$$ s. Gravity speeds both galaxies up as they fall together. How close they pass depends on Andromeda's sideways speed, which is hard to measure: 17 km/s in one study (van der Marel et al. 2012), 57 km/s in another (2019, with the Gaia satellite).

## Before the lab

1. Shrink the Sun to a grain of sand 1.5 mm across. If stars are 5 ly apart, how far away is the next grain of sand, in km?
2. A dwarf galaxy 10,000 ly across has its center 60,000 ly from the Milky Way's center. How much harder does the Milky Way pull on the dwarf's near side than on its far side? Treat the Milky Way's mass as all at its center.
3. If Andromeda kept coming at a steady 110 km/s from 2.5 million ly away, how many billion years would it take to arrive?
4. Some day the Milky Way and Andromeda will pass right through each other. Of the Milky Way's 100 billion stars, about how many do you expect to crash into a star of Andromeda: most of them, about half, a few million, or hardly any? Write your guess and the reason for it before you open the demo.

## Analysis

2. For the four Part 2 runs and the first Part 1 run, list the closest approach and the share of gold stars in tails, from the nearest pass to the farthest. Describe the trend in one sentence.

## Uncertainty

The model's numbers are exact for its setup; the big uncertainty is in Andromeda's sideways speed. Your two Andromeda runs differ only in that speed. By how many million years and how many kpc do their first passes differ? Which outcome would you quote, and how would you say it honestly?
