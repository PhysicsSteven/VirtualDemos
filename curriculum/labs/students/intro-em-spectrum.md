## Goal

Explore the whole electromagnetic spectrum and check that every kind of light obeys the same rule, wavelength × frequency = speed of light.

## Background

**One family.** Radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are all the same thing: electromagnetic waves, ripples of electric and magnetic field. In empty space they all travel at the speed of light, c = 299 792 458 m/s, about 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. They differ only in their wavelength and frequency.

**Wavelength and frequency.** The **wavelength** λ is the distance from one crest to the next, in meters. The **frequency** f is how many crests pass each second, in hertz (Hz). Since the wave moves one wavelength each time a crest passes,

λ × f = c, so λ = c ÷ f.

A higher frequency means a shorter wavelength. Visible light has wavelengths of about 400 to 700 nm (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m), radio waves can be meters to kilometers long, and gamma rays are smaller than an atom.

**Powers of ten.** The spectrum spans a huge range, so frequencies are written in scientific notation: 10⁸ Hz is 100 000 000 Hz, and 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 10⁸ = 3.

## Before the lab

1. An FM radio station broadcasts at about 100 MHz (1.00 × 10⁸ Hz). Use λ = c ÷ f to find its wavelength in meters.
2. If the frequency is made ten times higher, what happens to the wavelength?
3. List the seven bands of the electromagnetic spectrum in order from the longest wavelength to the shortest.

## Safety

No hazards in the virtual lab. Ultraviolet light, X-rays and gamma rays carry enough energy per photon to damage living cells, which is why sunscreen and lead aprons exist.

## Analysis

1. Is λ × f the same for every reading? Compare it with the speed of light, 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s.
2. Which band did each of your readings fall in? Did any surprise you?
3. How many times longer is the wavelength of your 10⁸ Hz radio wave than that of your visible light? How many times lower is its frequency?
4. Convert the wavelength of your visible reading to nanometers (multiply meters by 10⁹). What color is it?
5. Compare the wavelength of your 10²⁰ Hz gamma ray with the size of an atom, about 10⁻¹⁰ m. Which is smaller, and by how many times?

## Uncertainty

The demo's speed of light is computed from measured constants and agrees with the exact value to about one part in ten billion, so λ × f should come out the same in every reading to all the digits shown. If one of your products differs, where could the difference come from? (Think about rounding when you multiply.)

## Questions

1. Hydrogen gas in space gives off radio waves with a wavelength of 21.1 cm (0.211 m). What is their frequency? Radio astronomers map our galaxy with them.
2. A microwave oven works at 2.45 × 10⁹ Hz. What is the wavelength of its microwaves, in centimeters?
3. Earth's atmosphere lets visible light and many radio waves through to the ground, but it blocks most ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays, and much of the infrared. Use the strip under the spectrum, and the "Reaches the ground?" readout, to check this. Which kinds of telescopes can work on the ground, and which have to go above the atmosphere? Why do infrared telescopes on the ground sit on high, dry mountains?
4. Your eyes see only a tiny slice of the spectrum. From your readings, roughly how many powers of ten in frequency does the whole spectrum in the demo span, and how wide is the visible slice?
