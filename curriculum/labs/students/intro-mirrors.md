## Goal

Compare how a glass lens and a curved mirror focus white light, and see why every large modern telescope uses a mirror.

## Background

**Lenses bend light.** A lens focuses light by **refraction**: light slows down in glass and changes direction as it enters and leaves. How much it bends depends on the glass's **refractive index**, and the index is a little different for each color. Glass bends violet light more than red light, which is how a prism makes a rainbow.

**Chromatic aberration.** Because violet bends more, a lens brings violet light to a focus a little closer than red light. No single place is in focus for every color, so a star seen through a simple lens has a colored fringe. This is called **chromatic aberration**. Early refracting telescopes suffered badly from it.

**Mirrors reflect light.** A curved mirror focuses light by **reflection**. The angle at which light bounces off a mirror does not depend on its color, so a mirror brings all colors to the same focus. Its focal length is half its radius of curvature, f = R/2, for every color.

## Before the lab

1. A lens is made of glass whose refractive index is larger for violet light than for red. Which color do you expect to focus closest to the lens?
2. A concave mirror has a radius of curvature of 40 cm. What is its focal length? Will that be different for red and violet light?
3. Isaac Newton built one of the first reflecting telescopes in 1668. Based on the Background, why might he have wanted a mirror instead of a lens?

## Analysis

1. For the flint glass lens, which color has the shortest focal length and which the longest? Was your prediction in Before the lab 1 right?
2. For the flint glass lens, subtract the focal length at 400 nm from the one at 700 nm. This spread is how far apart the violet and red foci are. Do the same for the crown glass lens. Which glass has less chromatic aberration?
3. Divide each spread by 20 cm and give it as a percent of the focal length.
4. What is the spread for the mirror? Compare its focal length with your answer to Before the lab 2.
5. The spread grows in proportion to the focal length. A refracting telescope with a crown glass lens of focal length 20 m (2000 cm) would have a spread how many centimeters long? Use your percent from Analysis 3.
6. Describe the spot on the screen for the two lenses. Which color is on the outside of the spot, and why? (Hint: the screen is at the focus for yellow light; ask which colors have already crossed.)

## Uncertainty

The demo's lens is a thin lens with the glass makers' measured indices, and its mirror is a perfect sphere. Real lenses and spherical mirrors also blur the focus because rays far from the center do not cross at quite the same place as rays near it (spherical aberration). Look closely at where the mirror's rays cross: do the outer rays cross at exactly the same place as the inner ones? Say how this would affect a star's image.

## Questions
