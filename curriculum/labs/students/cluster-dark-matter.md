## Goal

Weigh a cluster of galaxies three ways, from the speeds of its galaxies, from its hot gas and from the way it bends light, and find out how much of its mass we cannot see.

## Background

**Zwicky's puzzle.** In 1933 Fritz Zwicky measured how fast the galaxies in the Coma cluster move. A cluster holds together only if its gravity can keep its fastest galaxies from flying off, and Zwicky found the galaxies far too fast for the mass he could see. The same rule that weighs a planet's orbit weighs the cluster: galaxies orbiting at speed v at radius R need a mass

$$M = \frac{v^2 R}{G}$$

inside R. With R in kpc, v in km/s and M in solar masses, $$G = 4.30\times10^{-6}$$ kpc (km/s)²/M☉. A redshift gives only the part of a galaxy's speed toward or away from us. In this cluster the spread of those speeds, σ, is related to the orbit speed by $$v = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma$$.

**Hot gas.** X-ray telescopes show that clusters are full of gas at about $$10^8$$ K, with more mass in the gas than in all the stars of the galaxies. Gas that hot moves at over 1,000 km/s and would escape unless gravity held it in, so its temperature weighs the cluster too. By Wien's law, a glow at temperature T peaks at the wavelength

$$\lambda_{\text{peak}} = \frac{2.90\times10^6\ \text{nm K}}{T}.$$

**Bent light.** Mass bends light. A galaxy right behind a cluster can be stretched into arcs, or into a whole ring, the Einstein ring. The ring's size gives the mass inside it, whatever that mass is made of.

**The Bullet Cluster.** Two clusters crashed through each other, about 150 million years before the light we see left them. Their galaxies, far apart from one another, sailed through. Their hot gas clouds, most of the ordinary mass, slammed together and stalled in the middle, where X-rays show them. Lensing then shows where most of the mass is.

## Uncertainty

The spread σ is measured from about 500 galaxies, so it is uncertain by about $$1/\sqrt{2 \times 500}$$, roughly 3%. How large is the resulting uncertainty in the mass, which goes as σ²? Is it big enough to explain the gap between the mass the speeds need and the stars and gas?
