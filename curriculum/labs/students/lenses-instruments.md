## Goal

Measure where thin lenses form images, focus a camera, and measure how an image is stretched along the axis.

## Background

A thin lens of focal length $$f$$ ($$f > 0$$ converging, $$f < 0$$ diverging) forms the image of an object a distance $$d_o$$ away at $$d_i$$, with

$$\frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}, \qquad m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}.$$

$$d_i > 0$$ is a real image on the far side of the lens; $$d_i < 0$$ is a virtual image on the object's side. A camera is a converging lens that makes a real image on a sensor; to focus on a nearer object, the lens moves farther from the sensor.

**Depth of an image.** A lens makes images of points at different distances at different places, so an object with depth along the axis has an image with depth too. For two points of the object at $$d_{o1}$$ and $$d_{o2}$$, with magnifications $$m_1$$ and $$m_2$$, the thin lens equation gives exactly

$$\frac{\lvert d_{i2}-d_{i1}\rvert}{\lvert d_{o2}-d_{o1}\rvert} = m_1m_2,$$

close to $$m^2$$ for a thin object. So an image is stretched or squeezed along the axis by about the square of its height magnification.

## Before the lab

1. A converging lens has $$f = 10$$ cm. Find the image distance and magnification for an object 15 cm from it, and for one 5.0 cm from it. Describe each image.
2. A diverging lens has $$f = -10$$ cm. Find the image distance and magnification for an object 20 cm from it.

## Safety

There are no hazards in the simulation. Never look at the Sun through a lens or camera viewfinder.

## Procedure

Before each part, predict what you will see. Then measure.

**Part 1: converging lens.** Choose the setup Converging lens ($$f = 10$$ cm; the object starts 30 cm from the lens). Click the object arrow and move it with the arrow keys (0.5 cm a press) until the Object distance dₒ readout in the Image section is about 30, 20, 15, 12, 8 and 5 cm, and press Record reading at each. Export to worksheet, then Clear.

**Part 2: focusing a camera.** With the object about 30 cm from the lens, click the screen and move it with the arrow keys until the picture on it is sharpest; read the distance from the lens to the screen on the 1 cm grid and write it under the Part 1 table (the table has no notes column). Repeat with the object about 20 cm away. (Nothing new is recorded.)

**Part 3: diverging lens.** Click the lens and press Diverging in its editor (the Focal length slider stays at 10 cm, so $$f = -10$$ cm). Move the object to about 30, 20, 10 and 5 cm from the lens and record at each. Export to worksheet, then Clear.

**Part 4: depth of an image.** Choose the setup A shape, point by point ($$f = 8$$ cm). Click the house and move it until its middle (halfway between the two dₒ values that the Object depth along the axis readout gives for its ends) is about 24, 20, 17, 14, 12 and 11 cm from the lens, and record at each. Then choose A shape in a magnifier and record it as it opens. Send this series; it goes to the Depth table.

**Using the virtual apparatus.** Changing a focal length does not move the lens, so move it afterward. Send each part to the worksheet before clearing, so each part gets its own copy of the Data table. The last two columns are for you to fill in (Parts 1 and 3).

## Analysis

1. For Part 1, fill in the $$1/d_o$$ and $$1/d_i$$ columns and graph $$1/d_i$$ (vertical) against $$1/d_o$$ (horizontal). Find $$f$$ from the intercept and say what the slope should be. Check $$m = -d_i/d_o$$. Describe the image when the object is beyond $$2f$$, between $$2f$$ and $$f$$, and inside $$f$$.
2. For Part 2, compare the screen distances with $$d_i$$. Which way did the screen move as the object came closer? Explain how a camera focuses, and why a camera cannot focus on an object closer than $$f$$.
3. For Part 3, make the same graph and find $$f$$. Why does a diverging lens always give a virtual, upright, smaller image?
4. For Part 4, graph the Depth ratio column (vertical) against the $$m_1m_2$$ column (horizontal). What slope do you find? Compare the depth ratio with the height ratio and with its square for each row. As the house moves toward the focal point, which grows faster, the height or the depth of the image? In the magnifier, is the letter F's virtual image stretched or squeezed along the axis, and by how much compared with its height?

## Uncertainty

The model has no noise, and its lenses are ideal thin lenses, so the traced image distances agree with the thin lens equation to the 0.01 cm they are given to. Your own uncertainty is in where you put the object and the lenses. Real lenses have thickness, spherical aberration and dispersion (color fringes).
