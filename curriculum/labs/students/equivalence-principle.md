## Goal

Test Einstein's equivalence principle in a windowless room, then follow it to two predictions about gravity, the bending of light and the turning of Mercury's orbit, and check them against the 1919 eclipse and a century of watching Mercury.

## Background

**What a scale reads.** A bathroom scale measures how hard the floor pushes up on you. Standing still on Earth, the floor holds you up against gravity, and the push is your weight $$mg$$, with $$g = 9.81$$ m/s². In an elevator speeding up on its way up, the floor must push harder, $$m(g + a)$$, so you read heavier. In a rocket far out in space, with no gravity at all, the floor must push you along with the rocket: if it speeds up at a, the scale reads $$ma$$. A scale calibrated on Earth turns the push into "kilograms" by dividing by g.

**The happiest thought.** In 1907 Einstein realized that a person falling off a roof would feel no weight. Inside a closed room you cannot tell standing still on Earth from speeding up at g in a rocket: a dropped ball falls to the floor in the same time, $$t = \sqrt{2h/a}$$ for a drop of height h, and the scale reads the same. That is the **equivalence principle**: locally, gravity and acceleration cannot be told apart. Falling freely is the same as floating with no gravity, which is why astronauts in orbit float: they and their station fall around the Earth together.

**Gravity bends light.** A beam of light enters one wall of an accelerating rocket and crosses to the other. From outside it goes straight, but while it crosses, the floor speeds up toward it, so it lands a little lower than it started:

$$\text{drop} = \tfrac12 a\left(\frac{L}{c}\right)^2$$

for a room of width L. If a rocket bends light, then by the equivalence principle gravity must bend light too. Near the Sun, Einstein's theory predicts that starlight grazing the Sun's edge bends by 1.75 arcseconds (″), twice what Newton's gravity would give if light were a stream of particles. Two expeditions measured it at the total eclipse of 29 May 1919, at Sobral (Brazil) and Príncipe (off West Africa).

**Mercury.** Mercury's elliptical orbit slowly turns, so its closest point to the Sun (the perihelion) moves around the Sun. The pulls of the other planets explain most of the turning; about 43″ a century was left over. Einstein's theory adds exactly that amount, with nothing adjusted to fit. A full turn is 360° × 3,600″/° = 1,296,000″.

$$c = 3.00\times10^8\ \text{m/s},\quad 1\ \text{kg} \leftrightarrow 2.205\ \text{lb on an Earth scale},\quad \text{proton diameter} \approx 1.68\times10^{-15}\ \text{m}.$$

## Before the lab

1. A person who weighs 150 lb at rest stands on a scale in an elevator speeding up on its way up at g/10. What does the scale read? Now put the same person and scale in a rocket far out in space, speeding up at g. What does it read?
2. A beam of light crosses a room 3.0 m wide that is speeding up at 9.8 m/s². How long does the light take to cross, and how far does the floor rise meanwhile? Compare the drop with the diameter of a proton.
3. Mercury orbits the Sun about 415 times a century. If relativity turns its orbit by 43″ a century, how much is that each orbit? How many years would relativity alone take to turn the orbit all the way round?
4. Astronauts float inside the International Space Station, about 400 km up, where gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground. What would a bathroom scale under an astronaut read, and why do they float? Write your answer and one sentence of reasoning before you open the demo.

## Analysis

1. Graph the rocket scale in newtons (vertical) against the rocket's acceleration (horizontal) and fit a straight line. What are the slope and the intercept, and what does the slope tell you about the person?
2. At which acceleration do the two rooms give the same scale reading and the same time to fall? At 1.10 g, what does the scale read in pounds? Compare with Before the lab 1.
3. How many times bigger is the drop at 10¹⁵ g than at g? Is that what $$\text{drop} = \tfrac12 a(L/c)^2$$ says it should be?
4. How long would relativity alone take to turn Mercury's orbit all the way round? Use the demo's relativity per century.

## Questions

1. Someone locked in a windowless rocket speeding up at 9.81 m/s² insists they are standing on Earth. Using your 9.81 m/s² row, is there anything they could do with the ball and the scale to prove otherwise?
2. In Part 3, from outside the box the light goes straight; from inside it curves down. Explain how the equivalence principle turns this into the prediction that the Sun bends starlight, and why the 1919 astronomers needed an eclipse to see it.
3. Return to Before the lab 4. In Falling is floating, what did both scales read, and how did the falling room on Earth compare with your 0 m/s² row (the rocket coasting in empty space)? Use that to explain why astronauts on the space station float even though gravity there is about 90% as strong as on the ground, and say whether your prediction matched.
