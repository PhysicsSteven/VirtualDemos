## Goal

Find out which way a comet's tails point and why, work out how long a comet takes to go around the Sun, and explain why a meteor shower comes back on the same date every year.

## Background

**What a comet is.** A comet is a lump of ice, dust and rock a few kilometers across, left over from when the planets formed. Far from the Sun it is frozen and dark, with no tail at all. Inside about 3 au (three times Earth's distance from the Sun), sunlight warms it enough that the ice turns straight to gas. The gas streams off and carries dust with it, making a glowing cloud around the solid nucleus, the **coma**.

**Two tails.** Two things come out of the Sun and push on that gas and dust.

- The **solar wind** is a stream of charged particles leaving the Sun at about 400 km/s. It catches the gas that sunlight has charged up and blows it straight out from the Sun: the narrow, bluish **ion tail**. The comet moves at tens of km/s, much slower than the wind, so the ion tail leans only a few degrees away from the straight out direction.
- **Sunlight** itself pushes gently on the dust grains. Each grain then follows its own orbit, a little behind the comet, so the **dust tail** is broad, yellowish and curved back along the comet's path.

Both tails are pushed outward by what comes from the Sun, so a tail works like a windsock, not like smoke trailing behind a train.

**How long the year of a comet is.** Comets move on long, stretched ellipses. Its **closest** distance to the Sun is $$q$$ and its **farthest** is $$Q$$. Kepler's third law uses the average distance

$$a = \frac{q + Q}{2},$$

and for anything going around the Sun, with $$a$$ in au and the period $$P$$ in years,

$$P = a^{1.5}.$$

Comets with periods under about 200 years mostly come from the Kuiper belt, a flat disk past Neptune. Comets with periods of thousands to millions of years come from the Oort cloud, a huge sphere of icy bodies out to about 100,000 au.

**Meteor showers.** Each time a comet rounds the Sun it sheds dust along its orbit. After many passes the whole orbit is lined with a stream of grains, mostly smaller than grains of sand. If Earth's orbit crosses the stream, Earth plows through it at the same place in its own orbit, and so on the same date, every year. The grains burn up as **meteors**, streaks of light high in the air. Earth moves at about 30 km/s. The grains hit the air fast when Earth meets the stream head on, and slowly when Earth catches up with the stream from behind.

Look at the **Heading** and **Angle between ion tail and motion** readouts at day 40, which are in your table. ## Analysis

1. From Table 1, list the readings where Halley is heading toward the Sun and those where it is heading away. In which readings does the ion tail stream out behind the comet (angle above 90°), and in which does it go out ahead (below 90°)? About what is the angle at perihelion, day 0?
2. Divide Halley's fastest speed in Table 1 by the solar wind's 400 km/s. Use that ratio to explain why the Ion tail from straight out column never gets past a few degrees.
3. Fill in Table 2: $$a = (q + Q)/2$$ and then $$a^{1.5}$$ for each comet. How close is $$a^{1.5}$$ to the demo's period? Which of the seven comets are short period (under 200 years), and which probably came from the Oort cloud?
4. From Table 3, how many days apart are the computed and observed dates for each shower? Halley's orbit gives two showers a year, the Orionids in October and the Eta Aquariids in May. What does that tell you about where Halley's orbit passes Earth's?
5. Rank the five showers from the largest angle between Earth's motion and the dust's to the smallest, and then by their speed into the air. Do the two rankings agree?
