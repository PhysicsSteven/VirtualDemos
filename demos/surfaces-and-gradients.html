Surfaces and Gradients

Type a function z = f(x, y) and see it as a surface beside its contour map. Cut it with planes to read the partial derivatives as slopes, lay a tangent plane on it, turn a direction arrow to read the directional derivative, and see the gradient point straight uphill, across the level curves. Find peaks, pits and saddles with the second derivative test, slide along a constraint until it touches a level curve (Lagrange multipliers), approach a point along different paths, and walk a point along a path to see the chain rule. Derivatives are exact.

    • Use x and y. + − * / ^ and parentheses; 2x, xy and x(y+1) multiply.
    • sin cos tan exp ln sqrt abs atan and the rest, pi, e; e^(-x^2) works.
    • The box c takes a number or an expression such as 2pi.
    • ln is the natural log and log is base 10.
    • ^ takes only the number right after it: e^2x is e²·x, so type e^(2x) for e to the power 2x.
    The model and its formulas

    ∇f = (∂f/∂x, ∂f/∂y), Duf = ∇f · u = |∇f| cos θ, tangent plane z = f(a, b) + fx(x − a) + fy(y − b)

    D = fxxfyy − fxy², at ∇f = 0: D > 0 and fxx > 0 a pit (minimum), D > 0 and fxx < 0 a peak (maximum), D < 0 a saddle, D = 0 the test says nothing

    ∇f = λ∇g and g(x, y) = c at a constrained extreme

    z(t) = f(x(t), y(t)): dz/dt = ∂f/∂x · dx/dt + ∂f/∂y · dy/dt = ∇f · r′(t)

    The boxes are read by a small parser written for this site (nothing you type is run as code). Every partial derivative shown is the exact formula from the rules of calculus, evaluated at the point, so the slopes, the gradient, the Hessian and D are exact. Critical points are found by Newton's method from a grid of starting points over the square the surface shows, and constrained extremes by Newton's method on ∇f = λ∇g, g = c, both to about twelve significant figures; a point outside that square is not searched for.

    The ball moves in the xy plane with acceleration a = −∇U/m − b v, m = 1 kg, U the typed function in joules, stepped by fourth order Runge Kutta. It is a particle in a potential energy U(x, y), not a ball rolling on a real hill (which would also spin and feel the slope's steepness).

    Charges: kq = 898.8 V·cm for q = 1 nC, from the CODATA 2022 Coulomb constant, rounded to four figures in the box; lengths in centimeters, so E comes out in V/cm (100 V/m). The ideal gas uses R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) (the exact SI value, rounded to four figures in the box) for n = 1 mol, V in liters, so P is in kPa.

    Chain rule: x′(t) and y′(t) are exact derivatives of the typed path, so the chain rule value is exact. The rate from the values along the path is the centered difference [z(t + Δt) − z(t − Δt)]/(2Δt) with Δt = 10⁻⁴ of the time range, which differs from dz/dt by about (Δt)²z‴/6; the readout shows the two side by side.

    The surface is drawn on a 72 by 72 grid and cut off where it leaves the box; contours on the map are traced on a finer grid. A limit is read from the values at a distance of 10⁻⁶ of the view from the point.
