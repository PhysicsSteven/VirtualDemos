Reasons for Seasons

Seasons come from the tilt of Earth's axis, not from its distance to the Sun. Earth is closest to the Sun in January.

Distance or tilt?

Distance
— Tilt here
—

Yearly swing in daily insolation, as ± percent of the middle value. The distance swing comes from 1/r², the tilt swing from the chart at constant 1 AU.

More about this

Earth is closest to the Sun around January 3, in Northern winter. Distance also changes both hemispheres together, so it cannot make their seasons opposite.

More about this

Stretch ellipse draws the orbit with e = 0.25 so its shape is visible. Timing and readouts still use the real orbit, e = 0.0167, which looks like a circle.

More about this

Sun and Earth sizes are not to scale. Day length uses the center of the Sun and ignores refraction, so real days are a few minutes longer. Sunrise and sunset times are local solar time. Perihelion is fixed at January 3; the date of the March equinox follows from that.
