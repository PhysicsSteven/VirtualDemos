Solids of Revolution

Spin a region under any curve around an axis and slice the solid into disks, washers or shells you can count. Raise n and the sum closes in on the integral. Stand squares or semicircles on a base, lay a chain along a curve for its length and bands for its surface area, and add up a cone's or a sphere's moment of inertia slice by slice.

What you can type
  • Use x as the variable; + − * / ^ and parentheses; 2x and 3sin(x) multiply.
  • sin cos tan, exp or e^x, ln, sqrt, cosh, abs, pi
  • The limits and k take a number or an expression: pi, sqrt(84), -1.
  • ln is the natural log and log is base 10.
  • ^ takes only the number right after it: e^2x is e²·x, so type e^(2x) for e to the power 2x.
How it works

The boxes are read by a small parser (demos/lib/vdmath.js); nothing you type is run as code. The interval from a to b is cut into n slices of width Δx = (b − a)/n. A disk or washer takes its radii at the middle of its slice, R and r measured from the line y = k: π(R² − r²)Δx. A shell around the line x = k takes its radius and height at the middle: 2π r h Δx. A cross section takes its side s = f − g at the middle: s², πs²/8 or (√3/4)s², times Δx.

The arc length chain joins the points of the curve at the ends of the slices, Σ √(Δx² + Δy²). Each surface band is the frustum of a cone through two neighboring points, π(r₁ + r₂) times the chord. The integrals they close in on are computed by 10 point Gauss–Legendre quadrature on 400 pieces, good to about 12 significant figures for smooth curves.

Moment of inertia about the axis: a uniform solid of mass M has density ρ = M/V. A thin washer adds ½ρπ(R⁴ − r⁴)Δx and a thin shell 2πρ r³ h Δx. A sphere's or cone's I is then compared with MR², R its largest radius.

Disks and washers here turn about a horizontal line y = k, and shells about a vertical line x = k, so each slice stays a function of x.
