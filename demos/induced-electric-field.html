Induced Electric Fields

A long solenoid seen end on. While its current changes, the changing magnetic flux makes an electric field that circles the solenoid, inside it and in the empty space outside. Move a closed path around and compare the work per charge around it, ∮E·dl, with −dΦ/dt.

Blind hides the field arrows, the field plot, E on the path and ∮E·dl. Predict first: is there an electric field outside the solenoid, where B = 0? Which way does it point? What is ∮E·dl around a path that does not go around the solenoid?

More about this

Model: an ideal long solenoid, B = μ₀nI inside and zero outside, with the current changing slowly enough that the field inside is uniform at each instant. By symmetry the induced field circles the axis, and Faraday's law, ∮E·dl = −dΦ/dt (OpenStax Eq. 13.10), gives E = (r/2)|dB/dt| inside and E = (R²/2r)|dB/dt| outside (OpenStax Example 13.8). The page adds up E·dl around the path in 720 short pieces and, separately, finds Φ from B times the area of the path that lies inside the solenoid (the overlap of two circles); the two agree. The solenoid's own end effects, its resistance and the field from any current in the ring are left out. Time runs slowed down; the factor is printed on the picture.
