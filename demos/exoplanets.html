More about this

Transits measure the radius; radial velocity measures the mass times sin i. A transit also tells you i is near 90°, so together they give the true mass, and mass over volume gives the density: rock, water or gas.

The model and its formulas

Model: one planet and its star in a two body orbit. The planet's own light is ignored, so there is no secondary eclipse. Limb darkening is quadratic with fixed Sun like coefficients (u₁ = 0.40, u₂ = 0.26): the middle of the star is brighter than its average, so at the middle of a central transit the dip is 1/(1 − u₁/3 − u₂/6) = 1.215 times (Rp/Rs)² for a small planet (1.210 times when Rp/Rs = 0.2). The duration readout uses the standard approximation for eccentric orbits. Noise is white and Gaussian. With noise, the readouts and Record reading give the measurement nearest the cursor (brightness from the closely spaced set inside the transit window, else from the whole orbit set) and the time of that measurement; without noise they give the true curves at the cursor. A mystery system draws a star of 0.70 to 1.30 M☉ (radius M^0.8) and a rocky planet, a mini Neptune or a giant, with a density in that kind's range, a circular orbit 0.03 to 0.10 AU out, and an impact parameter below 0.5. In the top view the star and planet are drawn larger than scale.
