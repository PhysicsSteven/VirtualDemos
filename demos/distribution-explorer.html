How it works

Every probability is computed from the distribution's cumulative distribution function in demos/lib/vdstats.js: exact sums for the discrete ones (the binomial and Poisson through the incomplete beta and gamma functions), and for the continuous ones the error function (normal), the incomplete beta function (t and F) and the incomplete gamma function (chi square), good to about ten significant figures. The book reads these from tables or a TI-83 or TI-84; the command shown is the one the book writes, with 1E99 for an open end.

For a discrete distribution, a and b are whole numbers and both ends count: "between" means a ≤ X ≤ b. For a continuous one, P(a < X < b) = P(a ≤ X ≤ b), since a single value has probability zero.

A percentile is the value with that area to its left: invNorm and invT on the calculator; for the other continuous distributions the demo finds it by bisection on the cumulative distribution function. For a discrete one it is the smallest x whose cumulative probability reaches the area.

The draws are simulated with seeded random numbers (the same seed gives the same draws): binomial and hypergeometric by counting successes trial by trial, Poisson by multiplying uniform numbers (or by inverting its cumulative distribution for a large mean), geometric and exponential by inverting their cumulative distributions, normal by the polar method, chi square as a sum of df squared standard normals, t as a standard normal over √(χ²/df), F as a ratio of two chi squares each divided by its df. The histogram is scaled to the curve: for continuous data its bars have area equal to the fraction of draws in them.

The normal approximation to a discrete distribution uses the continuity correction: P(a ≤ X ≤ b) is approximated by the normal area from a − 0.5 to b + 0.5.
