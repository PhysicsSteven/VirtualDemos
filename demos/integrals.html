  • sin cos tan sec csc cot, asin acos atan, sinh cosh tanh, exp or e^x, ln, log (base 10), sqrt, abs or |x|, pi
  • A data table: one reading per line, x then y, separated by a comma, a space or a tab. The x values must increase.
How it works

The box is read by a small parser (demos/lib/vdmath.js); nothing you type is run as code. With n pieces of width h = (b − a)/n, the Left, Right and Midpoint sums add h times f at the left end, the right end or the middle of each piece; the Trapezoid sum joins the points with straight lines; Simpson's rule (n even) fits a parabola through each pair of pieces.

The exact integral comes from an antiderivative F found by the basic rules (powers, e^x, sin, cos, ln and the like, with a linear inside), as F(b) − F(a). When the basic rules do not give one (the bell curve, x·e^x, |x|), the integral is computed numerically by adaptive Simpson's rule with Richardson extrapolation, good to about 13 significant figures, and the readout says so. Every sum is computed exactly as its formula says, in double precision.

For the slopes on the error graph, n doubles from 1 to 4096 and a straight line is fitted to log |error| against log n over the largest n whose error is still well above rounding (about 10⁻¹³ of the integral). A rule of order p has error close to C/nᵖ, so its slope is −p.

Area as b grows: the area from a to b for b out to a + 10¹². The limit is given when the area changes by less than 10⁻⁹ of itself over the last two factors of ten in b; otherwise the readout says it is still changing there. A data table is integrated only by the trapezoid, left and right rules, between the readings given: there is no curve between them, so there is no exact integral to compare with.

Angles are in radians.
