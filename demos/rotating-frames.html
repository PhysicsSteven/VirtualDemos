Rotating Frames

Roll a ball across a turning merry go round and watch it twice: from the room it goes straight, from the turntable it curves away, pushed by the Coriolis and centrifugal forces that only a rider feels. Then go to the Earth, which is a turntable too: a Foucault pendulum's swing turns once in a sidereal day divided by the sine of the latitude.

The model and its formulas

a′ = a − 2Ω × v′ − Ω × (Ω × r′) rolling ball: dv/dt = (2/7) Ω ẑ × v Foucault: Ω sin λ, one turn in T⋆ / sin λ

Turntable: a flat table of radius 2 m turning at a steady rate Ω about its center, counterclockwise seen from above when the speed is positive. The ball is a point on the table top; air drag is left out. Slides, no friction: no horizontal force acts, so in the room the ball moves in a straight line at a steady speed (an air hockey puck comes close). Rolls: a uniform solid ball (I = 2mR²/5) rolling without slipping from the start, with no rolling resistance. Friction from the table is then the only horizontal force; the no slip condition and the torque it makes give dv/dt = (2/7) Ω ẑ × v, so in the room the ball goes round a circle at 2Ω/7, turning the same way as the table (Weltner 1979, Am. J. Phys. 47, 984; Romer 1981, Am. J. Phys. 49, 985). Seen riding the turntable, positions and velocities are turned back by the angle the table has turned, r′ = R(−Ωt) r and v′ = R(−Ωt)(v − Ω × r); in that frame Newton's second law needs two more terms, the Coriolis acceleration −2Ω × v′ and the centrifugal acceleration Ω²r′ outward. Thrown by a rider, the ball's speed and aim are measured on the table, so the room sees the rider's own motion Ω × r added. The force arrows are forces per kilogram (accelerations), all to one scale. Foucault pendulum: a bob on a long wire, small swings (the wire stays within a few degrees of vertical), so the pendulum is a two dimensional harmonic oscillator, ω₀ = √(g/L), g = 9.81 m/s², with the horizontal part of the Coriolis acceleration, 2Ω sin λ (vnorth, −veast), Ω = 7.2921151 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s (the rate of the Earth Rotation Angle, IERS Conventions 2010). The equations are solved exactly: x + iy = e−iΩ sin λ t(P eiωt + Q e−iωt), ω = √(ω₀² + Ω² sin² λ). The swing's plane turns clockwise in the north (counterclockwise in the south) at Ω sin λ, once in T⋆/|sin λ|, where T⋆ = 2π/Ω = 86 164.099 s = 23 h 56 min 4.099 s is the Earth's turn relative to the stars (the sidereal day). Left out and stated: the vertical part of the Coriolis force (it changes the bob's weight by a few parts in 10⁶), the centrifugal force (it is folded into the measured g and the local vertical), air drag (a real pendulum must be given small pushes to keep swinging), and the slow turning of a swing that is slightly elliptical (Airy's precession, about 3ab/(8L²) ω₀ for semi axes a and b; real Foucault pendulums damp out ellipticity with a Charron ring). Spin the Earth 1000 times faster multiplies Ω by 1000 so each swing visibly curves; the readouts say so. The Paris setup uses Foucault's Panthéon pendulum of 1851: 67 m wire, latitude 48.846° N.
