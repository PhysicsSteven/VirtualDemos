Photoelectric Effect

Shine light on a metal and catch the electrons it knocks loose. Color decides their energy; brightness only decides how many.

Metal

More about this

Cesium, potassium, calcium, zinc and platinum use Michaelson's preferred values for clean polycrystalline surfaces (H. B. Michaelson, J. Appl. Phys. 48, 4729 (1977), Table III); sodium (2.28 eV) and copper (4.70 eV) use the values in the HyperPhysics table of work functions. A work function depends on the crystal face and on how clean the surface is, so tables differ by a few tenths of an eV: OpenStax Table 6.1 lists sodium 2.46, zinc 4.31 and platinum 6.35 eV.

Circuit

More about this

Negative V pushes electrons back toward the metal. The voltage that just stops the fastest ones is the stopping voltage V₀, and eV₀ = Kmax.

More about this

Record V₀ at several wavelengths. The points fall on a straight line of Kmax against f: the slope is h, and the intercept is the work function. Record V₀ saves the collector voltage you have set, so lower V until the meter just reads 0.00 nA first. The error bars are the ±0.01 V step of the voltage control. Untick Theory line and answers to hide the model's Kmax and V₀ while you measure.

Layers
The model and its formulas

Model: each absorbed photon frees at most one electron, with a kinetic energy spread evenly between 0 and Kmax. One electron per thousand photons (quantum efficiency 0.1%). Electron speeds on screen are slowed enormously; the fastest real ones move at hundreds of km/s.
