On the picture: [ and ] pick a body; the up and down arrows make it faster or slower and the left and right arrows turn its velocity (Shift for small steps); C puts it on a circular orbit; Delete removes it; Space plays or pauses; F fits the view; + and − zoom.

The model

Every body pulls on every other by Newton's law of gravity, F = G m₁m₂/r², in AU, years and solar masses (G = 4π² AU³/M☉·yr²). The motion is stepped with velocity Verlet, in smaller steps during close passes; the change in total energy shows the numerical error. Bodies are drawn bigger than life; their real radii decide when two touch, and then they merge, keeping their total mass and momentum. The orbit shown for the selected body treats it and its host as if they were alone, so P² = a³/(M + m).

Readings

Captured readings: semi major axis, period from the timer, ruler distance and star mass
More about this

Capture stores the timer and the ruler as displayed, with the star mass and the launch. The a column starts as half the ruler distance, which is the semi major axis when the points sit on perihelion and aphelion; edit it if you measured another way. The fit is a least squares line T² = slope·a³ + intercept, using the rows with the present star mass; the intercept should come out near zero.

Energy bars

Per kilogram of planet: kinetic energy v²/2, gravitational potential energy −GM/r (zero far away), and their sum ε. As the planet falls in, kinetic energy grows by exactly what potential energy loses, so the total stays put. A negative total is a bound orbit; zero or above, the planet escapes.

More about this

The star is held fixed and the planet's mass is neglected, so P² = a³/M exactly (a in AU, P in years, M in solar masses). Two planets do not pull on each other. The motion is integrated with velocity Verlet using a time symmetric step that shrinks near the star. A planet that comes within 0.005 AU (one solar radius) hits the star.
