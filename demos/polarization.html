More about this

Each step passes cos²(90°/N). The loss per step falls as 1/N², faster than the number of steps grows, so the total climbs toward 1: cos²ᴺ(π/2N) ≈ e^(−π²/4N), the dashed curve. N = 1 is crossed polarizers; N = 2 is the three polarizer surprise.

The model and its formulas

rs = (n₁cos θi − n₂cos θt)/(n₁cos θi + n₂cos θt)
rp = (n₂cos θi − n₁cos θt)/(n₂cos θi + n₁cos θt)
R = |r|², T = 1 − R, tan θB = n₂/n₁

s: E out of the page (dots) p: E in the plane of incidence (arrows) Unpolarized, (Rs + Rp)/2

Light reflected from a level surface such as water or a road has its s component horizontal, parallel to the surface. Polarized sunglasses have a vertical transmission axis, so they pass only the p part of the glare. At Brewster's angle the reflected and refracted rays are at 90° and Rp = 0.

Model

Ideal elements: a polarizer passes the field component along its axis and absorbs the rest; wave plates are lossless and give exactly a quarter or half wave of delay to the slow axis. Polarized input is followed with Jones vectors, Eout = J Ein, with E = Re[E ei(kz − ωt)]. Unpolarized input is followed with Stokes vectors and Mueller matrices built from the same Jones matrices, Mij = ½ tr(σi J σj J†); at the first polarizer this is exactly the one half rule.

Everything is drawn as seen by an observer facing the oncoming beam. Right circular means the field turns clockwise in that view (the optics convention). The ellipse size shows the field amplitude, √(I/I₀). For unpolarized light the arrow wanders at random, much more slowly than real light, whose polarization changes in about a coherence time.

Fresnel equations for a flat, smooth interface between two transparent, nonmagnetic media (μ = μ₀). T includes the factor n₂cos θt/n₁cos θi for the change in beam width and speed, so R + T = 1 for each polarization. Indices are for visible light: water 1.33, crown glass 1.50. Beyond the critical angle the reflection is total. Marker sizes show field amplitudes.
