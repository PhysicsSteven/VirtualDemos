Thin Film Interference

Light reflects from both surfaces of a thin film. The two reflections add or cancel depending on the extra path through the film and on which surfaces flip the wave, and white light comes back colored.

Display gain

Color model: the reflectance spectrum times CIE illuminant D65 (tabulated every 10 nm, interpolated linearly), integrated against the CIE 1931 2° color matching functions in the analytic fit of Wyman, Sloan and Shirley (2013), 380 to 780 nm, then converted to sRGB. A flat spectrum comes out exactly neutral. Gain 1× is true brightness against black, with a perfect mirror as white; a film reflects only a few percent, so 3× or 10× brightens it. Colors outside sRGB are desaturated and those too bright are scaled down.

With n₁ < nf < n₂ both reflections flip, so the shifts cancel and only the path counts: a quarter wave film puts ray 2 half a cycle behind ray 1. If nf² = n₁n₂ the two reflections are equally strong and cancel completely at λ₀. MgF₂ (1.38) is used because it is durable, though √1.52 = 1.23 would be ideal on glass.

Profile

t = ttop + (tbottom − ttop)(y/h)p, with y the depth. Linear (p = 1) is a simple wedge with evenly spaced bands. √ depth (p = ½) is the shape of a film draining under gravity between rigid surfaces, which squeezes the first colors into a thin strip at the top. Play drains the film: the profile thins as 1/√time, sped up. The black film at the top is far thinner than λ, so the path adds almost nothing and the single π shift makes the two reflections cancel. Real black films are a stable layer a few nm to about 30 nm thick that grows down from the top.

Two 50 mm glass slides touch at the left and are held apart by a spacer (a hair or a strip of foil) at the right. The gap grows linearly, t = x h/L, so the fringes are evenly spaced: one fringe for each λ/2 of extra gap (λ/2nf in general), Δx = λL/(2nfh). Drag the spacer in the side view.

A convex lens rests on a flat plate. The gap is t = R − √(R² − r²) ≈ r²/2R, so the rings crowd together outward. With an air gap the center is dark and dark rings sit at r = √(mλR/nf), m = 0, 1, 2, at normal incidence.

Reflectance formula

r = (r₁₂ + r₂₃e2iβ) / (1 + r₁₂r₂₃e2iβ)
β = 2π nf t cos θt / λ, R = |r|²

This is the exact single layer (Airy) result: it sums every reflection inside the film, not just the first two. r₁₂ and r₂₃ are the Fresnel coefficients of the two surfaces for the chosen polarization (unpolarized light averages s and p). Keeping only the first two reflections gives r ≈ r₁₂ + (1 − r₁₂²)r₂₃e2iβ, which is very close when each surface reflects only a few percent.

In the ray drawing the film is drawn far thicker than scale (the drawn thickness grows as the cube root of t). Rays are at their true angles, except that below 20° they are drawn at 20° so they can be told apart. The dotted line from where ray 2 leaves the film to ray 1 is a line of equal phase: from there on both rays travel the same distance, so the extra path is nf(AB + BC) inside the film minus n₁ times the stretch of ray 1 before that line, which works out to 2nft cos θt. The phase shifts shown are those of s polarization (or p if chosen); beyond Brewster's angle the p reflection loses its π shift. The wave picture shows the first two reflections only. The reflected waves are drawn side by side for clarity; in reality they overlap.
