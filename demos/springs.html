Springs and Oscillations

A mass on a spring is pulled back toward equilibrium with a force proportional to how far it is displaced. That one fact gives simple harmonic motion, and with damping and a drive, decay and resonance.

More about this

With k1 = k2, mode 1 has both blocks moving together, so the middle spring never stretches, and mode 2 has them moving opposite, so it adds 2kc. With unequal outer springs the modes are lopsided: one block swings more than the other, shown by the amplitude ratio below. Any motion is a sum of the two modes.

Damping
More about this

The oil pushes on each block with a force −bv. The damping ratio ζ = b/(2√(km)) sorts the motion: ζ < 1 underdamped, ζ = 1 critically damped, ζ > 1 overdamped. Drag the grip on the oil to change b.

Drive

More about this

The wall at the end of the spring moves as Xw cos ωt. That pulls on the block exactly like a force F₀ cos ωt with F₀ = kXw. The sweep starts at the steady state for its first ω and raises ω slowly; if b is small the oscillator needs time 2m/b to catch up, so the measured dots fall a little below the peak and ring just after it, more so for a lightly damped spring.

Measurements

Phase plot

Velocity against displacement for the block (block 1 with two masses), with the last few seconds as a trail. Without damping the path is a closed ellipse: the block passes the same points at the same speeds every cycle, because energy is conserved. Damping turns it into a spiral in to the middle. A drive settles it onto one ellipse at the drive frequency.

Stopwatch and ruler
More about this

Press Stopwatch (or W on the drawing) as the block passes a mark you choose, count N full oscillations, and press it again as the block passes the same mark going the same way. The stopwatch runs on simulation time, so slow motion makes timing easier, but your own reaction time still counts. It reads to 0.01 s. The gold ticks on the ruler hold the largest |x| since Reset peak.

More about this

Unknown spring picks a random k from 5 to 60 N/m and hides it: the k slider, the k labels, the energy values, the wall amplitude and the k column of the CSV. It turns Blind on, and the ζ buttons are off because they would give k away. The measured period stays. Change m, time the period and find k from the slope of T² against m. One block only. Turning it off shows k, so you can check your answer.

Model: ideal massless springs that obey Hooke's law, a frictionless track, a damping force −bv on each block, g = 9.81 m/s². The curves are the exact solution of the linear equation of motion, not a step by step integration; two coupled blocks (equal masses; outer springs k₁ and k₂, middle spring k_c, the same damping b on each) move as the sum of their two normal modes, found exactly from the 2 × 2 stiffness matrix. Positions are drawn to scale; the block's size grows with its mass but is not to scale. The drawing never shows a spring pressed solid, and the model has no such limit.
