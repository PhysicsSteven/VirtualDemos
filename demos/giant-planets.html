Set up

The test moon is a pile of 200 loose balls held together only by their own gravity. Start the clock and watch whether the planet's tide pulls it apart.

Io's real orbit has e = 0.004, kept up by the pulls of Europa and Ganymede. With e = 0 the orbit is a circle and the tide never changes.

Where the numbers come from

Where the moons of Jupiter and Saturn start: JPL's mean orbital elements for noon on 1 January 2000 (ssd.jpl.nasa.gov). The Roche limit for a fluid moon: S. Chandrasekhar, Ellipsoidal Figures of Equilibrium (1969). Io's tidal response k₂/Q = 0.015 ± 0.003: V. Lainey and others, Nature 459, 957 (2009). Io's heat flow, more than 2.5 W/m²: G. J. Veeder and others, Journal of Geophysical Research 99, 17095 (1994). Earth's heat flow, 47 TW: J. H. Davies and D. R. Davies, Solid Earth 1, 5 (2010). Tidal heating and resonances: C. D. Murray and S. F. Dermott, Solar System Dynamics (1999).
