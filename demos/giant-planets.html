Set up

The test moon is a pile of 200 loose balls held together only by their own gravity. Start the clock and watch whether the planet's tide pulls it apart.

Io's real orbit has e = 0.004, kept up by the pulls of Europa and Ganymede. With e = 0 the orbit is a circle and the tide never changes.

Where the numbers come from

Planets, moons and rings: NASA's planetary, satellite and ring fact sheets (D. R. Williams, NSSDCA, Goddard Space Flight Center). Where the moons of Jupiter and Saturn start: JPL's mean orbital elements for noon on 1 January 2000 (ssd.jpl.nasa.gov). The Roche limit for a fluid moon: S. Chandrasekhar, Ellipsoidal Figures of Equilibrium (1969). Io's tidal response k₂/Q = 0.015 ± 0.003: V. Lainey and others, Nature 459, 957 (2009). Io's heat flow, more than 2.5 W/m²: G. J. Veeder and others, Journal of Geophysical Research 99, 17095 (1994). Earth's heat flow, 47 TW: J. H. Davies and D. R. Davies, Solid Earth 1, 5 (2010). Tidal heating and resonances: C. D. Murray and S. F. Dermott, Solar System Dynamics (1999). Clouds: temperatures at 1 bar from the NSSDCA fact sheets; Jupiter's deep gases from the Galileo probe (von Zahn et al. 1998, J. Geophys. Res. 103, 22815; Wong et al. 2004, Icarus 171, 153) and Juno (Li et al. 2020, Nature Astronomy 4, 609); Saturn's from Atreya et al. 2003 (Planet. Space Sci. 51, 105); the Sun's from Asplund et al. 2009 (ARA&A 47, 481); vapor pressures from Wagner and Pruss 1993, Murphy and Koop 2005 and Fray and Schmitt 2009; ammonium hydrosulfide from Lewis 1969 (Icarus 10, 365); hydrogen's heat capacity from Leachman et al. 2009.
