The book's rules used here

Quartiles (OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e, 2.3 and 2.4): the median splits the ordered data into halves; Q1 is the median of the lower half and Q3 the median of the upper half. When n is odd the median itself is left out of both halves. This is the rule a TI-83 or TI-84 uses (1-Var Stats); spreadsheets and some software use other rules and can give slightly different quartiles.

k-th percentile (2.3): its position in the ordered list is i = k(n + 1)/100; when i is not a whole number, go that fraction of the way from the value at the whole part of i to the next value. Below position 1 it is the smallest value, past position n the largest. The 25th percentile found this way can differ a little from Q1.

Percentile of a value (2.3): (x + 0.5y)/n × 100, where x values are below it and y values equal it.

Outliers (2.3, 2.4): values below Q1 − 1.5 IQR or above Q3 + 1.5 IQR, with IQR = Q3 − Q1. The book's box plot runs its whiskers to the smallest and largest values; tick Modified box plot to stop them at the last values inside the fences and draw outliers as separate points.

Standard deviation (2.7): the sample s = √(Σ(x − x̄)²/(n − 1)). Skew (2.6): read from where the mean sits against the median and the long tail; the number shown is the sample skewness coefficient (n/((n − 1)(n − 2)))·Σ((x − x̄)/s)³, what spreadsheets call SKEW. Positive means a long tail to the right.

Histogram (2.2): bars start at Start and are Bin width wide; a value that falls exactly on a boundary goes in the bar to its right. The book picks a start one more decimal place than the data (60 − 0.05 = 59.95), so no value falls on a boundary.

Stemplot (2.1): the leaf is the last digit at the leaf unit, the stem everything before it; digits past the leaf unit are cut off, not rounded. Empty stems are kept, as the book does, so gaps show.

Where the data come from

Data sets marked "From the book" are copied from OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e (Holmes, Illowsky and Dean, 2023; CC BY 4.0), in the section named on each; access for free at openstax.org/details/books/introductory-statistics-2e. The lab readings are simulated here, from seeded random numbers, and say so: draw 1 is what every student sees on opening, and New draw makes another set.

Pendulum: one period timed by hand 25 times; the true period is 2.000 s and each timing is off by a normal reaction error with σ = 0.06 s, shown to 0.01 s. Laps: 20 laps of a 400 m track, 88.0 s plus 0.35 s more each lap as the runner tires, with normal scatter σ = 1.0 s, to 0.1 s. Decay counts: counts in 40 intervals of 10 s from a weak source, drawn from a Poisson distribution with mean 3.2. Lab temperature: 36 readings 10 min apart in a room warming from 20.0 °C by 1.5 °C over the six hours, with thermometer scatter σ = 0.15 °C, to 0.1 °C.
