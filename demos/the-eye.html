The model and its formulas

Blind hides whether the image is sharp, the blur, the eye's power and the prescriptions the buttons would give (the buttons still set the glasses). Predict first: can this eye see the object sharply? What power of glasses does it need?

Model: the cornea and lens act as one thin lens of power P, and the retina is a fixed 1.80 cm behind it (a simplified eye, chosen to match OpenStax Example 2.7, where an image at the near point forms 1.8 cm behind the lens). Accommodation sets P anywhere between the eye's relaxed power, which focuses its far point, and its strongest power, which focuses its near point: P = 1/d_o + 1/d_i (OpenStax Eq. 2.24). The eye picks the power that brings the image closest to the retina. Normal eye: near point 25 cm, far point at infinity. Nearsighted: far point 30.0 cm (Example 2.8). Farsighted: near point 1.00 m (Example 2.9). Older: far point at infinity, near point 2.00 m. The ranges of accommodation, 4.0 D for the young eyes and 0.5 D for the older one, are chosen typical values. Glasses are a thin lens 1.50 cm in front of the eye, as in the examples; the light is followed through both lenses exactly (thin lenses, paraxial). The pupil is 4.0 mm across; the blur is the size of the spot on the retina, and an image counts as sharp when the spot is under 0.02 mm (a chosen limit, a few cone spacings).
