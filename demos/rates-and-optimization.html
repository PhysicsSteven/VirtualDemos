Related Rates and Optimization

One quantity drives a whole scene. A ladder slides down a wall, a cone fills with water, a shadow grows as you walk from a lamp: see every rate change together and check the chain rule live. Then fold a box, fence a field, shape a can, run to a swimmer or aim a throw, and find the best choice where the slope of the graph is zero. The lifeguard's best path is Snell's law of refraction.

How it works

Related rates: one quantity changes at a steady rate you set (the foot of the ladder moves out at dx/dt, water flows in at dV/dt, the walker moves at dx/dt), and every other quantity in the scene follows from an equation that ties it to that one. The chain rule gives each rate: dy/dt = (dy/dx)(dx/dt), with dy/dx worked out exactly from the equation. Beside it, the rate from the motion is measured the way an experiment would: the change in the drawn quantity over ±0.1 ms of the run, divided by 0.2 ms. The two agree to every digit shown.

Optimization: the quantity is graphed against the choice, with its exact derivative below it. The best choice is where the derivative is zero; it comes from the closed form where there is one (the box, the fence, the can, the throw) and otherwise from bisection on the derivative to full precision (the lifeguard and the light ray).

Models: the ladder's top stays on the wall and its foot on the floor; the cone is a perfect cone, point down; the lamp is a point; the box walls have no thickness; the throw has no air drag, with g = 9.81 m/s²; light speeds are c/n with n = 1.000293 for air and 1.333 for water (OpenStax University Physics Vol 3, Table 1.1).
