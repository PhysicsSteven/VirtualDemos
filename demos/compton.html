Compton Scattering

A photon bounces off a free electron and leaves with a longer wavelength. The shift depends only on the angle, as it must if light carries momentum in lumps, like a particle.

More about this

The electron is free and at rest. Exact relativistic kinematics; the photon and the electron move in one plane, drawn here. In a real target the electrons are bound by a few eV to keV and moving, which broadens the shifted line (the Doppler width), and photons scattered by the atom as a whole keep their wavelength (the unshifted line).

Wave packets: the drawn wavelengths keep the ratio λ′/λ to scale, but λ itself is not to scale (a 70 pm wave is far too small to see). The electron moves at its true speed relative to the photons.

Photon colors: visible light is drawn in its true color. X-rays and gamma rays have no color, so they use a false color scale: the incoming photon is blue and the scattered photon shifts toward red in proportion to Δλ, reaching red at the largest possible shift, 2λC at 180°. Because Δλ depends only on the angle, a given angle gives the same color at every energy.

Angular distribution: the Klein–Nishina formula for unpolarized photons on free electrons, summed over final polarizations. Photons are sampled from it by rejection.
