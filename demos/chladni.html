Model

Thin plate (Kirchhoff–Love) theory: D∇⁴w = −ρh ∂²w/∂t² with flexural rigidity D = Eh³/(12(1 − ν²)). Small, linear motion of a flat, uniform, isotropic plate. Good while the bending wavelength is long compared with the thickness (k h well below 1; shown under Plate).

Every edge is free, and the drive is an ideal point force F₀ cos(2πft) at the brass post. The driver's own mass and stiffness and the small clamp around the bolt are left out; a real driver bolted to the plate shifts the resonances, so measured kit frequencies differ. The plate's rigid bouncing and rocking are included.

Circle: exact solution, w = [J_n(λr/a) + C I_n(λr/a)] cos nθ, with the free edge frequency equation solved to 12 digits. Square: no closed form; Rayleigh–Ritz with Legendre polynomials up to degree 30 in x and in y (961 terms, split into symmetry classes), converged to better than 1 part in a million for every mode shown.

Damping: every flexural mode has the same Q. The response is the sum over all computed modes. The sand's mass does not load the plate.

Sand is phenomenological, not a granular simulation: where the local acceleration amplitude |a| exceeds g a grain leaves the surface each cycle and, every 1/60 s, hops s × (a random step of mean length 1 in a random direction + a drift of 0.5 toward smaller |a|), with s = 0.6 mm × √min(|a|/g − 1, 4). The drift stands in for grains bouncing off the tilted, moving surface, which carries them toward the nodes. Where |a| < g a grain stays put. Grains that pass the edge fall off. Very light powder (lycopodium) gathers at the antinodes instead, carried by air currents over the plate (Faraday, 1831); air is not modeled.
