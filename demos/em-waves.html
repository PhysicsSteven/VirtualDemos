Electromagnetic Waves

Electric and magnetic fields that keep each other going and travel at c. E and B stay at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel, and peak together. Change the polarization, read off the energy a wave carries, and watch a dipole antenna launch one. In a plane, place wires and metal and watch the waves reflect, interfere and spread.

More about this

The slider is logarithmic: each step of 1 is ten times the frequency. Band edges are conventional, not sharp; X-rays and gamma rays overlap and are usually named by their source. Visible colors are approximate.

About the ground strip

The thin strip under the spectrum shows how much of each wavelength gets down through Earth's atmosphere to a telescope on a high, dry mountain: solid where the air is clear, faint where only some gets through, empty where none does. Edges: the ozone cutoff near 300 nm; the near and mid infrared windows J to Q and the 330 to 370 µm window with their transparency (IPAC Cool Cosmos, Infrared Windows); the radio window from 1 cm out to 30 m, below 10 MHz the ionosphere reflects radio waves back to space (Torge and Müller 2012), and from 1 cm down to 0.3 mm only patches between oxygen and water vapor lines at the best sites (Condon and Ransom 2016). The edges move with the weather, the site and the time of day, so they are drawn as a guide, not measurements. Telescopes for everything else fly above the air.

The model and its formulas

Ex = E₀ cos ψ cos(kz − ωt)
Ey = E₀ sin ψ cos(kz − ωt + δ)

An ideal polarizer passes only the component of E along its axis. For linear light at angle θ that gives Malus's law, I = I₀ cos²(θ − φ); circular light always passes half.

More about this

Handedness uses the optics convention: right circular light turns clockwise as you face the oncoming wave (δ = −90°). Engineers (IEEE) call the same wave left handed, so check which convention a book uses.

More about this

The dipole p = p₀ cos ωt points along the vertical axis. It sends nothing along its own axis and most at right angles to it; half power at 45°. Radiated power grows as ω⁴, so doubling f with the same p₀ gives 16 times the power.

More about this

Close in (r ≪ λ/2π) the field is nearly the dipole's own field, falling as 1/r³. Beyond about a wavelength the 1/r terms win: loops of E break off and move outward at c, with E, B and the direction of travel at right angles, a wave like the one on the Plane wave view.

More about this

Each wire runs straight through the page and carries an alternating current, so the picture is the same in every plane you could slice. E points along the wire, into or out of the page; B circles around in the page. Energy spreads over circles instead of spheres, so ⟨E²⟩ falls as 1/r instead of 1/r², and r⟨E²⟩ stays the same at every distance from one wire. Every length is in wavelengths: the picture is the same for radio waves meters long and microwaves centimeters long, as long as the metal conducts well.

More about this

Metal makes E along its surface zero, so a wave that hits it reflects, flipped over. A sheet λ/4 behind a wire sends its reflection back in step with the wave going forward: a reflector, as in a TV antenna. A parabola reflects waves from its focus into a parallel beam. Inside a closed metal box the field stays exactly zero (a Faraday cage); erase a gap in a wall and watch it leak in.

Model

A plane wave in vacuum, exact: E as in the formula above and B = ẑ × E/c, so B is always at right angles to E, in phase with it, and |B| = |E|/c. In SI units B in tesla is about 3 × 10⁸ times smaller than E in V/m, so it is drawn as cB. The drawing is scaled so E₀ fills it and always shows 2.5 wavelengths; time is slowed so one period takes 2.5 s. S = E × B/μ₀ = |E|²/(μ₀c) along ẑ; its time average is I.

Exact fields of an oscillating point dipole (length much less than λ), including the near field terms, in a plane through its axis. Lines are electric field lines, drawn as contours of the stream function sin²θ [sin(kr − ωt) + cos(kr − ωt)/kr]. Shading shows B, which points into or out of the page, multiplied by r so the far field shows evenly. A real half wave antenna has a slightly narrower pattern than sin²θ.
