The model and its formulas

Blind hides Young's modulus, the yield and breaking stresses, the stress and strain readouts, the material curve and Y from the fit, leaving the load, the diameter and the dial gauge. The Mystery metal is always blind about its own properties.

Model: an idealized stress against strain curve: straight to the yield stress, then hardening up to the tensile strength, breaking at the fracture strain; unloading follows a line parallel to the elastic one, leaving a permanent stretch. Values are typical for each metal; real samples vary. Necking, creep and the wire's own weight are ignored.

Shear and Bulk tests: linear elastic only, with the shear and bulk moduli of OpenStax University Physics Vol. 1, Table 12.1 (approximate values; the Mystery metal is the tension test's alloy and the Mystery liquid is made up). Shear: a 5.00 cm cube bonded between two steel plates, its base held and its top plate pushed sideways; the block shears uniformly (bending and the plates' own give are ignored). Bulk: a rigid steel cell with a 4.00 cm bore; a liquid fills it 10.00 cm deep under the piston, or a 2.000 cm metal cube with a strain gauge sits in oil inside it. The pressure rise is F/A of the piston, and ΔV/V₀ = −Δp/B exactly in the model; real liquids stiffen slightly as the pressure rises, which the model leaves out. Stresses stay at or below 20 MPa, where these metals stay elastic in shear (lead is left out of the shear test because it would flow); real glass and granite are brittle and could crack near the top of that range. Squeezing from every side, as in the bulk test, does not make a metal yield.

Blind in Shear and Bulk hides the moduli, the stress and strain readouts and the material line, leaving the load cell and the displacement or strain readings.
