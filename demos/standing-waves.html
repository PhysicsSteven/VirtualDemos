More about this

y₁ = A₁ sin(k₁x − ω₁t), y₂ = A₂ sin(k₂x + ω₂t), both at v = 2 m/s. With A₁ ≠ A₂ the sum is a standing wave of amplitude 2·min(A) plus a traveling wave of amplitude |A₁ − A₂|. The standing wave ratio is the largest over the smallest amplitude along x, (A₁ + A₂)/|A₁ − A₂|.

Why the pressure nodes, if pressure pushes?

More about this

Click or tap in the gap to drop a bead; drag before letting go to place it. Drag the reflector (it snaps to resonance) and the pressure grip at the bottom of the amplitude plot.

The pressure swings up and down 40 000 times a second, so its push averages to zero. What is left is a small steady force that grows as the wave amplitude squared. A bead much stiffer than air is pushed away from where the pressure swings most, the pressure antinodes. A bead much denser than air is drawn to where the air rushes back and forth fastest, since fast flow lowers the mean pressure around it (a Bernoulli effect). In a standing wave the air moves fastest at the pressure nodes, so both effects push the bead there. Gravity then pulls it a little below the node, until the restoring force balances its weight.
