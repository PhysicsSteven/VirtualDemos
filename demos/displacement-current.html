Displacement Current

A capacitor charges through a wire. No charge crosses the gap, yet there is a magnetic field around it. Ampère's law only works if the changing electric field between the plates counts as a current.

More about this

The capacitor charges as q = CV(1 − e−t/RC), so the current I = (V/R)e−t/RC dies away. Time runs about 10⁵ times slower than real. Ideal plates: the field between them is uniform and fringing is ignored; the wire is long and straight, 1 mm in radius. The field at the loop is the instantaneous (quasi static) Ampère–Maxwell value: retardation and radiation are left out, a correction of order (size/λ)² ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ for a 10 cm apparatus and a 35 µs time constant.
