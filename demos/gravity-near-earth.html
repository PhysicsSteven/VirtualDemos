Gravity Near Earth

g = GM/r² falls off with distance from Earth's centre. Near the surface it barely changes, but it is not zero even where the space station flies. Earth's spin also takes a little off, most at the equator.

GM = 3.986 × 10¹⁴ m³/s² · R = 6371 km

More about this

Model: a uniform sphere (GM and mean radius) turning once per sidereal day. The scale is at rest relative to the ground, so it reads the vector sum of gravity and the centrifugal term ω² r cos(latitude) outward from the axis; the plumb line tilts by the angle shown. The real Earth is also flattened, which the sphere misses: the comparison line uses the standard sea level formula (WGS 84). Heights on the slider run on a square root scale.
