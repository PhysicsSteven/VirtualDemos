Taylor Polynomials and Series

Type any function and add the terms of its Taylor polynomial one at a time: watch the polynomial hug the curve near the center a, see how far it stays within 1%, and find the error bound. The radius of convergence comes from the complex plane. Then build new series from known ones by substitution, differentiation and integration.

Play adds one term each second. Drag the diamond to move the center a, drag elsewhere to move the probe.

What you can type
  • Use one variable: x, t, θ (or theta), v or h.
  • + − * / ^ and parentheses; 2x, 3sin(x) and (x+1)(x−1) multiply.
  • sin cos tan sec csc cot, asin acos atan, sinh cosh tanh, exp or e^x, ln, log (base 10), sqrt, cbrt, abs or |x|, pi
  • ^ takes only the number right after it: e^2x is e²·x, so type e^(2x) for e to the power 2x.
How it works

The box is read by the site's own parser (nothing you type is run as code). Each derivative f⁽ᵏ⁾ is worked out exactly by the rules of calculus, so ck = f⁽ᵏ⁾(a)/k! is exact; the table prints it as a fraction when it is one. The polynomial is Tn(x) = c₀ + c₁(x − a) + … + cn(x − a)ⁿ.

Lagrange's remainder: f(x) − Tn(x) = f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(ξ)(x − a)ⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1)! for some ξ between a and x, so |f − Tn| ≤ M|x − a|ⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1)!, with M the largest |f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾| between a and x. M is found on a grid of 400 steps, so the band is the bound to within that sampling.

The radius of convergence R is the distance from a to the nearest point where f, continued into the complex plane, is not analytic: a pole (a zero of a denominator, or of cos inside tan) or a branch point (a zero inside ln or a fractional power, ±1 inside arcsin). They are found by Newton's method on the complex plane and checked with a small loop around each point (a removable point such as x = 0 in sin x/x is not one). |x| and sign(x) are not analytic at their corners, which count too. The root test from the terms, |cn|−1/n, tends to the same R as n grows.

In Build a series the coefficients are exact fractions, carried through each step: x → s·xᵐ moves cksᵏ to the power km and makes R into (R/|s|)1/m; d/dx and ∫₀ˣ keep R. The curve is the function the steps make: its formula where one is known, otherwise the integral computed numerically.

Angles are in radians. In the physics setups the variable is a ratio: θ in radians, v in units of c, h in Earth radii (R = 6,371 km, the mean radius), d/r for the dipole.
