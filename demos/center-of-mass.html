Center of Mass

The center of mass is the mass weighted average position. Find it for masses on a plate, then see that it keeps moving as if all the mass were there: a firework's pieces scatter but their center of mass follows the same parabola, and walking on a boat cannot move the center of mass of you and the boat.

Plate bounded by curves

The plate is where g(x) ≤ y ≤ f(x): type the top curve f and the bottom curve g, in meters.

More about this

Type the top and bottom curves in meters, like sqrt(0.09 − x^2), 0.4(1 − x/0.6) or x^2, and the limits, like 0, 1 or pi/4. The plate is a steel sheet 1 mm thick, σ = 7.85 kg/m². Drag the pin (keyboard: Tab to the picture, then the arrows), then press Release: the plate tips toward its balance point unless the pin is under it.

Blind hides the center of mass marker, its coordinates, the predicted boat shift, and the plate's moments, balance point and volume by Pappus.

More about this

Model: point masses on a massless plate; the firework is a point mass launched at 20 m/s and 60° that splits in two at the top of its flight, with no air drag; the boat floats on water with no drag, and the person walks from one end to the other at 1 m/s relative to the boat (boat 4.0 m long). The plate bounded by curves is a steel sheet 1 mm thick (σ = 7.85 kg/m²) cut into vertical strips, each taken at its middle; the integrals use adaptive Simpson's rule. On the pin it stays level when the balance point is within 1 mm of the pin (the size of the pin's tip); otherwise it tips about the line through the pin at right angles to the line to the balance point, θ'' = Mgd cos θ / I, with the products of inertia neglected, until it slides off at 30°.
