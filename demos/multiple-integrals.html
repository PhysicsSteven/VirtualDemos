Multiple Integrals

Add up a quantity spread over an area or a volume. Boxes under a surface turn into a double integral as the grid gets finer; slices taken in either order give the same answer; polar wedges, cylindrical and spherical cells fill round regions; the same sums give the mass, center of mass and moment of inertia of a body; and a change of variables stretches a grid by its Jacobian. Integrals are computed numerically; derivatives and Jacobians are exact.

What you can type
  • Use x, y, z, and r, θ (or theta), ρ (rho), φ (phi) where they mean something. + − * / ^; 2x and x y multiply.
  • sin cos tan exp ln sqrt abs, pi, e: e^(-r^2), sqrt(1-x^2), 2pi.
  • A bound may use only the coordinates outside it: the bounds on y may use x, the bounds on z may use x and y.
  • ln is the natural log and log is base 10.
  • ^ takes only the number right after it: e^2x is e²·x, so type e^(2x) for e to the power 2x.
How it works

Each box is read by the site's small parser (nothing you type is run as code). Partial derivatives and the Jacobian of a change of variables come from the exact rules of calculus.

An iterated integral is computed one coordinate at a time, inner first, by adaptive Gauss–Kronrod quadrature (7 and 15 points), splitting any piece until the two rules agree to about ten significant figures (eight for triple integrals). Where a bound has a square root that meets zero, as at the edge of a disk, the last few figures are less certain.

The Riemann sums use cells fitted to the region: the outer coordinate in equal steps, each inner coordinate in equal steps between its bounds there. Each cell's size is its steps times the Jacobian at its sample point: 1 for x, y, z; r for polar and cylindrical; ρ² sin φ for spherical. As the cells shrink the sum approaches the integral.

In Mass and inertia the density shape is scaled so the whole body has the mass M you type. The rod, plate and disk are thin: their density is per length or per area. The moment of inertia is I = ∫ d² dm, with d the distance from the axis.
