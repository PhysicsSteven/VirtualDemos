More about this

ψ = (ψA e−iEAt/ħ + ψB e−iEBt/ħ)/√2, evolved exactly. The two parts interfere, and the pattern repeats once per beat period. Colors drop the common phase e−iEAt/ħ, which nothing can measure.

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, double click to reset. With the cloud focused, arrow keys orbit and + and − zoom. Hover the radial plot to see that radius as a shell. While the cloud turns, fresh random positions keep replacing old ones, a twentieth of the cloud each second.

Cutaway removes the eighth of the cloud facing you, like a slice cut from a cake. The three cut faces show |ψ|² in those planes through the nucleus. Nodes are dark there: radial nodes as arcs, angular nodes as lines through the nucleus, outlined when Nodal lines is on.

Measure starts from an empty atom and finds the electron one position at a time, each drawn at random from |ψ|² (inverse CDF in r and θ, rejection sampling in φ and for a superposition). Each find flashes, and the finds build up the cloud.

About the model

More about this

Exact solutions of the Schrödinger equation for one electron and a point nucleus: ψ = Rnl(r) Ylm(θ, φ), with R from associated Laguerre polynomials and Y from associated Legendre functions with the Condon–Shortley phase. Spin, fine structure and the Lamb shift are left out.

Distances use the Bohr radius a₀ for an infinitely heavy nucleus. Energies use 13.598 eV, the measured value for hydrogen, which includes the small correction for the moving proton.

The Bohr model puts the electron on a circle of radius n²a₀. Here the electron has no orbit, only a probability cloud, and the energies still come out as −13.6 eV/n². Compare with the Bohr model in Spectra Explorer.
