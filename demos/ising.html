More about this

Errors are one standard error. ⟨|m|⟩ and ⟨e⟩: blocking analysis of the correlated series (Flyvbjerg and Petersen), taking the largest error over levels with at least 32 blocks, which errs on the large side. c = N(⟨e²⟩ − ⟨e⟩²)/T² and χ = N(⟨m²⟩ − ⟨|m|⟩²)/T: jackknife over 20 blocks. τint is the integrated autocorrelation time. With J = −1 the order parameter is ms.

Model

Exact model, sampled: an L × L square lattice with periodic edges, H = −J Σ sisj − h Σ si. Monte Carlo draws configurations with probability ∝ exp(−E/T); averages over a finite run carry the statistical errors shown. No error estimate can see states a run never visits: below Tc with h ≠ 0 the reversed state is metastable and may not be reached for a very long time. Random numbers: xoshiro128**, fixed by the seed.

Updates: Metropolis picks sites at random and flips a spin with probability min(1, e−ΔE/T), ΔE = 2s(JS + h) with S the sum of its four neighbors. Heat bath sets a spin up with probability 1/(1 + e−2(JS + h)/T), visiting the even sites, then the odd ones; sites of one color share no bond. Every step obeys detailed balance. Wolff grows a cluster through satisfied bonds, each added with probability 1 − e−2|J|/T, and flips it; it needs h = 0.

Time: one sweep is N single spin steps; for Wolff, a fixed number of clusters chosen so that about N spins flip (held fixed while a measurement averages, since stopping at exactly N flips would bias the averages). Sweeps are not seconds. Metropolis and heat bath mimic a lattice trading energy with a heat bath, but only the averages, not the speed, are physical; Wolff dynamics has no physical meaning at all.

Finite size: a finite lattice has no sharp transition. The peaks of c and χ are rounded and shifted, and ⟨|m|⟩ stays above zero above Tc (about N−1/2). χ uses ⟨|m|⟩, the usual finite lattice form; above Tc it is (1 − 2/π) times N⟨m²⟩/T.

Exact curves, h = 0 only: Onsager (1944) e and c, and the spontaneous magnetization m = (1 − sinh−4(2/T))1/8 (Yang 1952), for the infinite lattice; Tc = 2/ln(1 + √2). Dashed: Kaufman's (1949) exact partition function of the L × L lattice. No exact χ is known, so none is drawn. With J = −1 the same curves hold with ms in place of m.

Hysteresis: the loop is not an equilibrium property. Its width depends on the sweep rate, L and T; in equilibrium below Tc, m would jump at h = 0.
