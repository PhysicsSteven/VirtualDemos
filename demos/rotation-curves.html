Galaxy Rotation Curves

Stars and gas far from a galaxy's center orbit about as fast as those near it. The visible matter alone predicts they should slow down, as the planets do: the extra pull comes from a halo of dark matter.

Disk (Freeman 1970): v² = 2GM y²[I₀(y)K₀(y) − I₁(y)K₁(y)]/Rd, y = R/2Rd
Bulge: v² = GMr/(r + a)²
Pseudo isothermal: v² = 4πGρ₀rc²[1 − (rc/r) arctan(r/rc)]
NFW: v² = 4πGρsrs³[ln(1 + x) − x/(1 + x)]/r, x = r/rs

Circular orbits in the midplane. The disks are infinitely thin, the bulge and halo are spheres, and the speeds add in quadrature because their potentials add. The modified Bessel functions are computed from their integral forms to about 15 digits. The gas disk is modeled as exponential, while real neutral hydrogen is flatter and often thin in the center. Visible masses include helium for the gas.

The galaxy is an illustration: its stars are drawn from the model's mass distribution and move at the model's speeds; bulge stars are drawn on circles too, though real ones move on random orbits. In the edge on view each sightline through the disk collects gas at many radii, so the line spreads toward zero; its outer edge comes from the tangent point and gives v(r). Shifts use Δλ/λ = v/c, a line width of 10 km/s is added, and the galaxy's own recession is removed.
