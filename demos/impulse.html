Impulse and Momentum

A cart rolls into a bumper on a force sensor and bounces back. The area under the force against time graph is the impulse, and it equals the cart's change of momentum. A soft bumper gives the same impulse with a smaller force over a longer time.

More about this

The motion detector gives the velocity before (v₁) and after (v₂) the bounce, to 0.001 m/s, positive toward the sensor. The area is the trapezoid sum of the sampled sensor readings. Record stores one row per run (bumper, mass, velocities, rate, area, peak force, contact time); Export to worksheet sends the rows to the student worksheet, which can also save them as CSV; export a run's F(t) to integrate it yourself.

The model and its formulas

Blind hides the area, the momentum change and the peak force, leaving the graph, the velocities and the settings.

Model: a frictionless cart; the bumper pushes with F = kx while it is compressed, then unloads along a steeper line so the cart leaves with e times its speed (e is the coefficient of restitution). Each phase is simple harmonic, so F(t) is exact. Stiff spring k = 4000 N/m, e = 0.90; soft spring k = 400 N/m, e = 0.90; clay k = 1500 N/m, e = 0.10. The sensor reads the force to 0.01 N at the chosen sample rate.
