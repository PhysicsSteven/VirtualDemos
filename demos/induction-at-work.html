Induction at Work

Three everyday devices that run on Faraday's law. Shake a magnet through a coil to charge a flashlight, draw with a magnetic pen on a grid of wires, and swipe a card's magnetic stripe past a reading head. In each one a changing flux makes an EMF.

More about this

Blind hides the EMF plots, the light's glow and the readouts that answer the question for each device: the capacitor voltage, the tablet's estimate of where the pen is, and the decoded number. Predict first: what happens to the EMF when you shake twice as fast, when the pen stops, when the card goes through slowly?

Flashlight model: a NdFeB magnet (remanence 1.3 T, a typical value; 10 mm across and 20 mm long) treated as a point dipole, m = B_r V/μ₀ = 1.63 A·m², moving through a coil of 1000 turns of radius 12 mm spread over 10 mm. The dipole approximation is good when the magnet is outside the coil and gives the right size, not the exact shape, when it is inside. The coil's resistance comes from its wire: 75 m of 0.20 mm copper, 40.3 Ω. The magnet moves as x = A sin 2πft, driven by your hand whatever the coil does. A bridge rectifier (two diodes conduct, 0.30 V each) charges the capacitor whenever |ε| exceeds V_C + 0.60 V; the light is a white LED taken as 2.6 V in series with 20 Ω. The magnet size, turns, diode drop and LED values are chosen; the energy readouts add up to within 0.01 %, the error of the 0.2 ms time step.

Tablet model: the pen tip holds a small NdFeB magnet (1.3 T, 2 mm across, 5 mm long, m = 0.0163 A·m²) pointing down, 5 mm above wires 5 mm apart. Each wire's circuit closes far away, so the flux it encloses is that through a half plane, Φ = −μ₀m u/(2π(u² + h²)) for a pen a distance u from the wire, and the EMF is −dΦ/dt. The tablet finds the pen by fitting this shape to the five strongest wires; it ignores signals below 0.5 µV (a chosen detection threshold, with no electrical noise added). Real passive tablets work differently in one way: the pen's field is made to alternate, so a pen held still is still seen.

Card model: track 2 of a magnetic stripe, 75 bits per inch (ISO/IEC 7811), recorded in F2F: the magnetization flips at every bit boundary and once more in the middle of a 1. Digits are 4 bits, least significant first, with odd parity, between the start sentinel ; and end sentinel ? (the check character after it is left out). Each flip passing the head gives a pulse of EMF ∝ v g²/((x − x_k)² + g²) with g = 0.04 mm (a chosen head resolution); EMF is shown relative to a single pulse at 1 m/s. The decoder keeps time from the pulses themselves, which is why an uneven swipe still reads.
