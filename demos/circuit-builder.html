The equivalent resistance is what the battery sees: the rest of the circuit with every other battery replaced by its internal resistance. Power drawn from the EMFs always equals the power turned into heat and light.

Build

Pick a tool above the drawing, then drag from one dot to another, or tap two dots. A part dragged over more than one step gets wires to fill the gap; a wire that turns a corner goes across first, then down. Batteries and meters have their + end where you start. With Select, drag a part or a dot to move it and click a switch to flip it.

Keys on the drawing: 1 to 8 pick a tool, arrows move the cursor (or the selected part), Enter marks the start dot and then places, Tab picks the next part, + and − change its value, R rotates, F flips, Delete removes, Ctrl+Z undoes.

Model: ideal wires, switches and meters (an ammeter has zero resistance, a voltmeter draws no current). A battery is an ideal EMF ε with an optional internal resistance r in series. Bulbs are modeled as fixed resistors, so P = I2R; a real filament's resistance rises as it heats. Where wires alone form a loop, the current is shared as if every wire had the same tiny resistance. Node voltages are measured from the − terminal of the first battery in each separate circuit.
