What you can type
  • Use x or t as the variable. For the point, type a number, pi/2, inf or -inf.
  • + − * / ^ and parentheses; 2x, 3sin(x) and (x+1)(x−1) multiply.
  • sin cos tan sec csc cot, asin acos atan, sinh cosh tanh, exp or e^x, ln, log (base 10), sqrt, abs or |x|, sign, pi
How it works

The box is read by a small parser (demos/lib/vdmath.js); nothing you type is run as code. The parser simplifies as it reads, so x/x becomes 1 and x²/x becomes x: their holes are gone before f is ever evaluated.

Each one sided limit is found by closing in: f at x = a ± h for h = ½·max(1, |a|) halved again and again, down to where x can no longer be told apart from a in double precision (toward infinity, x = ±2ᵏ out to 2⁶²). The values are said to settle when three Aitken Δ² extrapolations in a row agree to 10⁻⁷ of 1 + |L| and the values themselves are within 10⁻³ of L; the estimate is taken before rounding takes over. Values that grow in size step after step without settling blow up to ±∞; values that keep turning back oscillate. A numerical search can be fooled by a function built to fool it; the readout says how each limit was found.

Where f is a quotient N/D with N(a) = D(a) = 0, L'Hôpital's rule is applied exactly: N and D are differentiated by the rules of calculus until one of them is not 0 at a. Where f(a) is defined and equals the limit found, f is continuous there and the limit is f(a).

The largest δ: each side is scanned at 2,000 evenly spaced points and at halvings toward a, out to the view's half width and in to 10⁻⁵ × max(1, |a|) from a (closer in, rounding takes the digits of x − a and of differences like 1 − cos x), and the first point outside the ε band is refined by bisection to rounding. A wiggle narrower than the scan's spacing could be missed. Toward infinity, N is found the same way, scanning inward from 10¹⁵ at 50 points a decade.

Zoomed in far enough, a difference of nearly equal numbers loses its digits to rounding (1 − cos x near 0, for one), and the graph turns to noise. That is the computer's arithmetic, not the function; the values graph shows where it starts.
