Resonators

What sets the pitch of an instrument? The size of its resonator and the speed of the wave inside it. Find the speed of sound with a resonance tube, pluck a string to see which harmonics it gets, and blow across a bottle whose note follows its volume, not its height.

Closed at one end: f = n c / 4(L + ΔL), n odd

More about this

An open end is a pressure node, placed ΔL ≈ 0.61a beyond the pipe (a = inner radius); a closed end is a displacement node. All modes plays modes 1 to 8 with amplitude 1/n: every harmonic for the open pipe, only odd ones for the closed pipe.

More about this

The air in the neck is a mass and the air in the body is a spring: blowing across the mouth sets the neck air bouncing on it. Less air in the body makes a stiffer spring and a higher note. The pitch depends on the volume, not on how tall the air space is, so the pipe formula gets a bottle wrong.
