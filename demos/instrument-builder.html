How M is found

θseen is the angle at which the eye sees the top of the object: the direction of the ray from the top of the object that passes through the center of the eye's lens. θwithout is the angle the object fills with no instrument: h/(25 cm) for a near object held at the near point, or its own angle for a distant one. M < 0 means the view is upside down. The textbook formulas take the final image at infinity (the eye relaxed); they and the traced M agree when it is.

What these mean

The aperture stop is the opening that limits the cone of light from the middle of the object. The entrance pupil is that stop as seen from the object, and the exit pupil is the stop's image after the last lens: every ray that gets through passes through it, so that is where the eye's pupil belongs. The fields are full angles (or, for a near object, the width of the object you can see) out to where half the light still reaches the eye. Brightness compares the light per area of the picture on the retina with the unaided eye looking at the same object: it falls when the exit pupil is smaller than the eye's pupil. No lens or mirror can make an extended object look brighter than it does to the bare eye. A star is different: it is a point, so all the light the instrument collects from it lands on one spot. Light from a star compares the light from a distant point on the axis that reaches the retina or sensor with what the bare eye or camera collects: (D/d)², where D is the entrance pupil of the whole instrument with the viewer's pupil d counted as a stop.

The model

Lenses are ideal thin lenses: a ray at height x leaves with its slope changed by −x/f, so every point of a plane is imaged exactly to a point (1/do + 1/di = 1/f), with no aberrations; they are drawn with some thickness, but the light bends at the middle plane. Angles are handled as slopes (tan θ). Rays are traced in three dimensions and stopped at the rim of each lens and stop. The eye is the reduced eye of The Eye demo: a thin lens 1.80 cm in front of the retina that accommodates from infinity in to 25 cm, so it sees sharply only when the final image lies 25 cm or more in front of it. The camera has a 50 mm lens, a 36 × 24 mm sensor and focuses from 45 cm to infinity. The pictures of what the eye and camera see are traced backward ray by ray from the retina or sensor through the pupil and every lens to the object, so blur, the edge of the field and dimming are all from the rays; the view through the instrument is brightened to fill the picture (the eye adapts), and the brightness readout says by how much. Light is one color (no chromatic aberration) and diffraction is left out; its limit is quoted.
