    Key

    How the picture is drawn

    Convection is drawn as tracer streaks circling counter rotating roll cells. Their extent comes from MESA's mixing_type and their relative speed from log_conv_vel (compressed so slow and fast regions both show); the roll pattern itself is illustrative, not computed. Overshoot shows as fainter, slower streaks; semiconvection as drifting layers; thermohaline mixing as thin radial fingers.

    In the 3D cutaway the outside is drawn from the model's Teff: its color is the blackbody color at Teff (Planck spectrum through the CIE 1931 color matching functions), with a fixed saturation boost that keeps the hue, and drawn a little dimmer below about 6000 K so cool stars read orange red and hot ones blue white. Its brightness falls toward the edge by the linear limb darkening law I(μ) = 1 − u(1 − μ) with u = 0.65 (0.55 in red and 0.8 in blue, so the edge is redder, as in real stars), where μ is the cosine of the angle between the line of sight and the surface normal. Granulation shows only when MESA has a convective envelope reaching the surface, and never on a white dwarf. The cells are illustrative and far larger than real ones: their size follows d/R = 0.012 x0.75, kept between 0.008 and 0.5 (small cells are also drawn with lower contrast), with x = (Teff/5772 K)(R/Rsun)(Msun/M), which grows with the surface pressure scale height over the radius. In the cutaway the two upright cut faces show the true colors; the floor of the cut is drawn 12% darker so the cut reads in 3D. The halo around the star and the background sky are decorative.

    True size (3D): the view radius follows the star's radius with smooth easing in log scale, so the swelling to a giant and the shrinking to a white dwarf play out on screen; zoom out past the star with the wheel, a pinch or the Zoom slider. The faint rings are the orbits of Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars at true scale (1 AU = 215.03 Rsun). Lost gas: the mass of each shell is the drop in the model's total mass between saved moments (MESA star_mass), so a run without mass loss shows none. The shells' shape (a uniform spherical outflow), their speed (an assumed 15 km/s, typical of winds from giants), their brightness, their color (teal once the star is hotter than about 25,000 K and can ionize the gas, dull orange before) and their fading beyond about 2 pc are illustrative.

    Glow marks zones with εnuc above 10 erg g⁻¹ s⁻¹ and above 10⁻⁴ of the moment's peak. Channel shares use MESA's pp, cno and tri_alpha columns; any remainder is other reactions.
