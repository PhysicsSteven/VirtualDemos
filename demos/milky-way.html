The Milky Way and Spiral Arms

We live inside a flat disk of stars, so mapping it is like mapping a forest from inside, in fog. Use the real globular clusters to find the center as Shapley did, sort the stars into two populations, time the Sun's lap around the Galaxy, and see why the spiral arms are a traffic jam rather than a fixed string of stars.

The model and its sources

Globular clusters: Harris 1996 (AJ 112, 1487), 2010 edition: galactic longitude l, latitude b, distance from the Sun, [Fe/H] and the reddening E(B−V). Positions use R₀ = 8.178 kpc (GRAVITY Collaboration 2019). Each cluster's RR Lyrae stars have the catalog's horizontal branch luminosity, M_V = 0.16[Fe/H] + 0.84 (Harris's calibration), about 50 times the Sun's visual luminosity (M_V,☉ = 4.81). Dust dims a cluster by A_V = 3.1 E(B−V) magnitudes, a factor 10^(−0.4 A_V); left uncorrected, d = √(L/b) comes out 10^(0.2 A_V) times too far. "Found only in infrared" means E(B−V) above 1.5 (more than 4.6 magnitudes of dimming). The swarm's center is the median of each coordinate, so a few very distant clusters cannot drag it.

The galaxy drawn behind the clusters is a sketch (simulated stars): a bar 5 kpc long tilted 27° to the Sun's line of sight, four arms of pitch 12°, a disk with a 2.6 kpc scale length and 0.3 kpc scale height (Bland-Hawthorn and Gerhard 2016, ARA&A 54, 529). The Sun's metal share is Z = 0.0134 by mass (Asplund et al. 2009); the book rounds it to about 2%.

Orbits: circular, t = 2πr/v, with 1 ly = 9.4607 × 10¹² km, 1 yr = 3.1558 × 10⁷ s (Julian) and 63,241 AU in a light year. The measured rotation curve is the fit v = 229 − 1.7(R − 8.122) km/s, R in kpc, from 5 to 25 kpc (Eilers et al. 2019, ApJ 871, 120). M = a³/P² and M = v²r/G agree. The ghost moves at v = 229 km/s × √(R₀/r), as if all the mass sat inside the Sun's orbit.

Spiral arms: stars on circles with v = 229 km/s × r/√(r² + 1 kpc²). Both start as two arms of pitch 18° (opener than the Milky Way's 12°, so the arms are easy to see). As the same stars, the arms turn with the stars and wind up: tan i = 1/(1/tan i₀ + r t |dΩ/dr|), about 1/(1/tan i₀ + Ωt) with a flat curve. As a density wave, the pattern turns rigidly at Ω_p and each star slows by a factor 1 + J·g while it crosses an arm (g = 1 at the arm's middle), so the stars crowd there 1 + J times as densely as between the arms. This is a kinematic sketch of a traffic jam, not a solution of the self gravitating density wave (Lin and Shu 1964). Gas clouds crossing the middle of an arm make one blue star each.
