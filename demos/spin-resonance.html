Model

The bench is modeled on the common spin resonance teaching kit used in many modern physics labs: a console with a marginal oscillator and frequency meter, a cell mount with scan coils on steel cores, an electron probe, a proton probe and a permanent Zeeman magnet.

Exact: hν = gμBB with g = 2.0036 held fixed for DPPH; ν = (γ/2π)B with 42.577478 MHz/T for ¹H (free proton, CODATA 2022) and 40.0776 MHz/T for ¹⁹F (γ = 25.18148×10⁷ rad s⁻¹ T⁻¹, IUPAC 2001). h, μB and μN are CODATA values. Chemical shielding, tens of ppm, is far below this instrument's resolution and is neglected.

Idealized: the scan current ramps exactly linearly (FAST: 40 ms ramp, 1 ms flyback, repeating at 20 Hz; SLOW: one 25 s ramp); the field is uniform over the sample; the scan field adds to the magnet field; the earth's field and stray fields are left out. The flyback is too quick for the detector, so no peak is drawn there. The magnet follows the maker's temperature law BZ = B20[1 − (t − 20)·7.5×10⁻⁴]. The probe coil is taken at right angles to the field.

Modeled, not computed from first principles: the line shapes are Lorentzians in field with illustrative widths (full width at half maximum: DPPH 0.25 mT, glycerine and water 0.05 mT, polystyrene 0.25 mT for the dipolar broadening of a solid, PTFE 0.20 mT, the plastic coil former 0.30 mT). Heights are chosen to resemble a real trace and scale with the number of spins in the coil; water is weaker than glycerine because its slow relaxation saturates.

The marginal oscillator is a behavioral model: the gain grows as (1 + ε)/(ε + 1 − s/sth) with ε = 0.08, the LED dims above 0.85 sth, oscillation stops at the threshold sth, and the noise rises with the gain and sharply just below the threshold. The frequency meter jitters by one count. All noise is simulated.
