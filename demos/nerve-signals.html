Nerve Signals and the ECG

A nerve fiber is a leaky cable: a small kick of charge fades, but a kick past threshold fires an action potential that runs the whole length. Time it between two electrodes and see myelin make it jump from node to node. Then watch the heart, a turning dipole, draw ECG leads I, II and III.

The model and its formulas

Blind hides the threshold of this axon and the speed the model predicts, the resting voltage the Goldman equation gives, and the heart's axis and dipole angle. Predict first, then measure.

E = (kT/ze) ln(c_out/c_in) · V = (kT/e) ln[(P_K K_out + P_Na Na_out + P_Cl Cl_in)/(P_K K_in + P_Na Na_in + P_Cl Cl_out)]

Resting potential: the Nernst potential of each ion and the Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz voltage, with k_B and e exact (2019 SI). Concentrations from Purves et al., Neuroscience, 6th ed. (2018), Table 2.1; where it gives a range (human Na⁺ inside 5 to 15 mM, Cl⁻ inside 4 to 30 mM; squid Cl⁻ inside 40 to 150 mM) the demo uses 12, 10 and 40 mM. Permeability ratios P_K : P_Na : P_Cl = 1 : 0.04 : 0.45 at rest and 1 : 20 : 0.45 at the peak of the impulse (Hodgkin and Katz 1949, squid axon). The field across the membrane uses its 8 nm thickness (OpenStax College Physics 2e, 20.7).

C_m ∂V/∂t = (d/4R_i) ∂²V/∂x² − g_Na m³h (V − E_Na) − g_K n⁴ (V − E_K) − g_L (V − E_L)

Axon: the Hodgkin-Huxley membrane (Hodgkin and Huxley 1952, J. Physiol. 117, 500) on a cable, at 18.5 °C, the temperature of their propagated action potential, with the rates scaled by 3^((T − 6.3 °C)/10): g_Na = 120, g_K = 36, g_L = 0.3 mS/cm², E_Na = 50, E_K = −77, E_L = −54.4 mV, C_m = 1 µF/cm², R_i = 35.4 Ω cm. The cable is cut into 600 pieces (bare) or into nodes 1 µm long and ten pieces per internode (myelinated) and stepped implicitly, 4 µs (bare) or 1 µs (myelinated) at a time; the speeds are within 1 % of a much finer grid. Hodgkin and Huxley computed 18.8 m/s for their 476 µm squid axon, and measured 21.2 m/s; this grid gives 18.6 m/s.

Myelin: the axon is 0.7 of the fiber's outer diameter (g-ratio), internodes are 100 fiber diameters long, nodes are 1 µm long, and the myelin's lamellae are 16 nm apart. Each lamella puts two more membranes in series, so an internode has 1/(2N) of the bare membrane's capacitance and conductance per area, with N lamellae. The nodes carry five times the squid membrane's channel density, a chosen value: with the squid density alone the impulse dies in the first internode, and real nodes of Ranvier pack sodium channels several times more densely. Thinned myelin keeps a tenth of the lamellae on internodes 12 to 14; lost myelin leaves them bare and without channels. Nodes are drawn wider than to scale and the axon thicker; lengths along the axon are to scale.

Stimulus: the electrode charges the membrane under it by ΔV at once. It covers half a bare length constant λ = √(R_m d/4R_i) (R_m from the resting conductance, 0.677 mS/cm²), or the first node and two internodes of a myelinated axon. The electrodes time the impulse where the voltage rises through 0 mV.

Speeds of real fibers (shaded on the graph): Erlanger and Gasser's classes as tabulated in Purves et al. (2018): C 0.2 to 1.5 µm and 0.5 to 2 m/s; Aδ 1 to 5 µm and 5 to 35 m/s; Aβ 6 to 12 µm and 35 to 75 m/s; Aα 13 to 20 µm and 80 to 120 m/s, fiber diameters with the myelin, drawn at 0.7 of that for the axon. Hursh (1939) found about 6 m/s per µm of fiber diameter in myelinated fibers.

I = p_x · II = p_x/2 + (√3/2) p_y · III = −p_x/2 + (√3/2) p_y · II = I + III

Heart: the heart's electrical activity as one dipole in the frontal plane (Einthoven's model), angles measured from the patient's left (lead I) toward the feet. P, Q, R, S and T are Gaussian pulses in time, each pointing its own way, so the dipole turns and traces a loop: P at +55°, the QRS turned so its area points along the mean axis you set, and T 15° from the R wave. Times are for 75 beats per minute; the T wave moves with Bazett's rule, QT ∝ √RR. The sizes and directions are a schematic choice that gives textbook shapes, not a recording. Each limb lead is the dipole's component along one side of Einthoven's triangle.

Simulated data: every trace is computed by the model.
