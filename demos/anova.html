One Way ANOVA

Are several group means equal? Drag each group's mean and its spread and watch the two variances the F ratio compares: how far the group means sit from the grand mean (between groups) against how far the points sit from their own group's mean (within groups). Then run many studies where every mean is equal and see the F distribution build up, false alarms and all. A second mode tests whether two variances are equal.

Play runs studies with every group mean equal (H₀ true) and fills the F histogram.

How it works

Each group's points are a seeded draw of n standard normal numbers, rescaled so the group's sample mean and sample standard deviation are exactly the values you set; dragging a mean slides the whole group, dragging a spread stretches it about its mean. New draw picks another set of shapes.

One way ANOVA (book 13.1 to 13.3): SS_between = Σ nⱼ(x̄ⱼ − x̄)², df = k − 1; SS_within = Σ (nⱼ − 1)sⱼ², df = N − k; each mean square MS = SS/df, and F = MS_between/MS_within, with the p value the area of F(k − 1, N − k) to the right of F. The squares in the picture have these two mean squares as their areas. With equal gaps d between k = 3 equal sized groups of equal spread s, F = nd²/s² exactly.

Test of two variances (13.4): F = s₁²/s₂², with df n₁ − 1 and n₂ − 1. It assumes both populations are normal, and is sensitive to that.

Play draws studies from normal populations that all share one mean and one σ (so H₀ is true) with the same k and n, and computes F for each. The fraction above the critical value is the false alarm (Type I error) rate; it should be close to α.
