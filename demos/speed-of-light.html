Model

The layout follows the common rotating mirror teaching kit used in many modern physics labs: laser, lens L1, beam splitter with a measuring microscope, lens L2, a motor driven mirror MR, and a concave fixed mirror MF several meters away.

Exact within the model: light takes Δt = 2D/c out and back. The mirror turns Δθ = ωΔt, with ω = 2π × (rev/s). A mirror turned by Δθ turns the reflected beam by 2Δθ, so the image of S on MF moves 2DΔθ, and L2 forms an image of that at the microscope with magnification A/(D + B). The shift is Δs′ = 4AD²ω / c(D + B), so c = 4AD²ω / (D + B)Δs′. With CW and CCW readings, Δs′ = |sCW − sCCW| and ω = 2π(fCW + fCCW); or measure one direction against the reading at rest. The fit uses the slope k of reading against signed speed: c = 8πAD² / (D + B)k.

A is set by the focus condition 1/f₂ = 1/A + 1/(D + B): for D = 12 m and B = 0.5 m, A = 0.2572 m. c = 299 792 458 m/s exactly (SI). Air (n ≈ 1.000 29) would slow the light to c/n, a 0.03 % change, far below this method's resolution of about 1 %, so the light travels at c here.

Idealized: thin lenses without aberration; a perfect point source S at the focus of L1; MF exactly at the image of S; the mirror reaches the set speed at once and holds it; MR is aligned so the beam meets MF. Light comes back only while the turning mirror sweeps the beam across MF, so the real image is a train of short flashes that looks steady; it is drawn as a steady spot here. The image has a Gaussian profile, about 50 µm across, chosen to look like a real spot.

Simulated, from the seed: the rest position of the image, its jitter (standard deviation 2 µm, from air currents and vibration), and the speed meter jitter (0.15 %). The 3D mirror is drawn turning slowly; at 1000 rev/s it would be a blur. In slow motion, time is slowed so the 80 ns flight lasts 3 s and the mirror's turn is drawn larger by the stated factor; the path D is not to scale in any view.
