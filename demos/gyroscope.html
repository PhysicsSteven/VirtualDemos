Gyroscope Precession

More about this

A gate at the front of the post times each full turn of the axle round the post, to 0.01 s. Capture stores the spin, d and the last full turn's period P. Plot Ω = 2π/P against 1/ω_s for one d: the slope is Mgd/I_s.

In 3D, Bench looks across the bench, Ride along turns the camera round the post with the axle so the wobble stands out, and From above looks down on the precession. The gold line traces the end of the axle over the last 4 s and the red one the tip of the L arrow, so the wobble (nutation) shows as loops or cusps along the circle; the strip marks each pass of the gate with a small triangle, and the gate's lamp lights as the axle passes. More about this

Model: a rigid symmetric wheel on a massless axle, a frictionless pivot, the spin about the axle held constant (no friction in the wheel's bearing). The motion is computed from the exact equations for a heavy symmetric top (RK4, 0.2 ms steps), so the wobble (nutation) and the dip at release are real, not added. Solid disk I_s = ½MR²; rim heavy wheel 0.905MR²; the wheel's moment about its diameter is I_s/2.

Changing the spin or d while it runs: the change acts along the axle (a motor in the hub, or sliding the wheel along the axle), so it exerts no torque across the axle. The axle's direction and its angular momentum across the axle carry over (so with a new d its turning rates scale by I_1 old/I_1 new) and the spin takes the new value at once. The axle then wobbles about a new steady tilt: the dashed gold line on the strip, where the effective potential for the tilt is least. The gate starts timing afresh after each change. Tilt at release and the steady start apply at release only.
