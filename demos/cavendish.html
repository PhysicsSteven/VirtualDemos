The Cavendish Experiment

Two small lead spheres on a beam hang from a thin fibre. Swing two large lead spheres up beside them and their gravity twists the fibre a few thousandths of a radian; a laser reflected from a mirror on the beam turns that into centimetres on a scale 5 m away. From the twist and the period of the swing, find G.

The model and its formulas

G = π² r² l ΔS / (M T² L)

Model: small spheres m = 20.0 g and large spheres M = 1.500 kg of lead (11 340 kg/m³), so their radii are 7.5 mm and 31.6 mm. The beam is 2l = 100.0 mm between the small spheres' centres and is taken as massless, with a massless mirror at its centre. In A and B each large sphere's centre is r = 46.5 mm from the nearer small sphere's centre, square to the beam, with the beam untwisted. The torque is the exact sum over all four pairs of spheres (uniform spheres attract as points), including the pull of each large sphere on the far small sphere, and changes as the beam turns. The fibre's torsion constant gives an undamped period of 360.0 s; air damping makes the swing die away by a factor of e every 720 s. G = 6.674 30 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (CODATA 2018). The scale is L = 5.000 m from the mirror, and the spot sits at 50.0 cm + L tan 2θ, read to 1 mm. The clock reads to 1 s. No noise, drift or seismic shaking is added.

Blind hides the twist angle, the forces, the predicted resting positions and the value of G, leaving the clock and the spot.
