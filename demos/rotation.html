Rotation and Torque

Push a door, race rolling objects down a ramp, throw a ball that skids and then rolls, drop a yo-yo beside a falling one, spin a skater, balance a seesaw. Torque makes things turn, moment of inertia resists it, and angular momentum stays the same when nothing twists from outside.

More about this

Every object that rolls without slipping loses the same potential energy mgh, but part of it goes into spinning. The larger I/mR², the bigger that share, and the slower it goes down. The frictionless block puts it all into sliding, so it wins.

Assumes enough static friction to roll without slipping, no rolling resistance and no air drag; static friction does no work on a rolling object. Objects are drawn larger than scale.

More about this

The floor pushes back only at the contact point. While that point slides (v ≠ ωR), kinetic friction μk m g acts there against the sliding: it changes v and, through its torque μk m g R, changes ω, until v = ωR. From then on the contact point is at rest, friction is zero and the object rolls at constant speed. Each point of a rolling object moves as if it turned about the contact point, so the top moves at 2v.

Friction acts along the floor and the weight and normal force cancel, so the angular momentum about any point on the floor, mR(v + kωR) with k = I/mR², stays the same the whole time. That fixes the rolling speed. Model: constant μk while sliding, no rolling resistance, no air drag. Drawn to scale, side view; the camera follows the object.

More about this

Both start from rest at the same height. The dropped one has only its weight on it and falls at g. The unwinding one also has the string pulling up: that slows its fall, and the string's torque Tr about the center spins it up. With a = αr (the string does not slip), mg − T = ma and Tr = Iα give a = g/(1 + I/mr²), whatever the mass. A thin axle makes I/mr² large, so a yo-yo falls slowly and spins fast.

Energy: the height lost, mgh, is shared between moving down, ½mv², and spinning, ½Iω²; the share in spinning is k/(1 + k) with k = I/mr². The string does no net work, since its end at the spool is at rest. Massless string, no air drag; the spools are drawn larger than the height scale.

More about this

With L fixed, KE = L²/2I, so the kinetic energy grows by the same factor that I shrinks. ω grows by that factor too, which is why the ω and KE lines on the chart lie on top of each other. The extra energy is work done by your arms: they pull each mass toward the axis, and the masses move inward along that pull.

More about this

A block on the left (x < 0) twists the beam counterclockwise, one on the right clockwise. Balanced means m₁x₁ + m₂x₂ + m₃x₃ = 0: a heavy block close in matches a light block far out. Uniform 4 m beam of 10 kg pivoted at its center, so its own weight has no torque. Blocks are treated as point masses fixed on the beam's center line.
