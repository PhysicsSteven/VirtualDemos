Slope Fields and Euler's Method

Type a differential equation dy/dt = f(t, y) and see its slope field: a short line at each point with the slope the equation gives there. Click to start a solution curve, step along it with Euler's method, and compare with the accurate solution. The phase line beside the field marks the equilibria and whether they are stable.

Play takes Euler's steps one at a time. Click the field to start a curve; drag to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom.

What you can type
  • Write the right side in t and y: -0.1(y - 20), t - y, y(1 - y/50).
  • + − * / ^ and parentheses; 2y, 3sin(t) and (y+1)(y−1) multiply.
  • sin cos tan, exp or e^t, ln, sqrt, abs or |y|, pi
  • A pasted dy/dt = or y' = at the start is fine.
  • ln is the natural log and log is base 10.
  • ^ takes only the number right after it: e^2x is e²·x, so type e^(2x) for e to the power 2x.
How it works

The box is read by the site's own parser (nothing you type is run as code). Each short line of the field has the slope f(t, y) at its center, drawn at the same angle the graph shows for that slope on these axes.

Euler's method steps from the start point with the slope at the start of each step: y ← y + h·f(t, y), t ← t + h. It takes as many whole steps of h as fit before the Run to t time. The accurate solution is computed with an adaptive Runge–Kutta method (Dormand and Prince's fifth order pair) to about ten significant figures, so the error readout is Euler's own error; where the equation has a formula for its solution (cooling, decay, the RC circuit, linear drag, logistic growth) the two agree to that accuracy.

The phase line is drawn when f depends on y only (an autonomous equation). Its equilibria are the zeros of f(y) in the view, found by bisection; an arrow up means y grows, down means it falls. A filled dot is stable (arrows point in on both sides), an open dot unstable, a half dot semistable. Isoclines join the points where the slope has one value; the one for slope 0 is the nullcline.

The mystery setups draw their k, room temperature, growth rate and carrying capacity at random each time the page opens or New mystery is pressed. The thermometer readings of the cooling cup are simulated from that model, rounded to 0.1 °C.
