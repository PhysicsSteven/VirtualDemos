Pendulums and Chaos

A pendulum keeps time only for small swings: big swings take longer. Hang a second pendulum from the first and the motion can turn chaotic, so two starts that differ by a millionth of a radian soon do completely different things.

Small oscillations

More about this

For small angles the equations become linear, and every motion is a sum of two normal modes. In mode 1 the rods swing together; in mode 2 they swing opposite. For equal masses and lengths ω² = (g/L)(2 ∓ √2). From rest keeps the present angles and sets both speeds to zero.

Copies and separation

More about this

Copy k starts with the upper rod turned k·δ more than copy 0, everything else the same. The separation d is measured in phase space, d² = Δθ₁² + Δθ₂² + (Δω₁² + Δω₂²)L₁/g. The Lyapunov exponent λ is estimated from a nearby orbit that is pulled back to 10⁻⁸ every 0.1 s, averaging ln of the growth; it is a finite time estimate.

Time and display

Model: point masses on rigid massless rods, frictionless pivots, no air drag, uniform g. The motion comes from the exact nonlinear equations of motion (from the Lagrangian), integrated with an adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) method at a tolerance of 10⁻¹¹ per step; the energy drift readout shows how well the integration keeps energy fixed. The small angle period 2π√(L/g) and the normal mode frequencies are the linearized limits, drawn as references.
