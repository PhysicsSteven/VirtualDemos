e/m of the Electron

Electrons from a 100 to 200 V gun bend into a glowing circle in the field of a pair of Helmholtz coils. Read the radius on the mirrored scale without parallax and measure the charge to mass ratio of the electron.

Model

The apparatus follows the PASCO SE-9629 manual: two coils of N = 130 turns with radius R = 158 mm (spacing taken equal to R, the Helmholtz condition), a bulb with helium at low pressure, an electron gun with a 6.3 V heater and up to 200 V on the anode, a constant current supply up to 3.5 A for the coils, a socket that turns the tube by up to 30° either way, and a mirrored scale on one coil. The deflection plates are drawn but not wired. The manual quotes e/m = 1.75 × 10¹¹ C/kg as the accepted value.

Exact within the model: each electron leaves the gun with kinetic energy eV and moves under F = −e v × B. With B uniform the path is a circle, r = mv/(eB), so e/m = 2V/(B²r²). e/m = 1.75882000838 × 10¹¹ C/kg and μ₀ are CODATA 2022 values.

Computed, when the coil field is on: B everywhere from the exact field of two circular loops (complete elliptic integrals), and the path from a step by step integration. Off the center the field is a little weaker, so circles come out a little larger than the center field predicts and e/m from the center field comes out low, by about 0.1% for small circles up to about 1% for a 5 cm circle. The earth's field option adds 2 × 10⁻⁵ T, about the horizontal part of the earth's field in the southern US, along the coil axis.

Idealized: each coil is one loop at its mean radius; electrons are treated without relativity (at 200 V that changes r by about 0.01%); the gun is a point 5 cm below the center of a 6.5 cm radius bulb; the scale is 8.5 cm behind the beam and your eye 30 cm in front of it. In a real tube the electrons lose a little energy in the anode and in collisions with helium, so the real e/m comes out high; that is not simulated. The meter jitter (about 0.2 V and 2 mA) and the flicker of the beam edges (up to about 1 mm) are simulated. The moving dots only show the direction of travel, not the speed.
