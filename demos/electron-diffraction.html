Model

The tube follows the common teaching electron diffraction tube: an electron gun, a thin polycrystalline graphite film at the end of the anode, and a fluorescent screen on the inside of a glass bulb. Its numbers are from the maker's data sheet: graphite spacings d₁₀ = 0.213 nm and d₁₁ = 0.123 nm, graphite to screen L = 125 ± 2 mm, bulb diameter about 130 mm (R = 65 mm), screen diameter about 100 mm, heater at most 6.3 V, anode 0 to 5 kV (2 to 5 kV here). Some lab handouts quote L = 130 mm or 135 mm: enter the value you use for L and R in the analysis.

Exact: λ = h/√(2meV(1 + eV/2mc²)) with CODATA constants. The term eV/2mc² is 0.2 % at 2 kV and 0.5 % at 5 kV, so the relativistic λ is shorter by 0.10 % to 0.24 %. Bragg's law 2d sin θ = λ (first order); each diffracted beam leaves at 2θ to the beam, on a cone. The demo finds exactly where each cone meets the sphere: the graphite sits on the axis, L − R = 60 mm behind the center of the bulb. The flexible ruler laid on the glass through the central spot measures the arc between the two crossings, D = R(α₊ − α₋); the caliper held in front measures the straight chord, D = R(sin α₊ − sin α₋).

The small angle formula λ ≈ dD/2L treats the screen as flat and 2θ as small. With the arc on this sphere its errors nearly cancel: d comes out within 0.2 % for both rings from 2 to 5 kV. With the chord, d comes out 0.4 % to 1 % high for the inner ring and 1.2 % to 3 % high for the outer ring. The Exact formula inverts the sphere geometry for the tool each row was read with.

Modeled, not computed from first principles: the ring widths from the beam size and the small graphite crystallites (full width about 1.3 mm inner, 1.5 mm outer), the ring brightness, the halo around the central spot and how the glow grows with V. Fainter rings (second order, and the 0.107 nm and 0.081 nm spacings) are left out. The bar magnet is a behavioral model: it tilts the beam before the graphite by about 7° at 25 mm on 4 kV, falling as 1/r³ and as 1/√V; its field inside the bulb is neglected. Parallax and the glass thickness are neglected.

Simulated: the beam's small tilt off the axis, a slow wander of the pattern of about 0.1 mm, meter jitter of a few volts, and the 0.5 mm scatter of the procedure's readings, all fixed by the seed.
