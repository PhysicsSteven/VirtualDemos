The model and its sources

Flicker: a ball of diameter D that glows evenly and lets its own light out (optically thin). Every part brightens at the same instant; the light from the slice at depth x arrives x/c later. A flash is seen spread over D/c, brightest in the middle (the slice through the center is the biggest); a region that turns on and stays on is seen rising for D/c. So a change seen in a time t means D is no more than about c t. Light hour, light day and light year use c = 299,792,458 m/s exactly, the Julian year, and the au = 149,597,870,700 m.

The engine: L = η ṁ c². η = 1 − √(8/9) = 5.72% for gas that spirals in to the innermost stable orbit of a black hole that does not spin (3 Schwarzschild radii) and 1 − 1/√3 = 42.3% for one spinning as fast as it can (Novikov and Thorne 1973); 10% is the usual round value (OpenStax Astronomy 2e §27.2); hydrogen fusion turns 0.7% of the mass into energy. The Eddington luminosity L_Edd = 4πG M m_p c/σ_T is where the push of the light on the electrons, which drag the protons with them, equals gravity on the protons. The disk temperature is the thin disk's, T(r)⁴ = (3GMṁ/8πσr³)(1 − √(r_in/r)) with r_in = 3 r_s (Shakura and Sunyaev 1973). Constants: CODATA 2022 (G, m_p, σ_T, σ, Wien's b) and the IAU 2015 nominal L☉ and GM☉.

The jet: a blob moving at speed v at angle θ to our line of sight. Light it gives off later starts closer to us, so it arrives sooner, and the blob seems to cross the sky at v_app = v sin θ/(1 − (v/c) cos θ) (Rees 1966). The fastest it can seem is γv, at cos θ = v/c. M87: features in the jet seen moving at 4c to 6c with the Hubble Space Telescope (Biretta, Sparks and Macchetto 1999, ApJ 520, 621); the jet points about 17° from our line of sight (Walker et al. 2018, ApJ 855, 128). Saturn's orbit: semimajor axis 9.58 AU (JPL), so 19.2 AU across.
