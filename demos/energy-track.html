The model and its formulas

K = ½mv², U = mg(y − y₀), E = K + U + Eth stays the same

on the track: m dv/dt = −mg sinθ ∓ μN, N = m(v²κ + g cosθ)

Eth grows by μN Δs while sliding

Model: the skater is a point mass that slides (no rolling, no air drag). The track is a smooth curve through its points (a natural cubic spline). On the track the motion is found in steps of 0.5 ms (fourth order Runge–Kutta) and the speed is then set from the energy balance, so K + U + Eth equals the starting total at every step. Kinetic friction is μN against the motion; at rest the same μ holds the skater on a slope where tanθ ≤ μ. The track pushes but cannot pull: where it would have to pull (N < 0) the skater leaves it, unless Stick to the track is on. In the air the path is the exact parabola. Landing on the track keeps the speed along the track and turns the speed across it into thermal energy (a perfectly inelastic bump); landing on the ground does the same with the downward speed, and the walls stop the skater. g: Earth 9.81 m/s², and the Moon 1.62, Mars 3.71 and Jupiter 24.79 m/s² from the NASA Planetary Fact Sheet.
