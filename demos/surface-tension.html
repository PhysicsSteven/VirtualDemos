Surface Tension and Capillarity

A liquid's surface pulls like a stretched sheet. Watch it lift water up thin glass tubes and push mercury down, squeeze the air inside drops and bubbles until a small bubble empties into a big one, hold up a steel needle and a water strider, and drag a sliding wire with a force that does not grow as the soap film stretches.

The model and its formulas

capillary height h = 2γ cos θ/(ρ g r)

rise in time (Washburn with gravity): dh/dt = (r²/8ηh)(ρ g h_eq − ρ g h)

drop: P_in − P_out = 2γ/r; bubble (two surfaces): 4γ/r

resting on the surface: w = 2γL sin φ, so at most 2γL

sliding wire: F = 2γl, whatever the film's length
