Capture stores the liquid, ball, tube and the gate time between the marks (0.01 s); v = 0.200 m / t. Stokes' law predicts v ∝ d², so plot v against d² for one liquid, ball and tube: η = (ρ_b − ρ_f) g / (18 × slope). The fit uses the rows that match the current settings.

The model and its formulas

Blind hides η, the Stokes prediction, the Reynolds number and the fitted η, leaving the timer and the densities. The Mystery liquid always hides its η. In Flow in a pipe, Blind hides the flow rate, speed, Reynolds number, regime and η, leaving the cylinder, the stopwatch and the pressure gauge.

Flow in a pipe: a smooth horizontal glass pipe fed from a constant head tank; the outlet valve sets the flow rate (0 to 60 mL/s in the 6 mm pipe, 0 to 100 mL/s in the 10 mm pipe). Two taps 0.500 m apart, far enough downstream that the flow is fully developed (the pipe before them is drawn shortened), feed a differential pressure gauge (0.1 Pa) and two manometer tubes. The pressure drop is the Darcy–Weisbach ΔP = f (L/D) ρv²/2 with f = 64/Re below Re = 2000 (exactly Poiseuille's law, ΔP = 8ηLQ/(πr⁴)) and Blasius's f = 0.3164 Re^(−1/4) for a smooth pipe above Re = 3000; between them, the range OpenStax calls unstable, f is interpolated on logarithmic axes. Water: η = 1.002 mPa·s at 20 °C and 0.653 mPa·s at 40 °C (OpenStax Table 14.4), ρ = 998.2 and 992.2 kg/m³ (CRC Handbook). The Mystery liquid is a water and glycerol mix with its own hidden η. The dye's path is drawn to show the kind of flow (straight in laminar flow, puffs in the unstable range, mixed across the pipe when turbulent) and slowed down; the eddies are illustrative, and the flow rate and pressure drop come only from the formulas.

Model: drag F = 6πηrv × K(d/D) × C_D Re/24, with the standard drag curve of Clift, Grace and Weber (Oseen's 1 + 3Re/16 below Re = 0.01) and Haberman and Sayre's wall factor K for a sphere on the axis of a tube in slow flow (the K you apply). At higher Re the walls matter less: the factor is weakened following Di Felice (1996), whose fit covers Re up to about 200. The ball's motion includes buoyancy and the added mass ½ρ_f V. Densities in kg/m³: steel 7800, glass 2500, nylon 1140. Liquids at 20 °C: glycerol 1261 and η = 1.41 Pa·s; castor oil 961 and 0.986 Pa·s; motor oil (typical SAE 30) 875 and 0.29 Pa·s; water 998 and 1.00 mPa·s.
