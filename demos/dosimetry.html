Dosimetry: Kerma, Equilibrium and the Ion Chamber

Photons hand their energy to electrons, and the electrons carry it deeper before they leave it as dose: watch kerma and absorbed dose part in the buildup and run side by side past it. Then collect the ions in a Farmer chamber and calibrate a proton beam the way a TRS-398 calibration does it, one correction at a time.

    The model and its formulas

    K_col(z) = Φ hν (μ_en/ρ) e^(−μz); D(z) = ∫ K_col(z − x) G(x) dx; past the buildup D/K_col = β = ∫ G(x) e^(μx) dx

    Primary photons only (a narrow beam view of a broad field, so scattered photons leave), water of density 1.000 g/cm³. μ/ρ and μ_en/ρ are NIST's. Each interaction makes a Compton electron with the Klein–Nishina spectrum and angle; it goes straight along its first direction for its ESTAR CSDA range and leaves its collision stopping power along the way. Real electrons scatter and zig zag, so the real buildup has a different shape: the model is a sketch of the mechanism, not a depth dose for planning (⁶⁰Co: d_max 4.5 mm here; Khan's tables give 0.5 cm measured). Coherent, photoelectric and pair events are left out of the electron spread (1 % of the interactions or less from 0.2 to 2 MeV, 6 % at 4 MeV). The Monte Carlo scores 50 depth bins (0.5 cm bins at depth) in batches of 4000 histories; the readout at the probe is read linearly between the two nearest bin centers, and so is its uncertainty.

    D_air = M/(ρ_air V) · W_air/e; D_w = D_air s_w,air p_Q (Bragg–Gray)

    D_w,Q = M k_TP k_pol k_s N_D,w k_Q; k_TP = (273.2 + T)/(273.2 + 20) · 101.325/P; k_pol = (|M₊| + |M₋|)/(2|M|)

    pulsed: f = ln(1 + u)/u, u = α n₀ d²/((k₁ + k₂) V); continuous: f = 1/(1 + m² d⁴ q/(6V²))

    k_Q = (s_w,air)_Q W_Q p_Q / ((s_w,air)_Co W_Co p_Co); s_w,air = 1.137 − 4.3 × 10⁻⁵ R_res + 1.84 × 10⁻³/R_res

    The chamber is simulated from these formulas and the key data below, so the protocol closes on itself: what you calculate is the dose the demo delivered, within the reading noise (0.05 %, simulated) and the two voltage approximation. The polarity effect (+0.10 % in protons, +0.05 % in ⁶⁰Co), the standards laboratory's dose rate and the plan's 1.000 Gy are simulated values. A real calibration uses the chamber's own certificate and the current tables of TRS-398 Rev. 1 (2024), whose k_Q values were recalculated with ICRU 90 key data and Monte Carlo chamber factors (thimble chambers moved by up to 1.7 %). This is a teaching version, not a calibration protocol.
