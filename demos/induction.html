Charging by Induction

Bring a charged rod near two metal spheres without touching them. Free electrons in the metal move, so the near side and the far side get opposite charges. Separate the spheres, or ground one, before taking the rod away, and the charge stays.

Blind hides the net charges and potentials; the marks on the spheres stay, as an electroscope or a charge probe would show.

Model: two perfectly conducting spheres on ideal insulating stands and the rod's tip as a point charge, solved exactly with Kelvin image charges (a thin wire or contact makes the spheres one conductor; ground holds a sphere at zero potential). The marks are drawn from the surface charge density in the plane of the drawing: their darkness is in proportion to the density, and their sign is its sign. Real charge sits as a tiny excess or shortage of electrons; only electrons move.
