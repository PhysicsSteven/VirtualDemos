Galaxy Collisions

Two galaxies swing past each other. Gravity pulls harder on the near side of each disk than on the far side, and draws out long tails and bridges of stars, while the stars themselves almost never touch. Then follow Andromeda, falling toward the Milky Way.

The model and its sources

A restricted N body model after Toomre and Toomre (1972, ApJ 178, 623): each galaxy is a single mass, a Plummer sphere of mass M and scale a (2 kpc for the disks of the encounter setups; 20 kpc for the Milky Way and 25 kpc for Andromeda, standing in for their dark halos), and its disk is 1,200 massless test stars on circular orbits spread from 0.2 to 1 of the disk radius. The stars feel both galaxies but not each other. The two centers pull each other as points softened by √(a₁² + a₂²), so their orbit is a parabola with the closest approach set (slightly wider when the spheres overlap). Leapfrog steps of 0.5 Myr (1 Myr for Andromeda) keep the energy to a part in a million.

What it leaves out: the stars' own gravity, gas (which does collide, makes starbursts and falls to the center), and the dark halos' particles, which soak up the orbital energy (dynamical friction). Without them the galaxies here swing apart again; real ones fall back and merge, often into one elliptical galaxy.

Andromeda: distance 770 kpc, approach speed 109.3 km/s and sideways speed 17.0 km/s (van der Marel et al. 2012, ApJ 753, 8; their model with halos gave a first pass in 3.87 Gyr and a merger in 5.86 Gyr); with Gaia, the sideways speed may be 57 km/s (van der Marel et al. 2019, ApJ 872, 24). Masses 1.0 × 10¹² M☉ (Milky Way) and 1.5 × 10¹² M☉ (Andromeda) as in van der Marel et al. 2012. Including the uncertainties and the other Local Group galaxies, a merger within 10 Gyr is about even odds (Sawala et al. 2025, Nature Astronomy).

Why stars miss: the Sun's diameter 2R☉ = 1.3914 × 10⁶ km (IAU 2015 nominal radius); the chance one star hits another while crossing a path L through stars spaced s apart is n π(2R☉)² L with n = 1/s³ (a star hits when the centers come within one diameter, gravity's focusing left out).
