How it works

χ² = Σ (O − E)²/E over every cell. Goodness of fit: E = n·p for each category. Independence and homogeneity: E = (row total)(column total)/(grand total). Single variance: χ² = (n − 1)s²/σ₀². The p value is the area under the chi square curve beyond the statistic (demos/lib/vdstats.js, the regularized incomplete gamma function), as the book's calculator gives it.

The chi square curve is an approximation for counts: it is good when every expected count is at least 5 (book 11.2), which is why small cells are flagged and the decay counts are grouped. The single variance test needs the data to be normal.

The simulation needs no approximation. Goodness of fit: n items fall into the categories with the hypothesized chances. Decay counts: n Poisson counts with the estimated mean, regrouped in the same bins with their own estimated mean. Independence: the grand total falls into the cells with chances (row share)(column share). Homogeneity: each row keeps its own total and falls into the columns with the pooled column shares. Single variance: n normal values with σ = σ₀. The simulated p value is the share of simulated statistics at or beyond the observed one.

The simulated sources: a die whose six comes up with the chance you set (the other faces share the rest equally); a long lived radioactive source counted for 1 s intervals, arrivals at random times at the set rate (a Poisson process), and a counter that is blind for its dead time after each count it records; a fair die and a fair coin thrown together; and a stopwatch whose readings of a 2.006 s pendulum period (a 1.000 m simple pendulum) scatter normally with the set σ, rounded to 0.01 s. Every draw is seeded, so a draw set always gives the same data.
