Which slit detector

An idealized detector: when it fires, it records which slit the particle went through and does nothing else. That record alone removes the interference: those particles form the sum of two single slit patterns. It fires for a fraction η of the particles, so the pattern is η(I₁ + I₂) + (1 − η)I₁₂ and the fringe contrast drops to 1 − η.

Only one particle is in the apparatus at a time in a real single particle experiment, and each one is detected at one point. With a few dozen hits the screen looks random. The pattern shows up only after hundreds to thousands.

Positions are y = L tan θ, measured from the center of the screen. The orders that land exactly on an envelope zero are missing. The falling envelope pulls each actual peak slightly toward the center, most visibly when d/a is small.

Intensity on the screen

I(θ) = I₀ · cos²(πd sin θ/λ) · [sin β/β]², β = πa sin θ/λ

The first factor is two slit interference: fringes spaced λL/d. The second is the diffraction of each slit: a broad envelope whose first zero sits at sin θ = λ/a. The pattern is their product, so the envelope sets how bright each fringe can be.
