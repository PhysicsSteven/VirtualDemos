Units, Scales and Estimates

The toolkit for the first week of physics: how big things are, converting units with exact factors, checking and finding formulas by dimensions, carrying the right number of significant figures, and making Fermi estimates.

SI units

Convert with a chain of exact factors

Each factor equals 1 (top and bottom are the same amount), so multiplying by it changes the units, not the quantity. Units that appear on top and bottom cancel.

Quantity

Find a formula from its dimensions

Guess that the answer is a product of powers of the quantities it could depend on. Choose the powers so the dimensions match the answer's. A pure number in front (like 2π) cannot be found this way.

Question

Combine measurements with the right number of figures

Multiplying or dividing: keep as many significant figures as the least precise factor. Adding or subtracting: keep as many decimal places as the least precise term.

Fermi estimate: multiply rough factors

Break the question into factors you can estimate, give each a low and a high value, and multiply. The answer is an order of magnitude, with a range.

About this tool

Lab

Blind hides the results (the converted value, the dimension check, the rounded answer, the estimate and the object names on the ruler and in the zoom) so students can work first.

Sizes on the ruler are typical or approximate values; ranges in nature are wide (a human hair is 20 to 180 μm). Conversion factors are the exact legal definitions (1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 cal = 4.184 J), except where marked.
