RC Circuits

A resistor limits how fast charge can reach a capacitor. Every change happens on the same clock, the time constant τ = RC, and always along an exponential.

Setups

Switch

Charge puts the battery in the loop. Discharge takes it out, so the capacitor drives current back through the resistor by itself.

Circuit

Resistors and capacitors come in standard values (E12 and E6 series), so the sliders step through those. You can also drag the power supply, the resistor and the top plate on the drawing, or the τ marker on the plots.

Time

Plot window

The plots and the clock show the circuit's own time. A τ of a millisecond is played in slow motion, a τ of several minutes is fast forwarded; the rate is always shown next to the clock.

Kirchhoff loop rule

Going around the loop, the battery raises the potential by ε, the resistor drops IR and the capacitor drops Q/C. The drops always add up to ε, at every instant.

Charging an empty capacitor, the battery moves charge Q = Cε through a potential difference ε, so it does work Cε2. The capacitor ends up with ½Cε2. The other half heats the resistor, whatever the value of R: a small R gives a large current for a short time, a large R a small current for a long time, and ∫I2R dt comes out the same.

Model: the bench supply is an ideal battery with no internal resistance, ideal wires and switch, no inductance, and a switch that flips instantly. Between changes the exact solution Q(t) = Qf + (Q₀ − Qf) e−(t−t₀)/τ is used, starting from the charge at the moment of the change, so there is no step size error. Dot speed is proportional to I, scaled so that ε/R of the resistor in the loop is one fixed speed. The window in the capacitor shows its two plates (a real electrolytic capacitor rolls them up). The plate symbols show Q in proportion to C × 12 V. The plate spacing is drawn on a log scale; for a parallel plate capacitor C ∝ 1/d. The resistor's color bands encode R. The oscilloscope probes VC (channel 1) and VR (channel 2); I = VR/R and Q = CVC are its math channels M1 and M2.
