White Dwarf Cooling

A white dwarf has no fuel left. It shines by leaking the heat stored in its ions, fades for billions of years, and freezes into a crystal from the center out.

Stars form at a steady rate over the disk age, with Salpeter masses 1.0 to 7.3 M☉ and main sequence lives of 10 Gyr (M/M☉)−2.5. Each leaves a white dwarf of 0.109 Mi + 0.394 M☉ (Kalirai et al. 2008) that cools on this page's tracks. The dots and the histogram are simulated from this model, not Gaia data.

Real white dwarf counts drop sharply below about 10−4.5 L☉ (Liebert, Dahn & Monet 1988): none has had time to fade further. Detailed cooling models turn that cutoff into an age for the local disk of about 8 to 10 Gyr (Winget et al. 1987, 9.3 ± 2 Gyr; Leggett, Ruiz & Bergeron 1998, 8 ± 1.5 Gyr).

Model

Core. Isothermal at Tc, with the density profile of a zero temperature Chandrasekhar white dwarf (ideal degenerate electrons, μe = 2, no Coulomb corrections), held fixed as it cools. Carbon/oxygen is 50:50 by mass. The radius stays fixed; real hot white dwarfs are a few percent larger.

Envelope. The textbook Kramers opacity relation with Shapiro and Teukolsky's envelope X = 0, Y = 0.9, Z = 0.1. Real envelopes are hydrogen or helium, and detailed models are brighter at a given Tc; this relation is the largest simplification here. Neutrino cooling, which dominates for hot white dwarfs above about 25,000 K, is left out, and so is any energy from contraction.

Heat. Ions only. Liquid: ideal gas 3/2 k (Mestel), or the Coulomb liquid fit Uex/NkT = −0.8992Γ + 0.596Γ0.3253 − 0.268 (DeWitt, Slattery & Stringfellow; fit range 1 ≤ Γ ≤ 220, held at 220 above), linear mixing for C/O. Solid: 3k per ion (Dulong–Petit).

Crystallization. One melting value Γm = 175 for a one component plasma (Potekhin & Chabrier 2000), applied to C/O with ⟨Z5/3⟩. Real mixtures melt at a somewhat different Γ and separate as they freeze (the solid is richer in oxygen); that phase separation and the settling of ²²Ne both release extra energy and are left out. Latent heat 0.77 kT per ion (Salaris et al. 2000). Debye cooling, when the lattice heat capacity drops below 3k, is marked where Tc ≈ 0.1 θD, θD ≈ 4×10³ ρ1/2 K (Althaus et al. 2010), but not modeled.

HR diagram. Schematic: each white dwarf is a blackbody seen through flat bands (BP 330 to 680 nm, RP 630 to 1050 nm, G 330 to 1050 nm), with zero points set by the Sun (MG = 4.67, GBP − GRP = 0.82). Real white dwarf spectra and Gaia passbands shift these colors by a few tenths of a magnitude.
