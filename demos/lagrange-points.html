More about this

L1, L2 and L3 sit on the line through the masses; their positions are roots of a fifth degree equation, solved here by Newton's method. L4 and L5 make equilateral triangles with the two masses for any μ. Growth times and libration periods come from the linearized motion about each point.

More about this

In the rotating frame C = 2Ω − v² stays fixed, so v² = 2Ω − C. Where 2Ω < C the speed would be imaginary: that shaded region is forbidden, and the gold curve where v = 0 is its edge. Frames

Model

The circular restricted three body problem: two masses on circular orbits about their center of mass, and a test particle too light to disturb them. Motion is in their orbital plane only. Real L1 and L2 spacecraft such as JWST fly three dimensional halo orbits; the planar Lyapunov orbits here are their flat relatives. Other planets, the Sun's pull on the Moon, the eccentricity of the real orbits and radiation pressure are left out.

The exact equations in the rotating frame, ẍ − 2ẏ = ∂Ω/∂x and ÿ + 2ẋ = ∂Ω/∂y, include the Coriolis (2ẏ, 2ẋ) and centrifugal terms. They are integrated with an adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) method with local error 10⁻¹² (10⁻¹⁰ for the swarm); the drift readout checks it. A particle that touches a body stops there, and one beyond 8 a stops too. The Sun centered view is the same motion transformed exactly, never integrated separately. The Sun–Earth system uses the Earth–Moon barycenter as m₂.
