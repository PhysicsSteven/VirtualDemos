River

The model and its formulas

vboat,shore = vboat,water + vwater,shore

More about this

The river is 60 m wide. The current is the same everywhere, or (Slower near the banks) zero at each bank and fastest mid river, u(y) = 4u y(W − y)/W², averaging 2u/3; a real river's profile is roughly like this. The boat keeps its heading and speed relative to the water, so its path curves when the current varies. The arrows at the left show the current across the river.

#Strings, mass and angleRing
More about this

Each string pulls the ring with its tension, which for a hanging mass at rest is its weight, F = mg with g = 9.81 m/s². Angles are counterclockwise from the 0° mark. The strings are massless, the ring is light and slides freely on the table, and every string is taken to point at the pin, ignoring the ring's few millimetres of play. The model table's sizes are chosen: strings leave the table 20 cm from the center, the ring is 4 cm across (to the middle of its 3 mm wire) and the pin is 8 mm across, so an unbalanced ring rests against the pin with its center 14.5 mm toward the net pull. With the pin pulled, the ring is pulled toward the pulleys with the hanging weights and can rest only where those pulls add to zero, the weighted geometric median of the pulleys (the point that minimizes the sum of each weight times its distance, which is where the hangers hang lowest). It is shown sliding slowly along the net pull and stopping at the first place the pulley friction can hold it; when one string outweighs the rest the ring runs toward the rim and is stopped there, as a real ring would be when a hanger reaches the floor. The 3D view is drawn to the same scale on a table 36 cm across; each hanger drops by as much as the ring moves toward its pulley. A hanger is drawn as 50 g with brass slotted masses (thickness from their mass and the density of brass, 8500 kg/m³) making up the rest; the split is a picture, only the total matters. Pulley friction lets each string's tension differ from its hanging weight by up to the amount set (given as the weight of that many grams; a chosen value, not a measured one), so the ring centers over a small range of masses and angles, as on a real table. At 0 the pulleys are ideal and the ring centers only when the weights cancel exactly.
