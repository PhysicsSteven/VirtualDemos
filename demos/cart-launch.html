Ball Launched from a Moving Cart

A cart rolls along a track and fires a ball straight up. The ball keeps the cart's forward velocity, so while both move steadily it lands back in the launcher. Make the cart speed up or brake after the launch and the ball misses; tilt the track and it lands in the cart again.

SI units · g = 9.81 m/s² · no air resistance

The model and its formulas

Blind pauses each launch at the top of the flight and hides where the ball lands until you resume, so the class can predict first.

Model: no air resistance and a point ball. The launcher fires perpendicular to the track, so the launch pushes the cart into the track, not along it, and the cart's speed is unchanged. On the level track the cart moves at constant speed, or with the set acceleration after the launch (a motor push or a brake; a braking cart stops and stays stopped). On the incline the cart rolls without friction, so its acceleration along the track is g sin θ, the same as the ball's. Seen from the cart, the ball's velocity and acceleration are relative to the cart; when the cart speeds up or brakes, the ball's acceleration relative to it is g minus the cart's acceleration, so it drifts backward or forward. The ball counts as caught if it comes down within 2 cm of the launcher's center. Sizes are to scale.
