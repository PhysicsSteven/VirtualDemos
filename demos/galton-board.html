Galton Board

Balls fall through rows of pegs and bounce left or right at each one. One ball is unpredictable; thousands pile up into the binomial distribution, and with many rows into a bell curve.

The model and its formulas

P(k) = n! / (k!(n − k)!) · pᵏ (1 − p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ mean np standard deviation √(np(1 − p))

Model: at every peg a ball goes right with probability p and left with 1 − p, independently of every other peg and every other ball, so the bin number k (counted from the left, starting at 0) is the number of right bounces in n tries: a binomial distribution. Balls do not collide or bounce twice; the fall drawn between pegs is only a picture of the choice. Random choices come from a seeded generator (mulberry32), so they are simulated, not measured. The sample standard deviation divides by N − 1. The normal curve is drawn with the binomial mean and standard deviation, one bin wide per unit of k; it is an approximation that improves as np(1 − p) grows. With net steps on, a bin is labelled 2k − n, the position after n steps of ±1 (a one dimensional random walk).
