Superfluid Helium

Below 2.1768 K liquid helium becomes a superfluid: it stops boiling, flows through powder no gas could pass, climbs out of its beaker as a thin film, and part of it refuses to turn with a spinning stack of disks. Four experiments with helium 4 and one with helium 3, computed from measured properties of helium.

SI units
Speed
Pumping on the bath (it starts at 4.22 K, boiling at one atmosphere)
Fountain: a tube packed with fine powder at the bottom, a heater just above it
Creeping film: a glass beaker hanging in the bath at 1.40 K
Spinning disks: 40 aluminum disks on a torsion fiber, immersed in the bath
Helium 3: 0.050 mol of liquid in a cell squeezed by a bellows, starting at 20 mK on the melting curve
