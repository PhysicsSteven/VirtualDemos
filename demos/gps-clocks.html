GPS Clocks and Relativity

General relativity: clocks higher in a gravitational field run faster. Special relativity: moving clocks run slower. A GPS satellite's clock gains about 38 microseconds a day on a clock on the ground; uncorrected, positions would drift by kilometres a day.

More about this

Model: Earth is a non-rotating spherical mass (Schwarzschild field); each clock's rate relative to coordinate time is √(1 − 2GM/(rc²) − v²/c²), with v its speed in the non-rotating frame. The ground clock sits on a sphere of radius 6371 km, at rest or carried round at the equator once per sidereal day. Earth's flattening and the Sun and Moon are left out. The clocks run 1 simulated hour per second.
