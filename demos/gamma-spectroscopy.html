Gamma Spectroscopy

A virtual NaI(Tl) scintillation spectrometer, set up like ORTEC Experiment 3 (SPA38 detector, EASY-MCA-2K). Every pulse is simulated from physics: Monte Carlo photon transport in the crystal, detector resolution, the electronics and counting statistics. Acquire, mark peaks, calibrate, and measure.

More about this

EASY-MCA-2K: 2048 channels over 0 to 10 V. At 900 V, coarse gain 10 and fine gain 1.39 the 661.7 keV peak sits near channel 475 and full scale is about 2.87 MeV, the setting of ORTEC Experiment 3.1 (channel 238 of 1024) with the ⁶⁰Co sum peak near channel 1790. The clock speed makes the simulated clocks run faster than the wall clock; live and real times are the simulated ones. Pulses below the LLD (electronic noise, X-rays) are ignored and do not cost dead time.

More about this

Simulated, not measured. Nothing here is recorded data. Each spectrum is built from a Monte Carlo calculation of how photons deposit energy in the crystal, smeared by the detector resolution, mapped to channels by the electronics, and counted with Poisson statistics, so two acquisitions never look the same.

The apparatus is the one in ORTEC Experiment 3, Gamma-Ray Spectroscopy Using NaI(Tl) (AN34): an SPA38 integral assembly (38 mm × 38 mm NaI(Tl) with its photomultiplier), 556 high voltage supply, 113 preamplifier, 575A amplifier and EASY-MCA-2K. Experiment 3.10 (linear gate, coincidence gating) is not simulated.

Lines and intensities per decay from ENSDF, retrieved from NNDC (NuDat 3 and the ENSDF decay data sets) on 2026-10-21: ¹³⁷Cs, Browne and Tuli, Nucl. Data Sheets 108, 2173 (2007); ⁶⁰Co, Browne and Tuli, NDS 114, 1849 (2013); ²²Na, Basunia, NDS 127, 69 (2015); ¹³³Ba, Khazov, Rodionov and Kondev, NDS 112, 855 (2011); ⁵⁴Mn, Dong and Huo, NDS 121, 1 (2014); ¹⁰⁹Cd, Kumar, Chen and Kondev, NDS 137, 1 (2016), with its Ag K X-rays from NuDat; ⁵⁷Co, Bhat, NDS 85, 415 (1998); ⁶⁵Zn, Chen, NDS 202, 59 (2025); ⁴⁰K, Chen, NDS 140, 1 (2017). The data for the five sources that were already here agree with these evaluations. Lines weaker than 0.3% and X-rays below 15 keV (Fe, Cu and Ag L) are left out; the 14.4 keV gamma ray of ⁵⁷Co is kept. Sources are point sources on the axis (the real ones are 2.5 cm plastic disks); decay since the reference date is ignored.

Activities: the RSS8 sources are labeled about 1 µCi (the mixed source about 0.5 µCi ¹³⁷Cs and 1 µCi ⁶⁵Zn) with no stated tolerance, so each is given a hidden true activity between 0.8 and 1.2 times its label, drawn once per visit (an assumed spread). The GF-137-M-5 standard is 5.00 µCi with a stated ±5%, and the GF-057-M-20 source 20 µCi; both are simulated at exactly those values, so in Experiment 3.5 the ±5% is the uncertainty to carry, not an error in the simulation.

Attenuation: NIST XCOM (Berger, Hubbell, Seltzer et al., Standard Reference Database 8) for NaI, Pb, Cu, Al and Fe; Hubbell and Seltzer, NIST Standard Reference Database 126 (X-ray mass attenuation coefficients), retrieved 2026-10-21, for Mo, Sn and Ta. All are interpolated log–log between table energies. The Hubbell and Seltzer grid is coarser (100 and 150 keV around the ⁵⁷Co line), so at 122 keV the interpolated μ/ρ is about 2% high for Mo, 1.4% for Sn and 0.2% for Ta compared with a fine grid; these are also the values in Table 3.4 of the ORTEC manual (0.685, 1.020 and 2.592 cm²/g). For Al, Fe and Cu the manual's 122 keV values (0.154, 0.272, 0.321) are 1 to 3.5% above the XCOM fine grid values used here (0.152, 0.263, 0.311) for the same reason. In the crystal: photoelectric absorption, Compton scattering with the Klein–Nishina angular distribution (free electrons), and pair production with both 511 keV photons followed. Coherent scattering is left out in the crystal. Electrons deposit their energy where they are made (no escape, no bremsstrahlung). Densities: NaI 3.667, Pb 11.35, Cu 8.96, Al 2.699, Fe 7.874, Mo 10.22, Sn 7.31, Ta 16.654 g/cm³.

Absorbers: ORTEC pure metal foil sets of 10 identical foils each, Al 0.030 in, Fe 0.005 in, Cu 0.010 in, Mo 0.003 in, Sn 0.004 in and Ta 0.005 in per foil (FOIL-AL-30 and so on), and the lead absorbers of the RAS20 kit: 1124 mg/cm² (0.039 in, ORTEC manual Table 3.4) and 2066, 3448 and 7367 mg/cm² (the Spectrum Techniques 20 piece set, as tabulated in Commun. Phys. 35, 347 (2025)). The RAS20 has a fifth lead absorber whose value was not found; it is left out. The aluminum absorbers of the RAS20 are not offered (the manual does not use them). Absorbers sit just in front of the source and follow the narrow beam law e−μx with μ including coherent scattering; scattering in the absorber (buildup) is not modeled. Mo, Sn, Ta and Pb absorbers also give their K X-rays (normal incidence on an infinite sheet, with the photoabsorption of Elam, Ravel and Sieber); Al, Fe and Cu X-rays are below 9 keV and left out.

Crystal size, from this Monte Carlo with the source 10 cm from the face (photons entering the face; bare crystal, no resolution or threshold): at 661.7 keV the probability of a full energy deposit is 0.198 for 38 mm × 38 mm, 0.260 for 2″ × 2″ and 0.349 for 3″ × 3″, and the peak to total ratio (full energy events over all interacting events) is 0.401, 0.470 and 0.567. At 122 keV almost every interaction ends in the photopeak (0.956, 0.959, 0.962); at 1332 keV the ratio is 0.234, 0.294 and 0.387. A smaller crystal lets more Compton scattered photons escape, so it has the lower ratio.

Peak to total ratio: the crystal is bare. There is no aluminum can or reflector around its sides, no light guide or photomultiplier behind it, and no source holder; electrons never escape or radiate. In a real detector each of these moves events out of the photopeak or adds scattered photons to the continuum, so the simulated ratio of full energy events to all interacting events is too high. For a 3″ × 3″ crystal with the source 10 cm away it exceeds the measured values in Heath's catalog (R. L. Heath, Scintillation Spectrometry: Gamma-Ray Spectrum Catalogue, IDO‑16880, 1964, Appendix III, Table I) by 2 to 17%, more at higher energy: 0.834 against 0.820 at 323 keV, 0.568 against 0.536 at 662 keV, 0.428 against 0.395 at 1114 keV, 0.328 against 0.280 at 1837 keV. How much each missing part contributes has not been worked out. No published 38 mm (1.5″) or 2″ value has been compared: the 1.5″ × 1.5″ curves in the ORTEC manual (Fig. 3.7, intrinsic peak efficiency at 10 cm, after Heath) and in Saint-Gobain's efficiency notes are graphs only. Expect the simulated 38 mm ratio to be high in the same way. Peak energies, resolution and spectral shapes are the teaching point; absolute efficiencies and peak to total ratios are not those of a particular commercial detector, so find activities against the standard (Experiment 3.5), not from a computed efficiency.

Iodine K X-rays: above the 33.17 keV K edge a photoabsorption leaves a K vacancy with probability 1 − 1/J = 0.834 (jump ratio J = 6.04) and gives a K X-ray with probability ωK = 0.893, which is then followed and can escape (the escape peak 28 to 33 keV below a low energy line). Lead: K edge 88.0 keV, J = 4.73, ωK = 0.978. X-ray energies, yields and jump ratios from Elam, Ravel and Sieber, Radiat. Phys. Chem. 63, 121 (2002).

Backscatter and lead X-rays: the holder carries a backing (a lead brick or a steel plate, XCOM data for Fe, 7.874 g/cm³) whose face is 5 cm behind the source, taken as a thick disk of radius 6 cm. Photons leaving the source backward that hit it are Compton scattered once (Klein–Nishina, free electrons) or, in lead, photoabsorbed in the K shell; the scattered photon or the K X-ray reaches the detector after attenuation of the incoming and outgoing photon in the backing (single scattering in a thick slab; multiple scattering is left out, so the backscatter is a lower estimate). Scattering in the castle walls and the table is left out (absorbers: see above).

Coincidence summing: ⁶⁰Co (1173 and 1332 keV in cascade) and ²²Na (1274 keV with the two annihilation photons, back to back, from 89.96% of decays). Both photons of a decay are followed and their deposits added, directions uncorrelated. Summing in ¹³³Ba and ⁵⁷Co (122.1 keV with 14.4 keV), chance pileup and X-ray summing are left out; ⁶⁵Zn has no cascade to sum.

Resolution: Gaussian with FWHM/E = a/√E + 0.01, with a set by the FWHM at 662 keV (7.0% by default, typical of a small NaI(Tl) detector; set it to the value you measure in Experiment 3.4). The light output is taken as exactly proportional to energy (the small nonproportionality of NaI is left out). Entrance window: 0.5 mm aluminum (typical).

Electronics: PMT gain ∝ V7 (typical of a 10 dynode tube); pulse height = 0.25 mV per keV × (V/900 V)7 × coarse × fine; ADC full scale 10 V (EASY-MCA-2K, up to 2048 channels) with a zero offset of 18 mV. Electronic noise: 3000 pulses per second with a half Gaussian height of 25 mV rms at gain 20. Dead time: nonparalyzable, τ = 8 µs per pulse between the discriminators (typical of an analog MCA); live time accumulates as dt/(1 + nτ). Counts in each channel are Poisson.

Room background inside the castle (assumed, typical): 3.0 counts per second above 20 keV for the 2″ × 2″ crystal, as a falling exponential continuum plus a flat part, with 0.3 photons per second of ⁴⁰K at 1460.8 keV and 0.05 per second of lead X-rays entering the face; scaled with crystal volume for the other sizes (0.419 times for 38 mm × 38 mm, 3.375 times for 3″ × 3″).
