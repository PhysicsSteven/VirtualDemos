Vector Fields, Work and Curl

Type a field F(x, y) = (P, Q) and see its arrows and the flow it makes. Add up the work ∫F·dr along a path, test whether it depends on the route, find the potential of a conservative field, measure the curl with a paddle wheel and the divergence with a small box, and check Green's theorem around a loop. Derivatives are exact; integrals are computed numerically.

Play sends a bead along the path, adding up the work, while tracers drift along F. Drag the dots to move the path; drag elsewhere to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom.

How it works

Each box is read by the site's small parser (nothing you type is run as code). The partial derivatives come from the exact rules, so the curl ∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y and the divergence ∂P/∂x + ∂Q/∂y shown are exact formulas.

Line integrals ∫F·dr = ∫(P dx + Q dy) and fluxes ∮F·n ds are computed by adaptive Simpson's rule along each straight piece or arc, to about nine significant figures. The summed curl and divergence inside a circle or a square are double integrals by the same rule; inside a freehand loop they are summed over a grid of 200 by 200 small squares, good to about three figures.

F is called conservative here when its curl is zero at every sample point in view and the work around every small grid loop in view adds up to zero. The potential f has ∇f = F (for a force, the potential energy is U = −f; for an electric field, the potential is V = −f). Its value at a point is the work from the reference point along a straight line; the contours come from the same work summed along a grid.

The paddle wheel is fixed in place and turns at ω = ½ curl F, the rate a small piece of fluid with velocity F spins. Tracers move with velocity F, so they show its direction and size; for a force they are not the path a mass would take.
