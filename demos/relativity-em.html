Magnetism from Relativity

A wire carrying current is neutral, so a charge moving beside it feels only a magnetic force. Ride along with the charge and length contraction leaves the wire charged: the same push is now electric.

A sideways push is largest in the frame where the particle is at rest: F′ = γᵤF. Sideways momentum is the same in both frames, but the charge's clock runs slow in the lab, so the lab sees that momentum delivered over a γᵤ times longer time. The path is computed separately in each frame, and the distance from the wire against the charge's own clock τ comes out the same.

B does not vanish in the charge's frame: there the ions and electrons both move, at different speeds, so the wire still carries a current. In the lab E = 0 at q, so E² − c²B² is negative, and since it is the same in every frame, no observer sees a purely electric field there.

EMF′ = γ EMF exactly: the loop's frame is the ring's rest frame, where the push on its charges is γ times larger, as for the wire. At 1 m/s, γ − 1 = 5.6×10⁻¹⁸.

Einstein, 1905

Einstein opened "On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies" with this example. The current in the loop depends only on the relative motion, yet the physics of the day told two different stories: a moving loop's charges feel a magnetic force; a moving magnet makes an electric field. The asymmetry, he pointed out, is in the theory, not in the phenomena. In relativity both are one field seen from two frames.

A moving magnet also carries an electric dipole, p = v × m/c² at low speed. Here the motion is along m, so it is zero, and E′ only circles the axis.

Controls and model

Drag the 3D view to turn it, scroll or pinch to zoom, double click to reset. Ringed dots are grips: drag the test charge to change r, or an arrow tip to change a speed. With the view focused: ← → speed, ↑ ↓ distance, F switch frame, Space pause.

Model: exact special relativity at any speed. The wire is two infinitely long, thin lines of charge: ions with density +λ₀ at rest in the lab, and electrons drifting at v with density −λ₀ in the lab, so the lab sees a neutral wire. In any frame, each line's density is its density in its own rest frame times its γ in that frame. Fields of an infinite line: E = λ/(2πε₀r), B = μ₀I/(2πr). The force is the Lorentz force q(E + u × B) on the test charge. Ignored: the test charge's own field and radiation, the wire's resistance, and the small surface charges that push current around a real circuit.

What is drawn: with speeds near c each dot stands for 5 nC of charge (λ₀ × 0.5 cm), 20 of each kind per 10 cm in the lab; their spacing shows each line's density in the frame drawn. The charges are points, so only their spacing contracts. The ticks mark each line's own spacing, in its rest frame: the ions' is the lab spacing, the electrons' is γᵥ times wider (in the lab their spacing equals the ions', contracted from their own). The contraction control exaggerates only the drawing: drawn spacing = lab spacing × (true spacing ratio)k, for the dots and the ticks alike; at ×1 the drawing is true. Counts and all other numbers are always exact. Distances are to scale (the wire is drawn much thicker than a line). Time is slowed: 0.16 ns of the frame's own time per second on screen. The test charge, an electron or positron, follows the exact relativistic equation of motion dp/dt = q(E + v × B), computed separately in each frame (RK4, 1 ps steps); its own field and its radiation (about 10⁻¹⁵ of its energy) are ignored. In the real wire the true spacing, one electron per 10⁻²³ m of wire, and its 10⁻²⁶ change cannot be drawn: equal spacing is the honest picture, and the charge is not set free.

Model: the exact field of a charge that has moved at constant velocity forever (Heaviside, 1888). It points straight away from where the charge is now: E = kq(1 − β²)/(r²(1 − β² sin²θ)^3/2), with θ measured from the direction of motion, and B = v × E/c². No acceleration, so no radiation. The view rides with the charge; the grid below is at rest in the lab and slides past at the charge's speed, slowed for the screen. Field lines are spaced so their density shows |E|.

Model: the magnet is a uniformly magnetized cylinder, which is the same as a sheet of current K = M around its side. Its field is the exact field of each current loop (complete elliptic integrals), summed along the length by Gauss–Legendre quadrature, 120 nodes; the flux through the ring uses the exact mutual inductance of coaxial loops. Everything moves along the axis at constant speed; fields in the loop's frame come from the exact Lorentz transformation, at everyday speeds too. The EMFs are sums of the push on the charges over 48 pieces of the ring; −dΦ′/dt′ is a central difference. Ignored: the field of the induced current itself (a thin ring of high resistance). The motion on screen is slowed, and near c the magnet and spacing are drawn contracted by γ.

Model: the exact Lorentz transformation of the fields at one event, for a boost along x. Arrows show E and cB on one scale, so a field of 300 kV/m and one of 1 mT (cB = 299.8 kV/m) look about the same length.
