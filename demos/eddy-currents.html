Eddy Currents: Magnet in a Pipe

Drop the same strong magnet down a copper pipe, an aluminum pipe and a plastic pipe. In the metal pipes the moving magnet's changing flux drives currents around the wall, and by Lenz's law those currents push back: the magnet drifts down at a slow, steady speed.

More about this

Model: the magnet as a point dipole on the pipe's axis and a thin walled pipe (w much less than a), which gives a drag force in proportion to speed with the constant k above (Levin and coworkers, Am. J. Phys. 2006). A real magnet that nearly fills the pipe falls at the same order of speed but not exactly this one. The magnet does not touch the wall; air drag is ignored. Conductivity follows ρ = ρ₂₀[1 + α(T − 20 °C)] down to −50 °C; below that the resistivity is taken as a straight line in T to the measured value at −196 °C (7.2 times the 20 °C value for copper, 8 for aluminum).
