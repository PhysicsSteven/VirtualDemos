Eddy Currents: Magnet in a Pipe

Drop the same strong magnet down a copper pipe, an aluminium pipe and a plastic pipe. In the metal pipes the moving magnet's changing flux drives currents around the wall, and by Lenz's law those currents push back: the magnet drifts down at a slow, steady speed.

More about this

Model: the magnet as a point dipole on the pipe's axis and a thin-walled pipe (w much less than a), which gives a drag force in proportion to speed with the constant k above (Levin and coworkers, Am. J. Phys. 2006). A real magnet that nearly fills the pipe falls at the same order of speed but not exactly this one. The magnet does not touch the wall; air drag is ignored.
