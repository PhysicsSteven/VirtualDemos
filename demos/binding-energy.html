Nuclear Binding Energy

How tightly is each nucleus held together? Measured masses give the binding energy per nucleon. It peaks near iron and nickel, so fusing light nuclei and splitting heavy ones both release energy.

Build a nucleus

Add protons and neutrons one at a time with the buttons on the picture, the arrow keys on it (up and down for protons, left and right for neutrons), or a click on a square of the chart. Let it decay follows the main decay mode.

Where this comes from

Every ground state in NUBASE2020 (F. G. Kondev et al., Chinese Physics C 45, 030001 (2021)): half lives, decay modes and their shares, natural abundances, and the AME2020 mass excesses, including the ones AME2020 estimates from systematics (marked estimated). A nucleus not in the table has never been made, or falls apart too fast to be seen; NUBASE lists some as particle unstable, bound neither for its last proton nor its last neutron. B = Z Δ(¹H) + N Δ(n) − Δ, so it counts the electrons with the protons, as in the other views. Sn and Sp, the energies to pull off the last neutron or proton, come from the same masses; a negative one means that nucleon is not bound. A mode NUBASE marks as not yet seen is left out, and Let it decay takes the largest share, so a chain with a branch follows only its main line.

More about this

Masses: AME2020, M. Wang et al., Chinese Physics C 45, 030003 (2021), taken from the NUBASE2020 table (F. G. Kondev et al., Chinese Physics C 45, 030001) as shipped with the pynucastro package. Only ground states with measured masses are shown (2550 nuclides); the 1008 values AME2020 estimates from systematics are left out. Stable and naturally occurring nuclides are those NUBASE2020 lists as stable or with a natural abundance.

Binding energies use atomic mass excesses, so the electrons are counted with the protons. This leaves the electrons' own binding in B, at most 0.8 MeV in total for uranium, a 0.04% effect. Q values are for nuclei at rest in their ground states.
