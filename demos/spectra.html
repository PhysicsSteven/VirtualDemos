More about this

A Balmer line is absorbed by a hydrogen atom whose electron already sits on level n = 2. In a cool star almost every atom is on n = 1 (Boltzmann factor), and in a hot star almost every atom has lost its electron (Saha equation). Only in between are there many atoms on n = 2: the Balmer lines are strongest in A stars, near 10,000 K.

Model, as in Carroll and Ostlie, An Introduction to Modern Astrophysics (2nd ed., §8.1): Boltzmann N₂/N₁ = (g₂/g₁) e−(E₂−E₁)/kT with gn = 2n² and E₂ − E₁ = ¾ × 13.598 eV; Saha NII/NI = (2kTZII/(PeZI)) (2πmekT/h²)3/2 e−χ/kT with χ = 13.598 eV. Approximations: partition functions ZI = 2 (ground level only) and ZII = 1 (a bare proton); neutral atoms counted as N₁ + N₂; the electron pressure is held fixed (Carroll and Ostlie use 20 N/m², where the peak falls near 9900 K) while a real atmosphere's changes with T; line strength also depends on things this model leaves out, so the strip under the chart only shows which way the lines go: each line is drawn darker on a log scale of N₂/Ntotal, from 10⁻⁴ of its peak (barely there) to the peak (black). More about this

The same photons, at the same moments, go into all six atoms. Only the quantum atoms (Bohr, de Broglie, Schrödinger) give hydrogen's real lines, and they absorb a photon only when its energy matches a jump between two levels, E = −13.6 eV/n² (levels up to n = 6 here). Thomson's electron has one natural frequency, so it answers one color, set by the size of the pudding. The classical planetary atom radiates as its electron circles, spirals in and collapses in 16 ps, giving a smear of every color rather than lines. Bohr's and de Broglie's atoms share the same levels: Bohr postulated them, de Broglie explained them as standing electron waves. Bohr's model says nothing about which way down an electron goes, so here each lower level is equally likely. Schrödinger's atom adds the angular momentum l, the rule that a photon changes l by one, and the rate of each jump, so its lines come out with their real relative strengths, and an electron that lands in 2s is stuck (it lives 0.12 s, against 1.6 ns for 2p).

Model: energies from RH = R∞/(1 + me/mp) (CODATA 2022), vacuum wavelengths, fine structure left out. Thomson λ = (2πa₀/α)(R/a₀)3/2. Classical collapse time r₀³/(4re²c) from r₀ = a₀, by Larmor's formula. Schrödinger rates A = (4/3)αω³/c² · lmax/(2l + 1) · |∫RnlRn′l′r³dr|² (Bethe and Salpeter §59, §63), within 0.05 % of NIST's. The lamp's photons spread ±0.6 % in energy, so a lamp set within 0.6 % of a line excites it; real atoms answer a far narrower band. Times are slowed: every excited state is held about one second on screen (its real lifetime is shown), 2s eight seconds, and the classical collapse takes five seconds. A photon on a line is absorbed every time and an ionizing photon one time in ten, so you can watch both; really a photon on a line is thousands of times likelier to be taken than one above the ionization energy. The photon from capturing an electron after ionization belongs to a continuum and is not drawn. Orbits are drawn with radius in proportion to n (really n²), and each Schrödinger cloud (m = 0, a slice through the nucleus) at its own scale.

Data

Line data: NIST Handbook of Basic Atomic Spectroscopic Data (J. E. Sansonetti and W. C. Martin), strong lines tables, air wavelengths from 300 to 1000 nm, for the 99 elements from hydrogen to einsteinium. Public domain. physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook

"I" marks lines from neutral atoms, "II" from atoms that have lost one electron. Line widths and colors on screen are drawn, not measured.

The tube and swatch colors are computed: each line from 360 to 830 nm, weighted by its NIST intensity, is summed with the CIE 1931 color matching functions (fit of Wyman, Sloan and Shirley 2013) and shown at full brightness in sRGB. Under All the tube uses the neutral atom lines, which dominate a low current discharge. NIST intensities are rough and depend on the light source, so the color is an estimate. Metals are really studied in flames, arcs or hollow cathode lamps; the tube stands in for any such source.
