More about this

T comes from the kinetic energy: in two dimensions the average is ⟨KE⟩ = kT. P measured is the momentum the molecules deliver to the walls, per unit wall length per unit time, averaged over about a second. In 2D, pressure is force per length (N/m).

A molecule is on the side its center is on. Time and the mean square displacement start when the divider opens (or is closed again). The displacement counts a bounce off a wall or the divider as if the molecule went on into a mirror image of the box: a box with mirror walls behaves exactly like an endless gas made of mirror copies of it, so this is how far the molecule wanders in that endless gas, and the walls do not cap it. T is from the kinetic energy, ⟨½mv²⟩ = kT.

More about this

Set each gas on its card beside the box, and the hole width above it. Changing a gas starts again with the divider closed: gas A fills the left half and gas B the right half, each at its own temperature. Then choose Hole or Removed above the box; that moment is t = 0. Masses are in atomic mass units u (helium 4, neon 20, argon 40, krypton 84, xenon 131). The molecules are hard disks of the diameter you choose. A molecule goes through the hole only if it fits, so the opening its center can use is the hole width minus the diameter.

More about this

Drag the right wall, or use the arrow keys on the box. A molecule bouncing off a wall that moves toward it comes back faster. For a slow squeeze of this 2D gas, T × A stays about constant. Pull the wall out faster than the molecules move and the gas barely cools.
