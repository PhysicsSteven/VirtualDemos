T comes from the kinetic energy: in two dimensions the average is ⟨KE⟩ = kT. P measured is the momentum the molecules deliver to the walls, per unit wall length per unit time, averaged over about a second. In 2D, pressure is force per length (N/m).

Drag the right wall, or use the arrow keys on the box. A molecule bouncing off a wall that moves toward it comes back faster. For a slow squeeze of this 2D gas, T × A stays about constant. Pull the wall out faster than the molecules move and the gas barely cools.

Molecules are hard disks 0.5 nm across, a bit larger than real argon (about 0.34 nm), drawn at that size or larger. Their finite size makes P run a few percent above NkT/A, more when the box is crowded.
