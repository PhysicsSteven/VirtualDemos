Radiobiology: Cell Survival and Fractionation

How many cells survive a dose of radiation, and what changes that number. Read the linear quadratic survival curves of two cell lines in air and without oxygen, run a simulated clonogenic assay and count the colonies, split a course of radiotherapy into 1 to 40 fractions to compare biologically effective doses in tumors and in late responding tissue, and see how the relative biological effectiveness of protons rises with LET and as the dose per fraction falls.

The model and its formulas

S = e^(−αD − βD²), ln S = −αD − βD², SF2 = S(2 Gy)

αD = βD² at D = α/β

OER(D) = D_anoxic / D_air for the same survival; low dose α_air/α_anoxic, high dose √(β_air/β_anoxic)

oxygen: m(p) = 1 + (m − 1) · p/(p + K) · (160 + K)/160, K = 3.8 mmHg, on α and on √β

PE = colonies / cells seeded (0 Gy); SF = colonies / (cells seeded × PE)

BED = n d (1 + d/(α/β)), EQD2 = BED / (1 + 2/(α/β))

RBE_max = 0.99064 + 0.35605 LET_d/(α/β)ₓ, RBE_min = 1.1012 − 0.0038703 √(α/β)ₓ LET_d

RBE = [√((α/β)² + 4D(α/β)RBE_max + 4D²RBE_min²) − α/β] / (2D)

Where the numbers come from
  • Linear quadratic model, α/β of 10 Gy for tumors and early responding tissue and 3 Gy for late responding tissue, the oxygen effect and the clonogenic assay: E. J. Hall and A. J. Giaccia, Radiobiology for the Radiologist, 8th ed. (Wolters Kluwer, 2019).
  • V79 and T1 α and β in air and in hypoxia, the means of the published values: J. Wenzl and J. J. Wilkens 2011, Radiat. Oncol. 6, 171 (doi:10.1186/1748-717X-6-171).
  • Oxygen dependence, K = 3.8 mmHg: the Alper and Howard-Flanders form as used by H. Zhu, J. Schuemann, Q. Zhang and L. Gerweck, arXiv:2201.02278 (2022), after Koch 1988 and others.
  • Prostate α/β 1.5 Gy (0.8 to 2.2): D. J. Brenner and E. J. Hall 1999, Int. J. Radiat. Oncol. Biol. Phys. 43, 1095. 60 Gy in 20 fractions against 74 Gy in 37: the CHHiP trial, D. Dearnaley et al. 2016, Lancet Oncol. 17, 1047.
  • Proton RBE model: A. L. McNamara, J. Schuemann and H. Paganetti 2015, Phys. Med. Biol. 60, 8399 (doi:10.1088/0031-9155/60/21/8399), with its fitted parameters (p₀ = 0.99064; some software, matRad among them, has 0.999064). Clinical RBE of 1.1 and the averages along a spread out Bragg peak: H. Paganetti 2014, Phys. Med. Biol. 59, R419.
  • Assay counting: N. A. P. Franken et al. 2006, Nat. Protoc. 1, 2315. Air 160 mmHg (21 % of 760 mmHg); arterial blood 95 and venous blood 40 mmHg: Guyton and Hall, Textbook of Medical Physiology.
