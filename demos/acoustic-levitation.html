More about this

A trap is a minimum of U + Wz along the axis; its depth is the energy needed to lift the bead over the lower of the two barriers beside it. Gravity pulls each trap below its pressure node (trap − node negative), by about asin(W/Fmax)/2k for a plane wave, so more for a denser bead. Away from the center the converging beams also shift the traps by tens of µm either way.

40 kHz is ultrasound, far above the roughly 20 kHz limit of human hearing. The Sound button plays a 400 Hz tone instead, the real frequency divided by 100, whose loudness follows the pressure amplitude |p| at the gold microphone. Drag the microphone through nodes and antinodes, or sweep the phase and listen to the pattern pass it.

Model

The levitator follows TinyLev (A. Marzo, A. Barnes and B. W. Drinkwater, Rev. Sci. Instrum. 88, 085105, 2017): 72 transducers of 10 mm diameter at 40 kHz, 36 in each of two bowls in rings of 6, 12 and 18 on a sphere of radius 60 mm, with the center position left open. The ring angles (10.5°, 21° and 31.5° from the axis) and the bowls sharing one center are this page's choices. Because the waves meet the axis at angles θ, the phase along the axis advances at k⟨cos θ⟩ near the center, so the traps there are λ/(2⟨cos θ⟩) = 4.76 mm apart rather than λ/2, closing toward λ/2 away from the center.

Each transducer is a piston of radius 5 mm in its far field: p = P₀V D(θ) ei(kd + φ)/d with D = 2J₁(ka sin θ)/(ka sin θ). The field is the sum over all 72, in free space: the bowls do not reflect or scatter sound, and there is no nonlinear acoustics. Within about 9 mm of a transducer face (its near field) this model fails, so that region is left blank. In the single emitter setup the flat reflector is rigid and infinite, replaced by the emitter's mirror image; sound reflected back off the emitter is left out, so there is no resonance build up.

Calibration, illustrative: P₀ = 0.30 Pa·m per volt peak to peak, from the Murata MA40S4S data sheet rating of 120 dB SPL at 30 cm for 10 V rms. Real arrays fall short of this (spread between transducers, square wave drive, heating), so a real levitator needs more drive than shown here.

Force: Gor'kov's potential for a small sphere, U = K₁|p|² − K₂|∇p|² with K₁ = πa³f₁/(3ρ₀c²) and K₂ = πa³f₂/(2ρ₀ω²), f₁ = 1 − ρ₀c²/Kp, f₂ = 2(ρp − ρ₀)/(2ρp + ρ₀). It holds for ka ≪ 1 in an inviscid fluid; the page warns above a = λ/10. Beads are rigid spheres (a real droplet flattens) and move only along the axis, where the traps also hold them sideways; they pass through each other, and acoustic streaming and heating are left out. Motion: (m + ½ρ₀V) dv/dt = −dU/dz − W + drag (Schiller and Naumann). Air at 20 °C and 1 atm: c = 343.2 m/s, ρ₀ = 1.204 kg/m³. Bulk moduli: foam 3 MPa (a rough figure), water 2.19 GPa, steel 160 GPa. Bead motion is shown 20 times slower than real.
