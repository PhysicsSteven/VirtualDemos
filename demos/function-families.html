Families of Functions

Turn a point around the unit circle and watch it draw sine and cosine, with the angle measured as arc length. Stretch and shift any function you type, and see what each number does. Reflect a graph in y = x to make its inverse, and watch the slope turn into 1/slope. Oscillations, phasors and exponential decay are these same moves.

How it works

The box is read by a small parser (demos/lib/vdmath.js); nothing you type is run as code. Angles are in radians.

Unit circle: the point is at (A cos θ, A sin θ) with θ = ωt + φ. On the unit circle (A = 1) the graph's θ axis uses the same scale as the circle, so the arc from (1, 0) to the point unrolls onto the axis at its true length: an angle in radians is that arc length divided by the radius.

Stretch and shift: g(x) = a·f(b(x − c)) + d is computed from f exactly as written, and its formula is f with x replaced by b(x − c), simplified. A point (x, y) of f lands at (c + x/b, a·y + d) on g.

Inverse: f must be one to one on the domain; the demo checks it at 4,001 points (the values must strictly rise or strictly fall). The mirror image is every point (x, f(x)) drawn at (f(x), x). The slope of f at x₀ is f′(x₀), exact from the derivative rules; the slope of the inverse at y₀ = f(x₀) is 1/f′(x₀). As a check, the demo also measures it from the inverse itself: f⁻¹ is found by bisection to rounding and its slope by a central difference.
