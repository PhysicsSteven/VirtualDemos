Black Holes and Lensing

Mass bends light. Slide a background galaxy behind a lens to make an Einstein ring, watch a microlensing event, ray trace a black hole's shadow, and see what gravity does to clocks, light and a person near the horizon.

More about this

Every pixel's light ray is followed backward with the exact Schwarzschild orbit equation d²u/dφ² = −u + (3/2) rsu², u = 1/r. Rays with impact parameter below bc fall into the hole: that is the shadow. Rays just above bc circle the photon sphere and pile up thin copies of the whole sky at the shadow's edge.

More about this

A static observer hovers at fixed r with a rocket and compares clocks with someone far away. The tidal stretch is for a person falling feet first; in general relativity it is exactly 2GML/r³ along the fall, the same as Newton's estimate, and half that as a squeeze sideways.
