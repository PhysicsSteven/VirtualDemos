Orbital Resonances

When an asteroid goes round the Sun exactly three times for each turn of Jupiter, Jupiter tugs it at the same places again and again and its orbit stretches until it crosses Mars's. Watch the kicks beside the real counts of asteroids, see how the same kind of timing keeps Pluto safe from Neptune, and find Neptune from its pull on Uranus as Le Verrier did in 1846.

The model and its formulas

Kepler's third law: P² = a³ (P in years, a in AU)

a body that goes round p times while the planet goes round q times: a = a_planet × (q/p)^(2/3)

Jupiter's 3:1 → 2.50 AU, 5:2 → 2.82 AU, 7:3 → 2.96 AU, 2:1 → 3.28 AU; Neptune's 3:2 → 39.4 AU, 2:1 → 47.7 AU
