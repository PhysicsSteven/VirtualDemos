Planet Temperatures and Habitable Zones

Sunlight spreads out as it travels, so a planet's share falls as one over the distance squared. Balance the sunlight a planet absorbs against the heat it radiates to find its temperature, add a greenhouse to match Venus, Earth and Mars, and move the star's habitable zone.

The model and its formulas

S = L / (4π d²)

absorbed per m² = S (1 − a) / f, f = 4 (whole planet), 2 (day side), 1 (under the star)

no greenhouse: σT⁴ = S (1 − a) / f

greenhouse: e σT⁴ = S (1 − a) / f, so T = T(no greenhouse) / e^¼

one layer absorbing a share ε of the heat radiation: e = 1 − ε/2; N layers absorbing all of it: e = 1/(N + 1)

habitable zone edges: d = √(L / S_eff), S_eff = S₀ + aT⋆ + bT⋆² + cT⋆³ + dT⋆⁴, T⋆ = T_star − 5780 K

air kept for billions of years if 6 v_rms < v_esc, v_rms = √(3kT/m)

Where the numbers come from
  • L☉ = 3.828 × 10²⁶ W, the nominal solar luminosity (IAU 2015 Resolution B3); 1 AU = 1.495978707 × 10¹¹ m (IAU 2012 Resolution B2).
  • σ = 5.670374419 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, k = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K, u = 1.66053906892 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, G = 6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ (CODATA 2022).
  • Distances, Bond albedos, escape speeds and measured temperatures: NASA NSSDCA planetary fact sheets (Mercury, Venus, Moon 2024; Earth 2024; Mars 2025; Jupiter 2024; Titan's mass and radius from the Saturnian satellite fact sheet, 2025).
  • Titan's surface temperature, 93.65 K: Fulchignoni et al. 2005, Nature 438, 785 (Huygens probe).
  • Habitable zone edges for a planet of Earth's mass: Kopparapu et al. 2014, ApJ Letters 787, L29, valid for star temperatures 2600 to 7200 K.
  • Proxima Centauri: 2900 K, log L = −2.82, planet b at 0.04848 AU (Suárez Mascareño et al. 2025). TRAPPIST-1: 2566 K, log L = −3.26 (Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. 2025); planet e at 0.02925 AU, 0.692 Earth masses, 0.920 Earth radii (Agol et al. 2021); values as listed by the NASA Exoplanet Archive.
  • The young Sun's luminosity: L = L☉ / [1 + 0.4 (1 − t/t☉)] (Gough 1981, Solar Physics 74, 21), with t☉ = 4.57 billion years.
  • The factor of 6 between escape speed and molecular speed: Freedman, Geller and Kaufmann, Universe, 10th ed., Box 7-2.
