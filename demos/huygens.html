Huygens's Principle

Light is slower in matter, v = c/n. Race light through water, glass and diamond, then use Huygens's wavelets to see why that slowing bends a wave at a boundary, how a mirror reflects it, and how a wave spreads through an opening.

More about this

Model: v = c/n with the indices of OpenStax Table 1.1 (589 nm; gases at 0 °C and 1 atm, liquids and solids at 20 °C). In the race, light takes t = nL/c to cross a lane of length L; the pulses are drawn slowed down by the factor printed on the picture. At a boundary, each point of the surface starts a wavelet when the incident front reaches it, and the wavelets grow at the speed of light in their medium; the new front is the line tangent to them. Fronts are one wavelength apart and the wavelength is drawn hugely enlarged. For the opening, the waves are drawn in two dimensions: every point of the opening is a source of circular wavelets of amplitude falling as 1/√r, and the picture is their sum at each instant (Huygens and Fresnel). That simple sum is not exact within a wavelength or so of the edges, and the brightness is scaled to fit.
