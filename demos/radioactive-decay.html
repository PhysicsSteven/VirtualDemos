σ = √(N₀f(1 − f)), where f is the expected fraction. For f small this is close to √N. The last line is the memoryless property at work: whatever their age, about half the survivors go in each half life.

More about this

No one can say when a given nucleus will decay. A nucleus that has survived 3 half lives is no more likely to decay next than a fresh one: its chance of decaying within the next half life is still exactly 50%.

Model: each step of length Δt, every nucleus draws one random number and changes with the exact probability for that time, p = 1 − e−λΔt. For a chain the step uses the Bateman probabilities, so a parent can even pass through the daughter within one step. The sample therefore follows the expected curves at any step size.
