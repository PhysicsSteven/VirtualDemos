Mass from an initial final mass relation, MWD ≈ 0.08 Mi + 0.49 M☉. Radius from a fit to the Chandrasekhar relation, R ≈ 0.0126 R☉ (M/M☉)−1/3 [1 − (M/1.44 M☉)4/3]1/2: heavier white dwarfs are smaller. No fusion, so R stays fixed and the star slides down a line of constant radius.

Cooling follows Mestel's law, L ≈ 10⁻³ L☉ (M/0.6 M☉)(t/1 Gyr)−7/5. Real white dwarfs cool faster than this at first, while neutrino emission from the hot core beats the photon luminosity (above about 25,000 K). Later the core crystallizes (beginning near 6000 K for 0.6 M☉, hotter for heavier stars), and the latent heat slows cooling for a while; after that, Debye cooling speeds it up again.

Model

Main sequence: L ≈ M⁴ for 0.43 to 2 M☉ and 1.4 M³·⁵ above (0.23 M²·³ below), R ≈ M⁰·⁸ below 1 M☉ and M⁰·⁵⁷ above, lifetime t ≈ 10¹⁰ yr × M⁻²·⁵. These fits describe a star midway through its main sequence; here it brightens by about a factor of 2 from start to end.

Everything after the main sequence is a schematic track: the shapes are right, the numbers are approximate, and durations are set as fractions of the main sequence lifetime. Real tracks depend on composition, rotation, binaries and mass loss. The split between neutron stars and black holes near 20 M☉ is uncertain.

The background stars are generated to follow the real distribution of nearby and bright stars, not a catalog.
