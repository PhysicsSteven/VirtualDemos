Terminal Velocity

Jump from a plane and the air pushes back harder the faster you fall, until the drag balances your weight and the speed stops growing. Change your body position, open the parachute, jump where the air is thin, or drop stacks of coffee filters, and measure the terminal speed.

More about this

The clock always shows the real time of the fall; the playback speed only changes how long you wait. Step runs the fall to the next whole step of the clock (1 s or 0.1 s for a skydiver, 0.05 s or 0.01 s for the filters) and stops there, so readings land on round times. The model and its formulas

D = ½ρCAv², ma = mg − D, v_T = √(2mg/(ρCA))

Model: the drag is ½ρCAv² against the motion, with a constant drag coefficient C (OpenStax University Physics Vol. 1, §6.4). Buoyancy and the air carried along with the body are left out, and the fall is straight down (a real exit also carries the plane's forward speed). g = 9.81 m/s² everywhere. Uniform air has the textbook's ρ = 1.21 kg/m³; air that thins with height follows the International Standard Atmosphere below 11 km (U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976: 288.15 K and 101 325 Pa at the ground, cooling 6.5 K per km), so ρ is 1.225 kg/m³ at the ground and 0.413 kg/m³ at 10 km.

Skydiver: C = 1.0 and A = 0.70 m² spread eagle, C = 0.70 and A = 0.18 m² head first, the textbook's Table 6.2 and its two examples. A change of position takes 1.0 s, with the drag area CA changing at a steady rate. The round canopy is a flat circular one 10.0 m across when laid flat, with C = 0.81 on that flat area (Scher and Young, NASA TN D-6423, 1971), so CA = 63.6 m². After the pull the lines take 1.0 s to stretch, then the canopy fills in 3.0 s, its drag area growing in step with time; under it the body hangs upright with the head first values. The 10.0 m, 1.0 s and 3.0 s are model choices. The canopy is drawn with its open area in proportion to its drag area.

Coffee filter: 0.86 g, 15.1 cm across the top, C = 0.71 on that circle (Boyle and Pantaleone, "The added mass of a falling coffee filter", arXiv:2206.14902, 2022, who measured 1.1 m/s for one filter). Stacked filters nest, so n of them have n times the mass and the same area. The motion detector under them reads the height above its face.

The motion is integrated with fourth order Runge–Kutta in steps of at most 10 ms and a hundredth of v_T/g; from rest in uniform air it agrees with the exact v = v_T tanh(gt/v_T) to better than 1 part in 10⁹. Readings: t to 0.01 s, distances to 0.01 m, altitude to 0.1 m, v to 0.01 m/s and a to 0.01 m/s². No noise is added.

Blind hides the terminal speed, the drag force and the weight, the drag area, the air density, the largest deceleration, the force arrows and the terminal speed line on the graph, leaving the clock, the height, v and a.
