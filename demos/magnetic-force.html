Charges in Magnetic Fields

The magnetic force on a moving charge is always at right angles to its velocity, so it turns the charge without speeding it up. Add an electric field and build a velocity selector, a mass spectrometer and a cyclotron.

With the free setups, the arrow keys first turn the launch velocity (left and right) and change its speed (up and down). Press S for the next item and Shift+S for the one before: the launch ring, ruler end A, ruler end B, each edge of the field rectangle and the E direction dial. Arrow keys move the selected item one screen pixel, Shift+arrow ten. M shows or hides the ruler, T starts or stops the timer, C captures a reading. In the velocity selector, mass spectrometer and cyclotron, up and down change the slit, the spread or the frequency, F fires 20 particles and C clears.

More about this

Lay the ruler across the track: drag its two ends onto opposite sides of the circle. It reads to one screen pixel, so zoom in (wheel or +) for a finer reading. The timer counts time in the simulation and stops while paused. Start it as the particle passes a mark, such as the launch ring, and stop it after a whole number of turns. Keys on the drawing: M ruler, T timer, S picks what the arrow keys move.

Hall effect in a strip
Strip
B

The voltmeter reads VH = Vtop − Vbottom. In the steady state the edge charge makes a field EH whose force qEH cancels qvd × B, so the carriers flow straight; VH = IB/(nqt), and its sign gives the sign of the carriers. The edge charge builds up in far less than a nanosecond, so only the steady state is drawn. The strip is 10 mm wide. One kind of carrier, free carrier model: copper has n = 8.49 × 10²⁸ m⁻³ (one electron per atom), whose measured Hall coefficient differs from this by tens of percent because its electrons are not free; the germanium samples use n = 1.0 × 10²¹ m⁻³, a typical value for doped teaching samples, not one particular crystal. The dots move at a speed chosen for the drawing; the true vd is in the readouts. Blind renames the strips and hides the carriers, their density and vd; the voltmeter stays.
