Gauss's Law

The electric flux through any closed surface equals the charge inside divided by ε₀. Charges outside send field lines through the surface too, but every line that goes in comes back out.

lengths in cm · q in nC · E in N/C · Φ in N·m²/C · ε₀ = 8.854×10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²)

    3D view: the whole closed surface, with its area elements drawn as tiles. Field lines leave each charge evenly in all directions, three times as many per nC as set above, so lines out minus lines in follows Qenc. Drag empty space to turn the view.

    Model: point charges in empty space, all in the plane z = 0, which is the slice you see. Their field lines in that plane stay in the plane, so the drawn lines are exact field lines. Lines are drawn in proportion to charge, but in a slice the spacing of lines does not show the field strength the way it does in 3D. The surface color fades where E·n is small. Arrows on the surface show the direction of E; their length grows with E and levels off for strong fields.
