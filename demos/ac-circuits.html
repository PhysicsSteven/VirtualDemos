AC Circuits and Resonance

A sine wave source drives a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor in series. Each part shifts the current's timing in its own way, and at one frequency the inductor and capacitor cancel.

Display

Oscilloscope

XY plots the current against the source voltage over one cycle, a Lissajous figure. In step it is a straight line; a quarter cycle apart, an upright ellipse. Where it crosses v = 0, i = ±I₀ sin φ.

Model: ideal parts (no coil resistance, no capacitor leakage, no wire inductance), an ideal source with no internal resistance, and lumped elements. Steady state uses the exact phasor solution and applies at once when you change a value. Switch on solves L di/dt + Ri + q/C = V₀ sin ωt exactly from i = 0 and q = 0. With an ideal capacitor alone the current jumps at t = 0. Dot speed is proportional to i, scaled to the current axis.

The bench: the resistor's color bands encode R, and the printed values on the parts follow the sliders. The coil's turn count and the capacitor's plate gap only grow or shrink with L and C (the gap on a log scale). The probes are drawn at the parts they measure; a real setup would need differential probes for vR and vL. The scope's four voltage channels share one V/div, and the current comes from a clamp current probe. The Δt cursors run from a peak of the source voltage to the matching peak of the current.
