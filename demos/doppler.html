Doppler Effect and Shock Waves

A moving source crowds its wavefronts ahead and stretches them behind, so the observer hears a different pitch. Faster than sound, the fronts pile into a Mach cone. For light, relativity adds time dilation.

More about this

The air is at rest. Only for sound does it matter which one moves: the medium picks out a frame.

Moving them on the picture

Drag the source to move it, and the grip at the tip of its arrow to set its velocity. The arrow shows how far it moves in the time printed on the drawing. In 3D, drag anywhere else to turn the view, and use the wheel or a pinch to zoom; double click puts the view back. The 3D view shows the same simulation: each crest is a sphere around the point it left from, and the flat view is a slice through the spheres (the ground for the ambulance, the upright plane of the flight for the jet). Everything in it is to scale.

More about this

f′ = f (v − vo·n̂)/(v − vs·n̂), where n̂ points from where the source was when it emitted this sound to where the observer is now. For an observer at rest this is f′ = f/(1 − (vs/v) cos θ). The dashed line on the drawing is that path.

More about this

f′ = f/(γ(1 − β cos θ)), θ measured in the observer's frame between the velocity and the line to the observer. Head on this is √((1 + β)/(1 − β)); across the line of sight (θ = 90°) it is 1/γ, a redshift from time dilation alone. For small speeds z ≈ vr/c: a star receding at 30 km/s shifts H-alpha by only 0.066 nm.

Hubble's law v = H0d, with v = cz, holds only for z ≪ 1 (roughly z below 0.1). Larger galaxy redshifts come from the expansion of space and need a cosmological model, not this formula.

Model

Point source in still, uniform air (v = 343.2 m/s at 20 °C). Each drawn circle is one crest, centered where the source was when it emitted it. The received frequency comes from that geometry: the program finds the emission time whose crest is reaching the observer now. Loudness falls off as 1/r in the audio but is not drawn. A real aircraft is not a point: its nose and tail shocks give two booms close together.

Special relativity, in the observer's frame. Light needs no medium and travels at c in every frame. The source's clock runs slow by γ, so it emits crests γ times less often than it would at rest; the rest is the same crowding as for sound. Colors are approximate screen colors; outside 380 to 750 nm the light is drawn gray.
